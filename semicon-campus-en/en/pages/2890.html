Our goal is that by working through all the courses in order, you will be able to explain semiconductor manufacturing from the underlying principles to real-world challenges on the fab floor.

Courses will be released in order as their articles are ready. Dates are current estimates and may change. Tap a course name to open its details.

Cleaning courseRolling release from winter 2026

Learn the process that removes particles, organics, metals, and native oxide from the wafer surface. 17 lessons are planned. Cleaning appears many times in the process flow and has a big effect on yield.

Recommended for

People new to semiconductors aiming for a job in the industry, manufacturing operators, sales and technical staff working with equipment and chemicals, and anyone who wants a first step toward a certification.

What you will learn

  • Why cleaning is needed so many times, and the types of contamination (particles, organics, metals, oxide)
  • The idea behind RCA cleaning, and the roles of chemicals, pure water, and drying
  • How cleaning is evaluated (particle counts, surface analysis), and common defects
  • The difference between single-wafer and batch cleaning, and how cleaning challenges change with scaling

Three levels

  • Level 3 Introductory: Explain in words the purpose of cleaning, the types of contamination, and basic cleaning methods
  • Level 2 Fundamentals: Understand how chemicals work, equipment types, and control items, and sort out process problems
  • Level 1 Advanced: Dig into challenges with scaling and new materials, and how to think about cleaning conditions
Device Basics courseAround spring 2027

Learn the foundations, such as what a semiconductor is and why a transistor works as a switch. This is the basis for all the other courses.

Recommended for

People from non-science backgrounds or other industries who want to change jobs, and anyone who wants to grasp the principles before learning manufacturing processes.

What you will learn

  • Properties of semiconductors, pn junctions, and how MOS transistors work
  • How memory and logic work differently
  • Scaling and Moore's Law, and the link between performance and power consumption
  • The big picture of how a circuit becomes a single chip

Three levels

  • Level 3 Introductory: Explain how semiconductors and transistors work, even without diagrams
  • Level 2 Fundamentals: Understand MOS structure, electrical characteristics, and the effects of scaling
  • Level 1 Advanced: Cover leakage, short-channel effects, and the ideas behind new structures (such as FinFETs)
Deposition courseAround spring–summer 2027

The process of stacking insulating, metal, and semiconductor films on the wafer. Learn the differences between CVD, PVD (sputtering), ALD, and epitaxy.

Recommended for

People aiming for manufacturing or process engineering jobs, and people working at equipment or materials makers.

What you will learn

  • Types and roles of thin films (insulation, interconnect, gate, and more)
  • The principles of CVD, PVD, and ALD, and when to use each
  • How to judge quality: film thickness, uniformity, and step coverage
  • The cutting-edge challenge of deposition into high-aspect-ratio structures

Three levels

  • Level 3 Introductory: Explain the purpose of deposition and the differences between the main methods
  • Level 2 Fundamentals: Understand how the reactions work and the conditions that determine film quality
  • Level 1 Advanced: Dig into deposition challenges in advanced structures and how to think about process conditions
Lithography (exposure) courseAround summer 2027

The process of printing circuit patterns onto the wafer. Covers photoresist, exposure tools, masks, and EUV.

Recommended for

Process and equipment engineers, science-minded people interested in optics or materials, and anyone who wants to dig deep into cutting-edge topics.

What you will learn

  • The basic steps of photolithography (coating, exposure, developing)
  • The factors that determine resolution (wavelength, numerical aperture, process factor)
  • Masks, resist, and overlay accuracy
  • Where immersion, multi-patterning, and EUV fit in

Three levels

  • Level 3 Introductory: Explain the flow of how a pattern is made
  • Level 2 Fundamentals: Understand the resolution equation and the control points of each step
  • Level 1 Advanced: Cover the challenges of EUV and scaling technology, and how to think about limits

Ion Implantation and Thermal Processing courseAround fall 2027

The process of adding impurities to set how electricity flows, then using heat to settle them. Learn doping, annealing, and diffusion.

Recommended for

Device and process engineers, and science-minded people interested in material properties.

What you will learn

  • The purpose of doping, and how n-type and p-type regions are made

  • Level 2 Fundamentals: Understand the relationship between implantation, activation, and diffusion

How the courses work

  • Starting with "Device Basics" or "Cleaning" makes the other processes easier to understand.
  • Each Lesson is made up of a short article, a check quiz, and a review of key terms.
  • Content is created after checking sources such as published papers, academic journals, and public documents.

How each course maps to certification exam scopes (such as the Semiconductor Technician Certification) will be organized and announced as courses are released.
