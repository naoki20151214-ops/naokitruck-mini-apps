On September 24, 2026, Trio-Tech International announced its fourth-quarter and full-year results for fiscal 2026, which ended on June 30, 2026. Consolidated revenue was $62,605,000, up 72% from $36,473,000 in fiscal 2025. Revenue from Semiconductor Back-End Solutions (SBS) in particular was $49,018,000, up 99% from $24,682,000 the year before. "Fiscal 2026" in this article is the fiscal year from July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026. So this is not a revenue increase that happened on September 24; it is news of results through the end of June that were published in September. Earnings release (SEC filing)

What grew?

SBS is a business that designs and manufactures equipment used in the semiconductor back-end process, such as burn-in systems and burn-in boards. It also provides electrical testing, environmental testing, and burn-in testing at its test labs in Asia. The 99% increase is not growth in equipment sales alone; it is revenue growth for the whole segment, which combines the equipment and testing services the company includes in SBS. SBS accounted for 78.3% of company-wide revenue in fiscal 2026. 2026 Form 10-K

Backlog, which may lead to future revenue, also rose to $24,172,000 as of June 30, 2026, from $11,030,000 the year before. Of this, SBS accounted for $19,773,000 and industrial electronics for $4,399,000. However, backlog is the remaining value of orders; it is not an amount that will immediately become revenue, nor does it guarantee profit. Delivery dates, profitability, order changes or cancellations, and when revenue will actually be recognized cannot be known from this figure alone.

The company says demand for reliability testing of AI-related chips, advanced computing, and semiconductors for EVs (electric vehicles) and autonomous driving drove growth. An important distinction is needed here. Revenue and backlog are results that can be confirmed in the financial filings, but the explanation that "AI and automotive demand was the main driver" is the company's view. Revenue by customer and the separate contributions of AI and automotive are not disclosed. The company's earnings explanation

What is the semiconductor back-end process?

Semiconductor manufacturing can be broadly divided into the front-end process, which forms circuits on silicon wafers, and the back-end process, which cuts out finished chips, puts them in packages, and inspects them. In the back-end process, chips are connected to external terminals and checked for whether they can withstand heat, moisture, and electrical stress. For AI GPUs, chips for high-performance computing, and automotive semiconductors, quality checks that assume long hours of operation and temperature changes are essential.

SBS here includes both manufacturing test equipment used in the back-end process and services that actually test customers' semiconductors. So the growth in SBS revenue shows that spending on the testing capacity needed when chipmakers and design companies move new products into volume production increased, at least in Trio-Tech's business. However, this alone does not mean the entire semiconductor back-end market grew 99%. It is only the company's year-over-year figure.

A beginner's guide to burn-in testing

What is burn-in?

Burn-in is a test that applies harsher temperature and electrical stress than in normal use to semiconductors for a set time, to screen out, before shipment, units that are likely to fail early due to manufacturing defects or variation. In human terms, it is like giving a product an intensive health checkup in a short time. A U.S. government technical document describes burn-in as screening to find time- and stress-dependent early failures within a practical amount of time. NBS technical document

However, applying strong stress is not enough on its own. If conditions are too severe, the test itself may trigger failure modes that would not occur in normal use. The technical key points are how to set temperature, voltage, current, and time, and how to link test results to actual lifetime.

Typical reliability tests

  • HTOL: A test that operates semiconductors at high temperature to check reliability over long hours of operation.
  • Temperature cycling: Repeats high and low temperatures to check whether materials and connections can withstand temperature changes.
  • Temperature humidity bias: Combines high temperature and high humidity with a voltage load to accelerate and examine degradation from moisture and electricity.
  • uHAST: A test that evaluates resistance in a high-temperature, high-humidity environment without applying voltage bias.

These are not all the same test. Each accelerates and checks a different failure mechanism, so they are combined according to the product's use and package structure. Texas Instruments also describes multiple accelerated tests using temperature, humidity, voltage, current, and more as part of reliability evaluation. Texas Instruments' explanation of reliability testing

Why this news matters

In the race for AI semiconductor performance, chips need not only computing power but also durability over long hours of operation. Automotive semiconductors must be designed for temperature changes inside and outside the vehicle and for long service lives. The more advanced semiconductor designs become, the more important the equipment and testing services that inspect finished products and find problems before volume production become.

The 99% rise in Trio-Tech's SBS revenue and the growth in backlog are a real example of this kind of testing-related spending leading to business expansion for the company. The company also says it expanded capacity in Malaysia and points to growth opportunities from fiscal 2027 onward in semiconductors for AI, high-performance computing, EVs, and autonomous driving. However, the specific capacity added in Malaysia, utilization rates, and future revenue are not disclosed. The outlook for fiscal 2027 and beyond should be taken as the company's plans and views, not completed production results. Annual report

Limits to keep in mind and what to check next

Looking only at revenue growth, it seems like very strong growth, but the gross margin for fiscal 2026 that can be confirmed in the filings we reviewed was 17%, down from 25% the year before. Even when revenue grows, margins can fall due to equipment costs, labor costs, product mix, and other factors. Also, the company's SBS revenue is not a statistic for the whole semiconductor back-end market but the results of Trio-Tech alone. Nor is it a figure that directly shows the growth rate of the overall AI or EV markets.

Going forward, we want to check, first, when the backlog will turn into actual revenue; second, how much the expanded capacity in Malaysia will be used; and third, whether revenue growth leads to a recovery in margins. If revenue by use for AI and automotive and dependence on major customers are disclosed, it will be easier to judge whether this growth is a temporary rise in orders or ongoing testing demand. As of September 27, 2026, no new official document correcting this earnings release or the 10-K has been found, but that does not guarantee there will be no future additional disclosures or corrections.

Summary

The confirmed facts this time are that in Trio-Tech's fiscal year ended June 2026, consolidated revenue rose 72%, Semiconductor Back-End Solutions revenue rose 99%, and backlog grew to $24,172,000. The company cites demand for reliability testing of AI and automotive semiconductors as the background, but this is not independent data showing the contribution by use or the growth of the overall market. Tests such as burn-in and HTOL are not technologies that boost performance; they are processes for selecting semiconductors that are less likely to fail after shipment and for checking quality. In semiconductor competition in the AI era, the equipment and services of this "less visible back-end process" are helping to support stable volume production.
