On October 1, 2026, Filtronic, a UK maker of high-frequency communications components, announced that it had received an additional order from SpaceX for E-band GaN solid-state power amplifiers (SSPAs). The order is worth $68.1 million (£51.3 million), the largest the company has ever received from SpaceX. This news shows that high-frequency, high-power semiconductor components used in satellite communications are part of the commercial supply chain, not just at the research stage. However, it was Filtronic that made the announcement. SpaceX did not disclose any specific satellites or equipment. This can be confirmed in Filtronic's announcement and the regulatory disclosure on the London Stock Exchange.

What happened?

The order covers E-band GaN SSPAs in Filtronic's "Cerus" series. According to Filtronic, this is a follow-on to the $62.5 million order for next-generation GaN E-band technology announced in August 2025, and most of this order is expected to be fulfilled during the company's FY2028. In other words, it does not mean $68.1 million worth of products had already been delivered and booked as revenue at the time of the announcement. When the order is fulfilled and revenue is recognized depends on future progress.

The announcement also does not disclose delivery quantities, unit price, output power, efficiency, manufacturing site, or exactly where it will be used at SpaceX. LEO (low Earth orbit) satellite communications is given as an example use, but it cannot be confirmed which Starlink satellites, ground stations, gateways, or generations will use these products. It is not appropriate to say for certain that they are "for Starlink." What can be said for sure from this news is that Filtronic announced an additional order from SpaceX.

E-band and SSPAs explained for beginners

What is E-band?

E-band is a wireless communications band that uses very high frequencies. In Filtronic's product case study, it is described as the ranges of 71–76GHz and 81–86GHz. High-frequency communications make it easier to secure wide bandwidth, which suits wireless links that send large amounts of data quickly. For example, one possible use is securing large capacity on the link connecting satellite ground stations to the network.

On the other hand, high-frequency radio waves are more affected by distance-related free-space path loss and by the atmosphere. Free-space path loss is the phenomenon where radio waves spread out as they travel through space and become weaker at the receiver. So in E-band, high-gain antennas that focus radio waves in the target direction, enough transmit power, and link designs that account for rain and atmospheric conditions are important. A NASA technical memo also treats E-band as a technology for multi-gigabit satellite communications, lunar surface communications, and terrestrial backhaul.NASA technical document

What are GaN and SSPAs?

GaN is a compound semiconductor called gallium nitride. In high-frequency power amplifiers, it is drawing attention as a material that makes it easier to aim for high power density, high-voltage operation, efficiency, and good thermal handling compared with GaAs (gallium arsenide), which has long been used. These advantages are not performance that every product achieves automatically. They are evaluated in combination with circuit design, cooling, and manufacturing technology.

SSPA stands for "solid-state power amplifier." It is a device that uses semiconductors to strengthen high-frequency signals. Its role is to amplify the signal created by communications equipment until it is strong enough to transmit from the antenna. An MMIC is a single semiconductor chip that integrates high-frequency amplifier circuits and similar functions. Some designs combine multiple MMICs, and equipment is configured based on the required output and reliability.

Technical and business background

In satellite communications, it is important to carry as much data as possible with limited frequency resources. High-frequency bands like E-band may be suited to high-capacity communications, but measures against signal attenuation and weather effects are needed. So not only antennas but also RF (radio frequency) amplifiers that can efficiently output high power determine the performance of the whole communications link.

In a technical memo published on September 1, 2025, NASA covered research applying E-band GaN HEMT MMIC high-power amplifiers to satellite communications, lunar surface communications, and terrestrial backhaul. A HEMT is a transistor that uses the high electron mobility that occurs at the boundary between different semiconductor materials. This NASA document provides independent background on E-band GaN technology, but it does not mean NASA's research results were directly used in this Filtronic product. Its publication date, September 1, 2025, is earlier than Filtronic's announcement date, and it is important not to confuse the two dates.

Also, the "Cerus 32" on a separate Filtronic page is described as a product case study using 32 GaAs MMICs. It cannot be confirmed to have the same specifications as the GaN product announced this time, so the output values and configuration of Cerus 32 cannot be applied directly to this order.Cerus 32 product case study

What this order shows, and its limits

This order shows that Filtronic views itself as having continuing commercial demand from SpaceX for GaN-based high-frequency amplifiers. The fact that it follows the 2025 order and is described as the largest ever is a growth factor for the company's business. It is important news when considering the possibility that E-band GaN SSPAs will be supplied to real communications infrastructure.

However, the order value is not profit, and it does not mean all future revenue is locked in. The statement that most of it will be fulfilled during FY2028 is Filtronic's own plan and outlook. The timing and amount of revenue recognition may change due to production capacity, specification changes on the customer side, delivery schedules, acceptance inspections, and so on. Also, from this announcement alone, it is not possible to independently measure how much SpaceX's satellite count or communications capacity will grow, or how much better GaN actually is than GaAs.

What to watch next

  • Whether actual delivery quantities, shipment timing, and revenue recognition proceed as the company expects.
  • Whether specific specifications for GaN Cerus products, such as output, efficiency, thermal design, and reliability, are published.
  • Whether it becomes clear where the products are used: in satellite-mounted equipment, ground stations, or gateways.
  • Whether any contract changes, postponements, or corrections appear in disclosures from SpaceX or regulators.
  • Whether actual operating performance in E-band communications, including losses from rain and the atmosphere, is shown.

As of October 5, 2026, no official Filtronic update correcting or withdrawing the October 1, 2026 announcement has been found. However, this does not guarantee there will be no future contract changes or that there is no non-public information. This news does not mean GaN will immediately replace everything just because it is promising for satellite communications. It shows that commercial adoption may advance as an option that meets the output, efficiency, and thermal design required for high-frequency, high-capacity communications.
