What happened: A years-long collaboration strengthened by contract

On September 15, 2026, U.S.-based Aeluma announced that it had signed a strategic agreement with Sumitomo Chemical Advanced Technologies (SCAT), a wholly owned subsidiary of Sumitomo Chemical, to speed up the development and production of photonics wafers for AI data communication. The two companies have already worked together for several years. What is new is not a first-time partner, but that they strengthened an existing relationship to expand development and production. Note that September 15 is the announcement date. The signing date and effective date of the agreement have not been disclosed. Aeluma's agreement announcement

In the initial phase, SCAT will use its existing MOCVD equipment in Phoenix, Arizona, with future capacity expansion also in view. Aeluma lists 150mm-diameter GaAs and 200–300mm silicon as candidate substrates other than InP. However, this describes available platforms. It is not an announcement that volume production has started at all wafer sizes of 150mm, 200mm, and 300mm. Agreement details and candidate substrates

Why it matters: AI data centers are connected by light

AI data centers move huge amounts of data between many compute units and servers. This is done with optical semiconductors that convert electrical signals into light and turn incoming light back into electrical signals. Photonics is the technology field that uses the generation, transmission, and detection of light. Sumitomo Chemical describes the InP epitaxial wafers used for optoelectronic conversion as one of the materials supporting the evolution of AI data centers. Sumitomo Chemical's InP wafer announcement

Aeluma takes the view that supply constraints on InP substrates are holding back development and production, and it is trying to set up a manufacturing route that can use other substrates. InP is a compound semiconductor made of indium and phosphorus, and GaAs is made of gallium and arsenic. Si is silicon, which is widely used in semiconductor manufacturing. Having more substrate options could reduce dependence on a specific material. However, the degree of supply constraints and the procurement benefits are the companies' own explanations, not results proven by third-party supply-and-demand statistics, prices, or lead-time comparisons. Aeluma's explanation of non-InP substrates

Understanding the technical terms: substrate, epitaxial layer, and MOCVD

A substrate is the base on which crystals are grown, and an epitaxial layer is a thin functional layer formed on top of it with the same crystal orientation. Compared to a building, the substrate is the ground and the epitaxial layers are the floors stacked up to suit the purpose. Properties such as receiving light or emitting light are tuned by the material, composition, and thickness of the layers.

According to Sumitomo Chemical's technical explanation, MOVPE grows thin layers on the nanometer scale while controlling atomic composition, and then evaluates their condition using X-rays, light emission, surface measurements, and more. MOCVD is a name for this type of growth method. It sends materials such as metal-containing organic sources as gases and builds thin crystal layers on a heated substrate. It is easiest to think of it not as simply adding a film, but as a process that precisely controls the material mix and thickness. Sumitomo Chemical / SCIOCS technical explanation

"Non-InP" here refers to the substrate. So we cannot confirm that the functional layers on the substrate contain no InP or indium at all. Rather than broadly rephrasing it as "products that do not use InP," we need to distinguish which part's material is being changed. Announcement of the non-InP platform

The goal of larger wafers, and the barriers to volume production

150mm and 300mm are wafer diameters. In general, larger wafers have room to place more devices at once. However, a large diameter alone does not prove low cost, high performance, or high yield. That is because film thickness and composition must be uniform across the whole wafer, and the same quality must be reproduced in every production run.

Sumitomo Chemical also explains that for epitaxial wafers for optical communication, controlling composition, surface condition, film thickness uniformity, and reproducibility is important. Being able to prepare a large substrate and being able to form stable functional layers across its whole surface are separate challenges. This announcement includes no measurement results showing yield, communication speed, power consumption, manufacturing cost, or performance compared with products on InP substrates. Explanation of quality control for epitaxial wafers

SCAT says it manufactures custom epitaxial wafers by MOCVD in Phoenix and supports everything from development to high-volume production. Its applications include fiber-optic communication. This existing base is the foundation for moving the collaboration forward, but the general capability of the whole plant is not evidence that products for Aeluma have completed qualification or that dedicated production capacity exists. Relationship with Japanese companies: not a move away from InP

Before this announcement, on August 31, 2026, Sumitomo Chemical announced that it had set up volume production of 4-inch InP epitaxial wafers at its Ibaraki Works and started sales. That case publicly covered setting up volume production and starting sales of a specific product. On the other hand, the agreement between Aeluma and SCAT strengthens a development and production setup that treats non-InP substrates as an option, and does not mean volume production has started at all wafer sizes. The two cases differ in both stage of progress and location. Sumitomo Chemical's August 31, 2026 announcement, Aeluma's September 15, 2026 announcement

From the public information, the Sumitomo Chemical group appears to be advancing its InP epitaxial wafer business while also working on a collaboration that uses other substrates. There is no basis for interpreting this agreement as a "withdrawal from InP." There is also no information showing that equipment, production capacity, or customers are shared between the domestic InP business and the U.S. collaboration.

Limits and what to watch next

What can be confirmed now is an agreement strengthening an existing collaboration, a plan to use SCAT's existing equipment in the initial phase, and a concept that treats 150mm GaAs and 200–300mm silicon as candidate substrates. The contract amount, contract term, minimum purchase volume, capacity by product, start-up timing for each wafer size, and timing for completing customer qualification have not been disclosed. We cannot yet say that the supply shortage has been resolved, that performance gains have been proven, or that commercial volume production is complete. Agreement announcement

What to watch next is which products and wafer sizes manufacturing starts with, whether prototypes pass customer qualification, whether uniformity and yield are shown in numbers, and whether it moves on to actual shipments. The key to evaluating this news accurately is to track separately the "plan" that shows equipment and substrate options, the "qualification" that confirms quality, and the "volume shipments" delivered to customers.
