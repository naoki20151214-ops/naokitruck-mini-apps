On October 1, 2026, JEDEC, a semiconductor standards organization, announced that it had published "JESD264 (Silicon Photonics Qualification and Reliability Requirements)," a standard covering qualification and reliability requirements for silicon photonics. The listed targets are AI networks, data centers, and telecom infrastructure. JEDEC calls it the first industry-wide standard to bring consistent qualification and reliability evaluation to silicon photonics devices. However, this "first" is JEDEC's own claim. It is not independent verification showing adoption in companies' mass-produced products.JEDEC announcement

What was announced?

The scope of JESD264 is not limited to a single semiconductor chip. The announcement lists complex photonic configurations including silicon photonics dies, integrated chipsets, modulators, photodiodes, waveguides, and lasers. Assembly processes, laser integration, manufacturing process control, and traceability are also named as key elements.Full text of the announcement (reprint)

JEDEC also describes it as a framework that clarifies reliability responsibilities among foundries, OSATs, and system developers, and improves consistency across the whole supply chain. An OSAT is a company that specializes in contract semiconductor assembly and test. The purpose of the standard is said to be handling qualification and manufacturing control for products involving these multiple companies under a common framework.

On the other hand, the announcement checked this time does not include JESD264's specific test items, sample counts, stress conditions such as temperature and humidity, pass/fail criteria, or effective date. The publication of the standard and companies actually starting to use it for mass production qualification need to be kept separate.

Silicon photonics explained for beginners

Silicon photonics is a technology that replaces some electrical signals with optical signals and integrates functions that guide light and convert signals onto a semiconductor. Sandia National Laboratories describes it as an electro-optical PIC that forms waveguides, p-n junctions, metal wiring, optical cladding, and more on a silicon-on-insulator (SOI) substrate. A PIC combines multiple optical functions into one photonic integrated circuit.Sandia National Laboratories explanation

A waveguide is a narrow path that carries light in a set direction. A modulator is a component that changes properties of light, such as intensity or phase, to put the information of an electrical signal onto light. A photodiode converts received light into an electrical signal. By converting electricity to light on the transmit side, sending it through optical fiber or similar, and converting it back to electricity on the receive side, this can be used for communication between devices.

However, silicon alone cannot provide every optical function. A technical overview from the IEEE Electronics Packaging Society explains that because silicon is not suited to efficient light emission, a common setup combines III-V compound semiconductor lasers as the light source and uses germanium (Ge) for detection. Coupling to optical fiber and advanced packaging, including 2.5D and 3D, are also involved.IEEE-related technical overview

Why is a reliability standard getting attention?

In silicon photonics, a single product involves not only electrical circuits but also optical elements, lasers, detectors, dissimilar materials, fiber coupling, sealing, and assembly. So checking only the electrical characteristics of the semiconductor circuits is not necessarily enough, and evaluation that includes multiple parts and processes is needed. The IEEE-related overview also explains that this technology involves light sources, detectors, optical fiber coupling, and advanced packaging.

A U.S. Navy SBIR award also confirms that developing reliability models is a challenge. In this project, the research goal is to identify and quantify failure mechanisms in silicon photonic PICs and build lifetime prediction models. The document reflects the concern that standard reliability models for silicon photonics are not yet well established.U.S. SBIR award information

A feature of JESD264 that can be confirmed from this announcement is that it covers not only testing but also dies and integrated chipsets, laser integration, assembly, manufacturing process control, traceability, and responsibilities between companies under the same standard. However, since the text of the standard itself has not been checked, we cannot say for certain how responsibilities are divided, how records are kept, or how binding the tests are.

What it means for AI data centers

JEDEC says a common foundation for testing and manufacturing control will lower deployment risk and support the spread of silicon photonics in AI, data centers, and telecom networks. In AI computing infrastructure, optical connection components may be supplied across multiple semiconductors, optical parts, and assembly processes. So the framework used to qualify parts and processes is a point to watch when thinking about future procurement and product evaluation.JEDEC announcement

However, these are the purpose and expected role of the standard. The announcement does not show adopting companies, qualification examples in mass-produced products, improvements in failure rates, cost effects, or regulatory obligations. The phrase "a step toward a common standard for mass production" is an interpretation showing that standardization may move in that direction. It does not mean adoption on the production floor is complete.

What to check next, and the limits

Going forward, we need to check the text of JESD264 and the companies and products that actually adopt it. Especially important are which parts fall under which qualification scope, what test conditions and pass/fail criteria are used, and how much manufacturing history is shared.

  • Whether foundries, OSATs, and system companies actually adopt JESD264
  • The scope of application for lasers, waveguides, modulators, and photodiodes
  • Published qualification cases, test results, failure rates, and reproducibility for mass-produced products
  • Specific requirements in the standard for division of responsibility, process control, and traceability

This news does not mean the reliability problems of silicon photonics have been solved by publishing a standard. It is a step in standardization that aims to handle a technology involving optics, electronic circuits, materials, packaging, and assembly under a common evaluation framework. To judge the actual effect on mass production and how widely it spreads, future adoption cases and a check of the standard's text are essential.
