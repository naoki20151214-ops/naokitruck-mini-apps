The September 17 announcement marks the start of R&D, not a finished amplifier

On September 17, 2026, Mitsubishi Electric announced that, after two projects were selected by NEDO, it will begin research and development aimed at scaling up quantum computers. The targets are cryogenic amplifiers for superconducting systems and laser systems for neutral-atom and ion-trap systems. From a semiconductor point of view, the notable part is that the company will apply its microwave IC technology to amplifiers that support quantum computing.Mitsubishi Electric announcement

What was revealed is a development plan, not the performance of a finished product or a mass-production track record. Smaller size, more channels, lower noise, and operation at cryogenic temperatures are all development goals. It cannot be read as saying that products achieving these goals have shipped, or that improved quantum computer performance has been demonstrated.Mitsubishi Electric English announcement

The dates also need to be kept apart. NEDO added the review results and the list of planned contractors for this category on July 22, 2026. September 17 is the date of Mitsubishi Electric's announcement, not the date of the internal selection decision. In NEDO's list, the company's two projects are classified as parts and materials development for large-scale quantum computers.NEDO update history, List of planned contractors

Why do quantum computers need amplifiers?

A quantum computer needs not only a way to operate qubits but also a way to read their states. In superconducting readout, a weak signal is amplified and the state is then determined in later processing. An amplifier is an electronic component that supports this signal path; the amplifier itself does not perform quantum computation.IBM Research technical paper

A low-noise amplifier is an amplifier that keeps down the extra noise the circuit itself adds when making a signal larger. Think of trying to hear a quiet voice: the job is not only to make the voice louder but also to reduce the hiss the equipment adds. Because signals from qubits are small, what matters is not just the gain but how little noise is added.Readout circuits and low-noise amplifiers

This challenge is not new. An IBM Research paper summary published in 2023 also treats scaling control and readout circuits as a challenge for future quantum computing systems. The technical background is that, alongside adding more qubits, the surrounding electronics must also be able to scale.2023 research summary

The role of microwave ICs and more channels

A microwave IC is an integrated circuit that handles high-frequency electrical signals called microwaves. Mitsubishi Electric plans to use this technology to develop ultra-small, multi-channel, low-noise amplifier modules that work even at cryogenic temperatures. Developing cryogenic amplifiers for quantum computers can be seen as supporting larger systems from the side that handles the signals quantum computing needs.Amplifier development plan

Multi-channel means being able to handle multiple signal paths. For example, it refers to handling, in one module, the functions for several paths that would otherwise each need their own. However, the announcement does not give a specific channel count or dimensions. You cannot convert "multi-channel" into "supports this many qubits"; we need to wait for the actual connection method and readout circuit design.Development goals stated by Mitsubishi Electric

As semiconductor news, the point is a perspective that does not judge quantum computer progress by qubit count alone. Electronic circuits that amplify and control weak signals are also needed to scale a system. This project can be described as a plan to bring a Japanese company's technology to those surrounding components. However, what the IBM materials support is the general technical challenge; they do not verify Mitsubishi Electric's design or performance.NEDO parts and materials projects, Technical background on electronics

Alongside smaller size: cooling and heat

For circuits used at cryogenic temperatures, making them small is not enough. IBM Research explains that integrating electronics on the cold side could reduce system complexity and cost, but that they must fit within the limited power and heat budget of the cooling system. This is because heat generated by the circuits also constrains system design.IBM Research cryogenic electronics

Given this background, future performance announcements should be read with power consumption, noise, and operating temperature in mind, in addition to size and channel count. This company announcement alone does not give those specific values or prototype results. We also cannot fill in the operating temperature, semiconductor material, or manufacturing process for this project by guessing from circuit examples at other research institutions.Background on cooling conditions, This company announcement

From cryogenic amplifiers to relearning electrical and measurement basics

When you read an amplifier development plan, treat signal size, noise, heat, and temperature conditions as separate check items. Don't confuse the start of research with the performance of a finished product; wait for specs and evaluation results to be published. You can combine learning that fills gaps in electrical and measurement basics with preparing to translate your equipment inspection or evaluation experience into technical job requirements. Operating specialized cryogenic equipment is learned through employer training; general materials do not replace it.

The laser and FPGA work targets different quantum approaches

The other selected project is a laser system for neutral-atom and ion-trap systems. Mitsubishi Electric will use its laser technology for processing machines and low-latency control technology using FPGAs and other devices, aiming for a high-power, highly stable system. Low-latency control means reducing the time delay involved in control. FPGA control here appears in the description of the laser project; it does not indicate FPGAs in the amplifiers for superconducting systems.Description of the two development themes

Next, watch for published specs and hands-on technical verification

Mitsubishi Electric says it will work with research institutions including AIST to carry out technical verification toward practical use. This is future work. The next things to look for are results of cryogenic operation, measured noise and power consumption, and implementation conditions when multiple channels are built in. Only once these are published will it become easier to evaluate the concrete effect on scaling.Plan to work with research institutions, Technical constraints relevant to evaluation

The project period set by NEDO is fiscal 2026 to 2028, and industrialization around 2030 is the goal of the overall program. This schedule does not guarantee a release date or start of mass production for Mitsubishi Electric's amplifiers. For now, it is appropriate to view this as the stage where R&D selection and the company's development plan have been confirmed, and to follow future prototype and verification results.NEDO program description
