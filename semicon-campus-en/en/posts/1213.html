On September 21, 2026, Coherent announced "PhotonLink," an integrated optics platform for AI data centers. The August 25 information was an advance notice that the platform would be unveiled at ECOC on September 21; it is not the same as the official launch date. What we can confirm this time is the technology areas covered, the number of engagements the company reports, its plan to expand manufacturing capacity, and its revenue outlook. No specific customer names, contract values, shipment volumes, or measured performance in volume production have been made public. Coherent's official announcement, advance notice

What does PhotonLink integrate?

In AI data centers, huge amounts of data move between GPUs, CPUs, and network switches. As computing chips get faster, the links between chips must get faster too. Electrical wiring tends to lose more signal over longer distances, and higher speeds also raise problems with power consumption and wiring density.

In an optical link, a laser creates light, data is loaded onto that light and sent through waveguides or optical fiber, and a detector on the receiving side turns it back into an electrical signal. Coherent describes PhotonLink as a foundation that supports this entire optical path with multiple parts, materials, and processes. The company's product description organizes its functions into four stages:

  • Generate: Create light with InP lasers and GaAs VCSEL arrays.
  • Shape: Modulate and couple the light and suppress reflections. Silicon photonics, micro-optics, and isolators are involved.
  • Guide: Guide the light with specialty fiber, polarization-maintaining fiber, fiber guides, and so on.
  • Receive: Receive the light with photodetectors and convert it into an electrical signal.

So PhotonLink is not a new standalone laser product. Coherent has laid out a plan to supply external laser light sources, precision fiber assemblies, optical modules, and more, including assembly and testing.

What is the difference between CPO and NPO?

CPO (Co-Packaged Optics) is an approach that builds the optical engine into the same package as the switch or compute chip. An optical engine is the set of parts that converts between electrical and optical signals, such as lasers, modulators, optical coupling parts, and photodetectors. Because it shortens the high-speed electrical wiring that runs just outside the chip, it may reduce signal loss and power.

NPO (Near-Packaged Optics), on the other hand, places the optical engine near the chip instead of building it into the same package. It makes it easier to keep flexibility in placement and replaceability than CPO, but some electrical connection remains between the chip and the optical engine. In short, CPO means "built into the same package," and NPO means "placed nearby."

PhotonLink is described as targeting CPO, NPO, and new chip-to-chip optical links. However, this does not mean both approaches are already in volume production as one finished product. It is a proposal that brings technology, parts, assembly, and testing for several architectures into a single supply system. Peer-reviewed papers also describe CPO as a candidate for expanding bandwidth, while pointing to challenges such as heat, dense wiring, electrical interfaces, and packaging. What we learned from this announcement

According to Coherent, customer engagements for PhotonLink number more than 10 for CPO, more than 10 for NPO, and more than 5 for chip-to-chip links. The company also says it has secured anchor customers and long-term agreements for CPO and NPO. However, the announcement does not list customer names, contract values, target products, start of volume production, or shipment volumes. "Engagements" may include evaluations and design discussions, and they do not mean the same thing as orders, shipments, or revenue.

On the manufacturing side, Coherent says it has shipped more than 300 million InP lasers and more than 1 billion InP and GaAs photodetectors in total on its existing data center platforms. It also says it will expand its 6-inch InP and specialty fiber manufacturing capacity to launch PhotonLink. These are self-reported figures from the company's announcement, and the materials we checked do not show that they have been independently verified.

As for revenue, Coherent expects PhotonLink revenue to begin ramping in the fourth quarter of calendar 2026. This is the company's plan and outlook, not revenue that has already been recognized. Actual revenue and its size will need to be checked in future earnings materials.

Expected benefits and limits

If the parts and processes involved from generating light to receiving it are brought into one supply and design system, customers may be able to simplify parts selection, design qualification, and coordination among multiple suppliers. This is a benefit that follows from how PhotonLink is structured, but it is not an effect proven by this announcement.

From the announcement alone, we cannot conclude anything about cost savings, lower power consumption, reduced latency, volume-production yield, or an advantage over competitors. In CPO, the closer the optical engine gets to the chip, the more you must think about heat management, fine optical coupling, electrical connections, replacement and repair after failures, and manufacturing tests all at once. For full adoption in AI data centers, the reliability of the whole package and long-term operation matter, not just part performance.

Points to watch going forward

The first thing to check is whether PhotonLink-related revenue is actually recorded in the fourth quarter of 2026, and how large it is. Next, the focus will be how many of the 10-plus engagements move from evaluation to volume-production contracts. If customer names, product generations, and shipment timing become clear, it will be easier to judge whether this is a proposal for R&D or close to real data center deployment.

Specific specs such as optical engine power consumption, data rate, reach, thermal characteristics, volume-production yield, and repairability also matter. PhotonLink shows a big direction as an integrated platform for AI infrastructure, but for now it consists mostly of plans and the company's explanations. Future earnings, customer adoption announcements, and independent performance evaluations will show how far the concept is proven as a volume-production product.
