On September 21, 2026, fJscaler announced that it had begun shipping samples of its 100GHz BiCMOS TIA for AI and cloud data centers to strategic customers. The main specifications given were 100GHz bandwidth, 0.85 pJ/bit power efficiency, and 18 pA/√Hz input-referred noise density. At the same time, it announced a family of photonic integrated circuit (PIC) products for 1.6T coherent-lite and IM-DD optical links, and said it would show them at ECOC 2026.fJscaler announcement

However, sample shipping means the company has started providing evaluation products or prototypes to customers. It does not show that mass production, general sales, or formal adoption have happened. An exhibition plan is also different from a mass production start date. In this article, we separate what the company announced from the general background that can be confirmed in technical materials.

A TIA is the entry point that turns light signals into electrical signals

TIA stands for "transimpedance amplifier," an important circuit placed at the very start of an optical receiver. When a photodiode receives light from an optical fiber, it produces a tiny current that matches the light's intensity. The TIA converts this current into a voltage signal that later circuits can handle easily. In other words, it is the entry point for reading an optical signal as an electrical signal.Texas Instruments TIA explainer

In AI training and inference, GPUs and dedicated accelerators exchange huge amounts of data. As computing chips get faster, connections between servers and between chips also need more bandwidth. High-speed communication over electrical wiring alone tends to run into problems with distance, loss, and power, so data centers use optical communication. On the receiving side, what matters is whether the signal can be turned back into an electrical signal accurately and with low power.

In high-speed TIAs, parasitic capacitance in the photodiode and wiring is also a major constraint. Input capacitance, feedback resistance, and the amplifier's gain-bandwidth product shape the frequency response, and more capacitance affects bandwidth, noise, and stability. So at the 100GHz level, design must cover not just the TIA itself but also the photodiode, package, and wiring.Explainer on TIA bandwidth design

What do 100GHz and 0.85 pJ/bit mean?

Bandwidth is not the same as data rate

100GHz is the frequency bandwidth of electrical signals the TIA can handle. Wider bandwidth makes it more likely that faster signals can be handled, but the 100GHz figure alone does not guarantee an effective data rate of 200G per lane or 1.6 Tbps. It must be evaluated together with the modulation format, number of lanes, transmit-side driver, photodiode, package, equalization, optical loss, bit error rate (BER), and more.

fJscaler says this TIA can support serial links of 200G per lane or more. This is a claim based on the company's announcement. From the information available, we cannot see third-party measurements or conditions such as temperature, input light, or BER. So rather than concluding "100GHz means 200G per lane is proven," it is more accurate to see this as the stage where verification has started in customer evaluations.Company's specification description

Power efficiency and input-referred noise density

0.85 pJ/bit is presented as a measure of the energy needed to process one bit of data. A smaller number may mean less power to send and receive the same amount of data. In AI data centers that use huge numbers of optical transceivers and switches, a gain in each unit could affect power and cooling loads across whole racks.

18 pA/√Hz is the input-referred noise density. Simply put, it shows how much noise appears at the TIA's input. A smaller number makes it easier to read weak signals. However, receiver sensitivity is decided by the whole system, including the photodiode, optics, circuits, and error correction. You cannot judge link distance or actual BER from this value alone.Announced performance values

Sorting out BiCMOS, IM-DD, and PIC

BiCMOS is a semiconductor technology that combines bipolar transistors and CMOS. Bipolar devices are suited to high-speed analog signal processing, while CMOS makes it easy to integrate digital and control circuits at high density. fJscaler says it combines the speed of SiGe with the integration of CMOS, but this announcement does not reveal the process generation or specific circuit design.

IM-DD means "intensity modulation, direct detection." The transmitter changes the brightness of the light, and the receiver reads that brightness directly with a photodiode. The setup is relatively simple and is used for short-reach data center links. The announcement says the 100GHz TIA is designed to work with back-illuminated InP photodiodes.

PIC stands for "photonic integrated circuit," which puts light-handling components together on one substrate. Coherent-lite appears as a product category that aims for longer reach while keeping complexity and power lower than full coherent systems. fJscaler announced a family of PIC products for 1.6T coherent-lite and IM-DD, but did not give model numbers, optical specifications, prices, or mass production timing.Announcement about the product family

What this announcement means

This move can be read as a plan by fJscaler to extend the analog and BiCMOS technology for optical communication it has described before to meet the high-bandwidth, low-power needs of AI data centers. The company's official website lists existing products such as a 64GBd dual-channel TIA and optical communication products in the 100G, 200G, and 400G classes.fJscaler official website

Lowering TIA power is not a challenge for this company alone. An OFC 2026 paper abstract by NTT researchers also reports a high figure of merit for a TIA for processor-to-processor optical links. This shows that low-power receiver circuits are widely researched, but it is not a document verifying fJscaler's product performance and is no basis for directly comparing the two.OFC 2026 paper abstract by NTT researchers

Also, 1.6T is used in the context of the total capacity of a link or system, so you cannot derive an effective throughput of 1.6 Tbps from the TIA's 100GHz bandwidth alone. Actual link performance is only decided once multi-lane configuration, transmit and receive circuits, optics, and signal processing are all included.

Limits and points to check next

What can be confirmed is the start of sample supply to strategic customers, plus the specifications and product plans the company presented. Mass production results, customer names, formal adoption, third-party measurements, and detailed datasheets cannot be confirmed. Which circuits are included in the 0.85 pJ/bit figure, how 100GHz was measured, temperature, input light conditions, BER, and sample quantities are also important for judging performance.

Going forward, we need to watch the following points.

  • How the 100GHz bandwidth was measured, and the temperature, power supply, and input light conditions
  • How 0.85 pJ/bit is defined, and link evaluations including actual BER
  • Verification results that include the photodiode, package, and transmit-side driver
  • Lane configuration and effective throughput for 200G per lane and 1.6T links
  • Model numbers, mass production timing, customer adoption, and shipment volumes for the PIC products

The direction of high bandwidth and low power is attractive for AI data centers. On the other hand, moving from sample shipping to mass production and adoption requires customer evaluations, reliability testing, manufacturing yield, and whole-system verification. The key to judging the real strength of this technology is not just what is shown at ECOC, but whether measurement data with disclosed evaluation conditions or actual adoption announcements appear.
