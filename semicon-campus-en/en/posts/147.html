Why semiconductor talent is getting attention now

Documents from METI and the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) treat training and securing talent for the semiconductor industry as an important theme. A document from the Kanto Bureau of Economy, Trade and Industry also cites an estimate that nine major companies will need at least 40,000 additional semiconductor workers over the next 10 years.

However, a talent shortage does not mean anyone can get in easily. Rather, it is important to understand the processes, read the terms in job postings, and think about which job type your experience can connect to.

What people new to semiconductors should prepare first

The first thing to do is not to read difficult technical books cover to cover. Start by translating your own experience into the language of the semiconductor industry.

  • If you have factory experience, organize your experience with equipment operation, inspection, quality records, and improvements
  • If you have equipment experience, organize your experience with maintenance, checks, troubleshooting, tools, and electrical or mechanical work
  • If you have quality experience, organize your experience with inspection, measurement, data checking, and defect analysis
  • If you have chemistry or materials experience, organize your experience with chemicals, analysis, mixing, evaluation, and experiments
  • If you have a humanities background, first learn the overall process flow and job titles so you can read the terms in job postings

The semiconductor industry is highly specialized, but it is also made up of many jobs: manufacturing, equipment, quality, materials, logistics, sales, procurement, and more. You don't need to decide from the start that "it's impossible for me." But instead of thinking "it's easy even without experience," it is important to look carefully at which entry point your experience is closest to.
