What was announced? EPP uses silicon as a "container for components" too

On September 16, 2026, onsemi announced the "Embedded Power Platform (EPP)," an integration platform for power semiconductors. The concept uses a silicon wafer as the base of the package and builds in multiple semiconductors and control functions. It targets uses that handle large amounts of power in limited space, such as AI data centers and electric vehicles (EVs). What we can confirm is the announcement of a new platform, not news that mass-produced products are in wide use. Source: onsemi announcement

EPP is designed so that silicon (Si), silicon carbide (SiC), and gallium nitride (GaN) devices can be integrated into a common silicon-based structure. onsemi says it may achieve 3–5 times the power density of current approaches, depending on the application. However, this is the company's performance claim. It does not mean the same multiplier applies to every use or that third-party verification has been completed. Source: performance and target applications

Technical background: chip performance also depends on wiring and heat dissipation

Power equipment needs more than the semiconductors that switch current. It also needs drivers that operate them, controllers that decide how they run, and isolation functions that electrically separate circuits. Traditionally, these components were placed separately and connected with thin metal wires or board traces. The distance between components, the connections, and the paths for heat to escape can limit how small and how capable the whole device can be. Source: EPP technology explainer

A package here is a structure that not only protects the semiconductor but also supports external electrical connections and heat dissipation. A die is a small piece of semiconductor with circuits built in. EPP embeds multiple dies into a silicon wafer-level structure and optimizes the electrical, thermal, and mechanical structure together from the early design stage. Even an electrically excellent layout is hard to use if heat cannot escape, so handling several conditions at once makes sense. Source: integrated structure and design approach

Key points of how it works: replacing thin wires with a redistribution layer

One feature of EPP is replacing conventional wire bonds with a redistribution layer (RDL). Wire bonding connects a semiconductor to its destination with thin metal wires. An RDL forms wiring layers on the structure to connect the needed terminals. onsemi explains that this wafer-level wiring connects multiple dies closely and reduces the parasitic effects that come with wiring. Source: interconnection with RDL

Parasitic inductance is a property of wiring and similar structures that resists sudden changes in current. It occurs even without intentionally added components and is linked to voltage disturbances and noise when current is switched at high speed. So besides improving the semiconductors themselves, shortening the connecting wiring to limit this effect is also important. EPP aims for improvements that include these connections. Source: aim of reducing parasitics

Another feature is the ability to combine Si, SiC, and GaN. Each material suits different voltages and operating frequencies, and this is not about replacing everything with one material. Read technically, the core of EPP seems to be bringing the needed semiconductors and control functions into a common structure, rather than any new material itself. onsemi says it will use standard 12-inch silicon wafer manufacturing capacity for the integration process, but this does not mean SiC or GaN devices themselves will be made on 12-inch wafers. Source: supported materials and manufacturing base

What it means for AI and EVs, and how to read the performance numbers

Power density is a measure of how much power can be handled in a given volume or area. If it is high, the same power may be handled with smaller equipment. In AI data centers, installation space and cooling for power supply and protection equipment are design challenges; in EVs, the volume and losses of power converters are. EPP aims to improve these, but AI computing speed does not rise by the same factor as the power supply's power density. Source: intended uses for AI and EVs

The numbers onsemi gave need to be read separately by target. All of the following are the company's announced figures, not general performance from independent evaluation. Source: performance by application

  • Platform overall: claims 3–5 times the power density of current approaches, depending on the application.
  • Early solid-state circuit breaker: for a device that interrupts current, the company says it was about 50% smaller and ran about 20% cooler than existing designs.
  • EV traction inverter: for a power converter that drives the motor, it claims up to 4 times the power density and 15% lower power loss than conventional approaches.

The comparison baselines, test conditions, and absolute values cannot be confirmed from public materials. In particular, "about 20% cooler" alone does not tell us how many degrees lower it actually is. The inverter's loss reduction rate also cannot be turned directly into an improvement rate for the whole vehicle's power use or driving range. The numbers should be treated as claims for each application and kept separate from proven results in mass-produced vehicles. Source: published performance figures

SUBARU moves to early evaluation, not an announcement of mass production adoption

The connection to Japan is SUBARU. According to a separate release onsemi published on the same day, September 16, 2026, SUBARU, as one of the early evaluation companies for EPP, will get early access to engineering samples, simulation models, and technical support to evaluate power architectures for future electric vehicles. This is a stage of learning the technology and examining whether it can be applied, not a decision to adopt it or an announcement of vehicle models that will use it. Source: onsemi announcement of SUBARU evaluation collaboration

This effort is worth watching as a concrete point for examining whether EPP suits real vehicle designs. On the other hand, the basis for the collaboration details is onsemi's release, and it does not mean SUBARU has published independent performance verification. Joining an evaluation and receiving commercial orders or going into mass production vehicles are separate stages. Source: scope of the collaboration

What to watch next: samples, comparison conditions, and reliability

onsemi expects to start providing EPP samples in 2026 to strategic customers in automotive and AI, among others. This is a future plan, and this announcement alone does not show that sampling has started. Mass production timing and pricing also cannot be confirmed, so we need to wait for more information on when the announced design will be available as a product. Source: sample delivery plan

Points to judge going forward include whether samples are actually delivered, performance data with comparison baselines and test conditions, and reliability for long-term use and resistance to temperature changes. These materials give no specific data on lifespan or automotive quality qualification. EPP shows a direction of designing wiring, heat dissipation, and control as one, but to judge its value, it is important to follow not just the size-reduction multiplier but also whether efficiency and reliability go together in customers' real systems. Source: EPP announcement
