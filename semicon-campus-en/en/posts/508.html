Learning level Beginner

This article is for people who learned about GPUs from short videos and want to grasp how they differ from CPUs and why they are used in AI.

A GPU (Graphics Processing Unit) is a semiconductor that is good at processing large numbers of similar calculations at the same time. It was originally developed for the calculations needed to draw images on a screen. Today, that parallel processing power is also used for the massive calculations behind generative AI.

CPUGood at decisions, ordering, and overall control
GPUGood at processing large numbers of similar calculations
AIRuns on a division of roles between the two

The one sentence to remember first

GPUs are used in AI because they are good at processing large numbers of similar calculations at the same time.

What does a GPU do?

Naoki

Isn't a GPU the part that makes game graphics look nice?

Professor Owl

Image processing is where it started. To create a screen image, you need to repeat similar calculations for a huge number of pixels.

Naoki

So it is like a factory where many workers share the same kind of task.

Professor Owl

Yes. But the factory is just a comparison to help you grasp the degree of parallelism. In reality, many processing units work together to calculate.

CPUs can also run multiple tasks in parallel. What sets GPUs apart is that they are designed to run many more tasks at the same time and increase the total amount of work done.

Summary so far in one line: GPUs are good at processing large numbers of similar calculations in parallel.

Why did it spread from image processing to AI?

Naoki

The calculations for drawing a screen and the calculations for generative AI seem like totally different things.

Professor Owl

The goals are different, but both repeat similar calculations on combinations of numbers many times. That makes it easy to use the GPU's parallel processing.

Naoki

So the system for massive calculation that grew up with images was also used for AI.

Professor Owl

That is a good way to understand it. But it does not mean all AI processing is the same calculation. The parts that are easy to parallelize are handed to the GPU.

Image processingCalculates the color, brightness, and shape of many pixels.
AI calculationsRepeatedly calculates huge combinations of numbers.
Shared strengthCan process work that is easy to run in parallel all at once.

Summary so far in one line: the strength in parallel calculation that grew up with image processing also suited the massive calculations of AI.

How did GPUs go from gaming parts to the stars of AI?

Naoki

Were GPUs made for AI from the start?

Professor Owl

No. 3D games needed similar calculations done all at once for many pixels. That demand is what grew the GPU's massive parallel computing.

What grew GPUs was not AI but the demands of 3D graphics. To draw 3D images smoothly, similar calculations must be repeated many times in a short time for the shapes of 3D objects and the many pixels that make up the screen. In meeting that need, GPUs developed toward running huge numbers of calculations in parallel.

Naoki

But could researchers just use a gaming part for AI calculations as is?

Professor Owl

At first, GPUs were built for image processing and were hard to use for general calculations. Then, in the era of Jensen Huang and others leading NVIDIA, CUDA appeared and opened the door to using GPUs for general parallel computing.

CUDA opened up "the part that draws pictures" to research computing. Even if a GPU has computing power, it will not be widely used if researchers find it hard to handle. CUDA made it easier to apply the GPU's parallelism to scientific and research calculations.

Naoki

Did just opening the door show that GPUs were suited to AI?

Professor Owl

What showed the value concretely was AlexNet in 2012. Alex Krizhevsky and his colleagues used two GeForce GTX 580 cards and demonstrated the effectiveness of GPUs in large-scale image recognition.

AlexNet proved that abilities grown for gaming could be used for AI too. By connecting the computing entry point NVIDIA provided with proof from researchers, GPUs spread into the computing foundation of AI.

GPUs were not born for AI. Games grew them, CUDA opened the door, and AlexNet proved they work for AI.

How do CPUs and GPUs work together?

Naoki

If it is a PC for AI, could it run with just the GPU and no CPU?

Professor Owl

That will not work. The CPU runs the whole system and hands calculations suited to the GPU over to it. The GPU then works through the massive calculations it receives.

Naoki

So it is a division of roles: the CPU directs, and the GPU handles the massive calculations.

Professor Owl

On top of that, the memory that delivers data to the GPU and the software that uses both are also essential.

To keep a GPU working, the data needed for calculations must be delivered fast enough. HBM gets attention in AI GPUs because it delivers large amounts of data with wide bandwidth.

Summary so far in one line: CPUs and GPUs are not rivals. They are partners that share the work each is good at.

A GPU does not make everything faster

To make use of a GPU's strengths, it is important that the work can be easily split up in parallel, that the needed data can be supplied fast enough, and that the software is built for GPUs.

Type of workBetter suited toReason
Many decisions and branches, where order mattersCPUIts design for complex control and running each task quickly pays off
Large numbers of similar calculations that can be split upGPURuns many tasks at the same time and easily increases the total amount of work
Real AI systemsCPU, GPU, and memoryControl, calculation, and data supply need to be combined

Note: Actual speed is not decided by whether you have a GPU alone. It depends on the whole system, including the type of processing, memory, software, and power consumption.

Why are GPUs and HBM talked about together?

The more calculations a GPU runs at the same time, the more data it needs as input. That is why memory bandwidth matters: how much data can be moved in a given time.

HBM is memory that stacks multiple DRAM dies and is mounted close to a GPU or similar chip to get wide bandwidth. The relationship between GPUs and HBM is illustrated in What is HBM?

Frequently asked questions about GPUs

Were GPUs invented for AI?

No. GPUs developed through image processing such as 3D graphics. The massive parallel computing ability that grew there became easier to use for general calculations through CUDA and similar tools, and later spread to AI.

Are gaming GPUs and AI GPUs the same?

They share the basic idea of parallel processing, but they are not identical. AI products are enhanced for their use, with improvements in the operations AI uses heavily, memory bandwidth, connections between products, software, and more.

Does having a GPU make any kind of processing faster?

No. GPUs shine when similar calculations can be split into many tasks. For processing with many decisions and branches, or where data supply cannot keep up, adding a GPU does not always make it faster.
