September 17, 2026: a manufacturing partnership in India announced

On September 17, 2026, Nexperia and Tata Electronics announced a strategic partnership on semiconductor manufacturing and technology development. They will build the foundation to produce Nexperia's MOSFET product lines at the 300mm semiconductor fab under construction in Dholera, Gujarat. They also plan to work together on assembly and testing of Nexperia's discrete semiconductors at the facility in Jagiroad, Assam. Source: Tata Electronics joint announcement

What was announced is a framework for cooperation that includes manufacturing preparation. It is not an announcement that mass production has started, and no target part numbers, production volumes, mass production start date, or contract value were given. The partnership spans three areas: wafer fabrication, assembly and testing, and building the base for technology and related industries. Source: Nexperia joint announcement

What is a MOSFET? A switch that controls power

A MOSFET is a transistor that controls the current flowing between terminals called the drain and source, using the voltage applied to a terminal called the gate. Power MOSFETs, which handle electrical power, work as switches that turn current on and off in power supply circuits and elsewhere. Picturing an "electrical faucet" that opens and closes with an electrical signal makes the role easier to grasp. Source: Nexperia technical note TN00008

When on, it needs low resistance to keep power loss and heat down; when off, it must withstand the voltage applied to it. The drift region inside the device is key to balancing these. According to Nexperia's technical note, this region supports most of the drain-source voltage when the device is off. Lowering resistance while keeping the ability to withstand voltage is an important design challenge. Source: Nexperia technical note TN00008

Real performance is affected not only by the device structure but also by the resistance of connections to the outside and by the paths that carry heat away. To understand power MOSFETs, you need to look not only at how fine the circuitry is but also at current-carrying ability, voltage rating, and heat dissipation. However, the voltage range and specific specifications of the products being prepared for production have not been announced. Source: Nexperia technical note, Joint announcement

Front-end and back-end processes, and what 300mm means

The front-end process forms many semiconductor devices on a thin disk called a wafer. In the back-end process, terminals are connected to the diced chips, which are placed in protective packages and tested. In this announcement, Dholera was named as the site for the MOSFET front-end process, and Jagiroad as the site for cooperation on assembly and testing of discrete semiconductors. Discrete semiconductors are devices such as transistors handled as individual parts. Source: Tata Electronics joint announcement

By covering both processes, the partnership spans the wide range from making chips to finishing them as products. However, specifics on how the processes connect, such as whether chips made in Dholera will be sent to Jagiroad or which packages will be used, have not been announced. The fact that both sites appear in the announcement alone does not mean an integrated production route is complete. Source: Tata Electronics joint announcement

"300mm" is the wafer diameter, or 30 cm. On the other hand, numbers like "28nm" indicate the generation of the manufacturing process. The size of the disk and the generation of the processing technology are separate pieces of information, so describing it as a 300mm fab does not tell you the product specifications. Nor can the technology range the government gave for the whole fab be read directly as the specifications of Nexperia's MOSFETs. Source: Joint announcement, Indian government description of the fab

Background: the fab plan approved in 2024

The Dholera fab is a plan that was already underway before this partnership. On February 29, 2024, the Indian government announced the fab by Tata Electronics and Taiwan's PSMC as an approved project. The investment at the time was 910 billion rupees, with a planned capacity of 50,000 wafer starts per month. The original document says "91,000 crore," and since 1 crore means 10 million, this is the amount. The target areas listed include 28nm high-performance computing chips and power management chips for EVs, telecom, automotive, consumer devices, and more. Source: Indian government, announcement of February 29, 2024

A government document from November 2025 described the Dholera fab plan as using 110 to 28nm manufacturing technology, with a capacity of 50,000 wafers per month, and production starting in 2027. This is the plan for the fab as a whole as published at that time. It does not confirm 2027 as the start of mass production for Nexperia's MOSFETs, and 50,000 wafers per month is not an allocation for the company. Source: Indian government, description of the fab in November 2025

In this announcement, Tata Electronics describes its total investment in the Dholera fab and the Jagiroad facility as $14 billion. This amount also describes both facilities as a whole, not money spent only on the partnership with Nexperia. It is important to read the scale of the fab and the terms of individual manufacturing contracts separately. Source: Tata Electronics joint announcement

Why it matters: it may add manufacturing routes

The significance here can be read as preparing new manufacturing routes in India for both the front-end and back-end processes. For Tata Electronics, it adds a product and technology partner to its manufacturing base. For Nexperia, it widens its manufacturing options. Both companies also state that the goal is to improve manufacturing flexibility and supply chain resilience. Source: Nexperia joint announcement

However, no performance indicators were given that would show supply actually became more stable or manufacturing costs went down. The joint announcement is a primary source on the two companies' plans and goals, but it is not independent verification of the effects. The contribution to the supply chain needs to be evaluated once shippable volumes and the division of roles with existing sites become clear. Source: Tata Electronics joint announcement

What to watch next: product details and the mass production schedule

Points to check going forward are the part numbers and voltage ratings of the target MOSFETs, when prototyping and mass production start, and production volumes. The packaging method used at Jagiroad and logistics between processes will also be useful information. These were not announced this time, and from the description of "building the foundation," you cannot infer that product qualification or mass production preparation is complete. Source: Tata Electronics joint announcement

On the technical side, it is also worth watching "yield," the share of products made that come out as good parts, and the status of electrical characteristic and reliability checks. To judge whether a new manufacturing site leads to stable supply, we need information on production scale and stable output that follows the partnership announcement. Source: Nexperia joint announcement
