On September 29, 2026, Nuvoton Technology Corporation Japan announced the KA85010UA, a sixth-generation battery monitoring IC with built-in EIS. It supports batteries of up to 26 cells and integrates, on a single chip, measurement of cell voltage, current, and temperature plus EIS measurement, which examines the internal state of the battery. The company plans to start providing samples in January 2027. These samples are planned for evaluation; they do not mean mass-production shipments, general sales, or adoption in vehicles or energy storage systems.Nuvoton official announcement, Japanese-language announcement

What was announced

The main feature of the KA85010UA is that it can monitor up to 26 cells in a battery while also performing EIS (electrochemical impedance spectroscopy) measurement. Typical battery monitoring checks each cell's voltage, the current flowing, and the temperature near the surface. This is important information, but it does not let you directly see all the reactions and degradation going on inside the battery.

The company's Japanese-language announcement also lists a voltage measurement accuracy of ±2.0mV (EOL), ISO 26262 ASIL-D support, and AEC-Q100 support. However, these are specifications and compliance claims based on the company's announcement. They should be viewed separately from independent third-party evaluation of mass-produced units or data showing safety benefits in real vehicles or energy storage sites.Announced product information

What kind of technology is EIS?

EIS is a measurement method that applies a small AC component to a battery and finds the impedance from the resulting voltage and current response. Impedance is a concept similar to resistance in DC, but it changes with the AC frequency. The battery's terminals and wiring, electrolyte, electrodes, and internal reactions and diffusion each show up in the response at different frequencies. So examining the change at each frequency may make it possible to analyze changes in state that are hard to see with simple voltage measurement.Nuvoton's explanation of EIS

Another important term is SOH (State of Health). This is a metric that shows how healthy a battery is compared with when it was new, that is, how much of its usable capacity and performance it retains. Combining EIS data with temperature, state of charge, and other factors may help estimate SOH or detect signs of abnormalities. However, being able to capture EIS waveforms is not the same as being able to accurately judge the degree of degradation or a dangerous state.

Why EIS for battery monitoring now?

Electric vehicles and large-scale energy storage systems need to manage many cells safely. Responding only after detecting sudden changes in voltage or temperature may not give a full picture of the early stages of an abnormality or the state of the battery at rest. A technical report by Sandia National Laboratories, posted by the U.S. Department of Transportation's NHTSA, notes that monitoring voltage and temperature alone has limits in predicting changes before thermal runaway, and examines the possibility of using rapid EIS to assess stability and early signs of failure.Technical report posted by NHTSA

On the other hand, conventional EIS has its own challenges. Full-spectrum measurement, which measures a wide range of frequencies one after another, takes time, while measuring only a single frequency gives limited information. Even faster methods can require dedicated measurement circuits and complex analysis. This product can be seen as a step toward making EIS easier to build into battery monitoring systems, not just lab instruments. However, this shows the potential of the technology; it does not mean diagnostic performance has been confirmed for every battery.Background from the technical report

The key is a circuit that reduces heat

Nuvoton says it uses its own inductor method, rather than the conventional resistor method, to generate the AC current for EIS measurement. In the resistor method, current flows through a resistor, so part of the power is lost as heat. In the inductor method, FETs control the direction of the current, and energy stored in a coil is circulated as AC current to reduce losses.

According to the company's evaluation, power loss and board temperature rise during EIS measurement were reduced by about 93% compared with the conventional resistor method. However, this figure is a comparison result under specific conditions shown by the company. The announcement gives different battery voltage and superimposed current conditions for power loss and for temperature rise, so it cannot be confirmed that the same ratio applies to every battery, temperature, load, and board design.Inductor method and evaluation results

The path from research to product

In December 2025, Nuvoton announced joint research with the National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST) in which it built and validated an algorithm that estimates a battery's internal temperature by fitting EIS data, taken under different states of charge and temperatures, to an equivalent circuit model. An equivalent circuit model approximates the complex electrochemical phenomena inside a battery by replacing them with electrical parts such as resistors and capacitors.Announcement of joint research by Nuvoton and AIST

Linking this research with the new IC announcement shows a flow that goes beyond the measurement circuit: interpreting the collected data with models and algorithms and building it into real applications. The company says it compared results with a dedicated EIS instrument and with IEC 62620 1kHz AC internal resistance measurement under specific conditions and confirmed high consistency. However, consistency of measured values does not directly prove that the system can find abnormalities with high accuracy or prevent accidents in a real system.

Limits and points to watch

This announcement alone does not confirm specific abnormality detection rates, false alarm rates, SOH estimation error, internal temperature estimation error, lifetime prediction error, time needed for measurement, or which battery chemistries are covered. EIS diagnostic results may vary with cell design, temperature, state of charge, noise, reference data, analysis models, and judgment thresholds. To use it as a safety function, system validation is needed that covers not only the IC's own performance but everything from data collection to warnings and shutdown control.

Going forward, we want to see whether sample shipments actually begin as planned in January 2027, and whether evaluation kits and detailed measurement conditions are published. It also matters whether the timing of mass-production shipments, customer adoption, long-term evaluation in real vehicles and ESS, support for different battery materials, and the accuracy of diagnostic algorithms are disclosed. What we can say now is only that a plan to build EIS into a 26-cell battery monitoring IC has been announced. It is a step toward bringing research technology into the field, but it is not news that mass production or practical benefits have been achieved.
