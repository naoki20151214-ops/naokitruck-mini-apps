On October 13, 2026, SEMI announced its outlook that equipment spending for 300mm front-end fabs (wafer plants) worldwide will reach $149 billion in 2026 and grow to $227 billion in 2029. The 2026 figure is a forecast of a 31% increase from the previous year. This is not a confirmed total of equipment makers' actual sales, but a market forecast based on fab plans, construction, expected start of operations, and similar information.SEMI announcement

What happened

The core of this news is SEMI's view that the expansion of manufacturing capacity for AI, high-performance computing, memory, and advanced logic will continue. SEMI's 3Q26 edition of the "300mm Fab Outlook" forecasts equipment spending growth of 16% in 2027, 12% in 2028, and 18% in 2029.

It also forecasts construction starts for 300mm fabs or production lines, excluding pure R&D fabs, at 20 worldwide in 2026 and 18 in 2027. In the United States, the figures are 3 in 2026 and 4 in 2027. However, these are forecasts of construction starts, and they do not mean that all fabs have been completed or have started mass production.SEMI news release

What is a 300mm wafer?

A wafer is a thin silicon substrate on which semiconductor circuits are formed. A 300mm wafer is a round substrate 300mm in diameter, or 12 inches. In the front-end process, many copies of the same circuit are formed on a single wafer, and after completion they are cut apart into individual dies. Each die is then put into a package to become a semiconductor product.Intel technical explainer

The larger the diameter, the more chips of the same type can potentially be made from one wafer. On the other hand, larger wafers require manufacturing equipment, wafer handling, temperature control, materials control, and more to be aligned with high precision. So "300mm equipment spending" does not mean buying lithography tools alone. It means investment in the full set of front-end equipment that processes wafers, including deposition, etching, cleaning, and inspection.

Why AI and memory are driving investment

SEMI forecasts that Logic & Micro (including foundry) will remain the largest product segment through 2031, with Memory in second place. For 300mm memory equipment spending, it expects growth of 32% in 2026, 15% in 2027, 12% in 2028, and 21% in 2029. SEMI cites strong AI-related memory demand as the background.SEMI 300mm Fab Outlook

AI training and inference need not only computing chips that process large amounts of data quickly, but also high-performance memory that temporarily holds data. If chipmakers judge that demand will last a long time, that leads to fab expansions and orders for manufacturing equipment. However, these materials alone do not let us separate out, in numbers, how many dollars AI demand added to the investment. The link between AI demand and growing investment is SEMI's market outlook.

A history of 300mm mass production

300mm manufacturing is not a new technology appearing for the first time. On August 22, 2001, TSMC announced that it had produced fully functional 300mm wafers at Fab 12, and that products and test chips made with 0.15µm technology showed high yield comparable to its 200mm manufacturing facilities. Yield is the share of manufactured chips that pass inspection and can be used as products. The higher the yield, the more good chips you get from the same materials and time.TSMC's 2001 announcement

This announcement describes TSMC's results as of 2001. It does not directly prove the performance or cost of every fab today. However, it is a historical example that helps us understand the long track record of 300mm manufacturing moving from the research stage to mass production technology.

Things to keep in mind when reading the numbers

The $227 billion for 2029 is not an actual result but a SEMI forecast. It may change due to the economy, semiconductor demand, delays or cancellations of fab plans, and policy changes. SEMI tracks 424 fabs and production lines worldwide and reflects updates, additions, and closures since the previous edition, but the public page alone does not show a complete breakdown by region, equipment type, or company.

Also, rising capital spending points toward more manufacturing capacity, but it does not confirm actual equipment orders, fabs starting operations on schedule, yield in mass production, chip prices, or whether there will be oversupply. Even after an investment plan is announced, it takes time before equipment is moved in and the first silicon ships.

What to watch from here

  • Whether the fab construction starts forecast for 2026 and 2027 actually begin.
  • Whether equipment move-in, first silicon shipments, and the start of mass production at each fab go as planned.
  • Whether demand for AI logic and memory continues to support growth in equipment spending.
  • Whether growing investment eases supply shortages, or creates oversupply in the future.
  • Whether SEMI's next forecast revises the $227 billion outlook or the growth rates.

This announcement shows that the semiconductor industry in the AI era is investing not only in design but also in large-scale manufacturing infrastructure. However, it is important not to treat forecasts as the same thing as real production growth, and to check actual results at each stage: construction start, equipment installation, yield, and shipments.
