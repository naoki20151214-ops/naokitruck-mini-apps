When we think about AI processing power, GPUs that do the computing are not the whole story. The way data moves between devices also matters. On September 15, 2026, Delos Data announced that it had closed more than $100 million in funding. On the same day, it also announced products such as the "Nonstop AI Data Interface," which supports connections between AI hardware. This news can be read as development investment aimed at improving AI inference through better data movement and continuous operation. Funding announcement, Product announcement

What was announced: a closed round and new connectivity technology

The funding amount disclosed is "over $100 million." Investors named include Matrix, Playground, and Socratic Partners. The company plans to use the money to hire more software and hardware engineers, develop products, and speed up sales. It did not say that all of the money will go into chip manufacturing. Also, September 15 is the date the closing was announced; the date the funds were actually settled was not given. Delos Data's use of funds and funding overview

On the product side, the company announced that it is adding the "Nonstop AI Data Interface" and the "Nonstop AI Reference Architecture" to its lineup. The first is connectivity technology that supports data movement between devices. The second is a design framework for building systems. One point to keep clear: announcing a product concept and completing mass production and shipping are separate stages. This announcement does not show that the connectivity chips are already shipping widely. September 15 product announcement

Why it matters: a focus on "waiting for data," not just compute speed

"AI inference" is the process of using a trained AI model to generate outputs, such as answers to questions. A "cluster" is a setup in which multiple computing devices are connected and work together. Delos targets these groups of inference hardware. The company aims to design the links between compute, memory, and storage as one system, from software to servers to chips. Official product description

To put the company's problem in everyday terms: instead of only adding more workers, it is about improving the routes that deliver the parts they need. Even with powerful computing hardware, long waits for data mean that power goes unused. That is why it makes sense to invest in how connections are designed and managed. However, how much shorter wait times improve inference performance depends on the workload and the hardware combination. Product design philosophy, May technology announcement

Three forms of the Data Interface and the approach to failures

The Data Interface is described as a connection layer placed between devices and the network. An "interface" is the contact point or mechanism used to pass data between different devices. The company has shown three implementation forms, listed below. These should be understood as announced forms, not as evidence of how widely finished products are in use. Data Interface announcement

  • I/O chiplet: Built in as a small semiconductor component that handles data input and output. I/O means input/output, and a chiplet is a small chip that handles one separated function.
  • Near-packaged optics: Optical connections placed close to the chip package. The idea is to bring optical data transmission into the design, not just electrical connections.
  • Card: Connectivity provided as a card-type component.

Beyond these three forms, the company says it handles fault detection and recovery in hardware. The goal is to make it easier to keep processing even when connections are disrupted. But the product name "Nonstop" should not be taken as a guarantee that the system never stops under any failure. The available materials do not show independent testing that would back up such a guarantee. Explanation of forms and fault handling

Background: from a server concept in May to the chip connection layer

This is not the first time Delos has shown its work. On May 27, 2026, it announced a cluster setup and server design that expand computing resources through cable connections. It introduced a design that brings connection I/O to the front of the server and "Mosaic" software that visualizes the connection domain. The September announcement adds a data interface to this effort. May 27 announcement, September additional announcement

In the official product description, Clusters covers designing, testing, and running setups; Server is the platform that links separated devices; and Data Interface is the chip that connects compute, memory, and storage. A shared framework called "MoXI" is designed to treat different types of compute devices, memory, models, and connection setups as one common domain. In other words, this funding supports not only the connectivity chip itself, but also the software and systems that manage how it is used. Product lineup and MoXI

From connectivity chips to reading public documents and PC practice

After reading funding news, sort out which data the connections move and which latency or power figures were evaluated. It is important not to compare product performance based on funding amounts alone. One path is to reread the English public documents; another is to set up a practice environment for small data-processing tasks. Decide the specs you need for a practice PC based on the software requirements, and keep that separate from buying special connectivity chips.

Checklist before comparing: the operating systems the practice software supports, the data size, and CPU, memory, and storage. Separate what you can try with your current setup from what needs an upgrade, and then pick candidates.

What does "10x" measure? Separating company goals from proof

Performance numbers need care. The funding announcement claims a 10x improvement in latency and efficiency, while the product announcement lists 10x goals for performance, fault tolerance, and scale. "Latency" is the waiting time for things like passing data, and "fault tolerance" is the ability to keep working or recover when a failure happens. These are different metrics, so they cannot be lumped together as "AI answers got 10 times faster." Performance wording in the funding announcement, Target values in the product announcement

The materials we could check are the company's own explanations. We could not find independent test results that identify the comparison baseline or measurement conditions. So there is no basis for using these multipliers as measured inference speed, power savings, or cost savings. Explanations appearing on several official pages are also not the same as a third party confirming the performance. Company announcement stating goals

What to watch next: sample delivery and measurement conditions

According to the September 15 announcement, customer samples of the Nonstop AI Server are planned for the end of 2026. This does not mean they have been delivered, nor does it mean general sales or mass shipments have started. This schedule also cannot be taken as the shipping date for every form of the Data Interface. The Data Interface's mass production start date, foundry partner, manufacturing process, confirmed customers, and shipment record are not stated in the materials we could check. Server delivery plan

Going forward, key points will be whether samples are actually delivered and which hardware setups and AI workloads were used to measure the effects. For fault handling, knowing which failures are covered and how long recovery takes would make it easier to judge the benefits of adoption. The core of this news is the funding and a more concrete development plan. Progress on performance and commercialization should be judged later, based on test results and delivery records.
