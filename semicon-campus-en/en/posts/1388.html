On September 29, 2026, Everspin Technologies announced that it had demonstrated a platform that connects MRAM to a server over CXL at the SNIA Developer Conference 2026. This news matters because it shows, in a real system setup, the idea of placing a layer of "persistent memory," which keeps data even when the power is off, between DRAM and NAND SSDs.Everspin official announcement

What was demonstrated

According to the announcement, the demo system consisted of Everspin's PERSYST MRAM, a Wolley CXL controller, an AMD Alveo U250 FPGA, a Supermicro AS-1116CS-TN server, a 32-core AMD EPYC 9355 processor, and a 1GB DDR4 MRAM UDIMM. Everspin and Wolley say they showed a working platform that reads and writes MRAM through a CXL interface.Details of the demo setup

One thing to note: a "demonstration" is not the same as "shipping a mass-produced product." What we can confirm is the announcement of a system that ran at a conference. No price, shipping date, supported OS or middleware, or customer adoption for high-capacity products for AI servers has been announced.

Understanding MRAM and CXL for beginners

What is MRAM?

MRAM is memory that stores data using a magnetic state rather than an electrical state. DRAM is volatile memory that loses its contents when the power goes off, but MRAM is non-volatile memory that keeps data after a power loss. Because of this, it may reduce the need to reload data from an SSD at restart, or to protect DRAM contents with a battery. Everspin also explains that MRAM keeps data after a power loss or system restart, and can reduce reliance on moving data from DRAM to SSD in advance.Explanation of MRAM uses

What is CXL?

CXL is a standard interconnect that links CPUs with memory, accelerators, I/O devices, and more. It does more than just send data. It has "memory semantics," which let CPUs and devices share attached memory in an easy-to-use way, and "coherency," which keeps data consistent. Simply put, it is a way to make it easier to add memory that sits farther away into the system's memory hierarchy.CXL Consortium explanation

CXL 2.0 describes switching to connect multiple devices, memory pooling to share memory among multiple hosts, support for persistent memory, and use alongside DDR memory. This makes it possible to design systems that assign memory to the workloads that need it, instead of tying it to a specific CPU socket.CXL 2.0 features

Why it matters in the AI era

In a typical server, DRAM serves as a fast work area close to the CPU, and NAND SSDs serve as high-capacity persistent storage. DRAM is fast but vulnerable to power loss. SSDs keep data, but using them directly as memory brings access latency and I/O overhead. CXL-connected MRAM is an idea to place a layer between the two that combines persistence with memory-like access.

In AI, candidates include checkpoints, which save the state of training partway through, and the KV cache, which holds past information used in inference. If checkpoints can be saved quickly, it may shorten the time needed to resume training after a failure or restart. If the KV cache can be made persistent, it may also reduce the burden of rebuilding everything after an inference server restarts. However, these are use cases proposed by Everspin, and how much performance or cost would improve for specific AI workloads has not yet been confirmed.Proposed AI uses

How to read the performance claims, and the limits of this news

Everspin says this platform provides nanosecond-class access at the cache-line level and writes up to 100 times faster than NAND-based SSDs. However, this figure is a claim in the company's announcement. The official release does not give the type of SSD compared, access size, queue depth, software setup, power conditions, detailed measurement results, or independent third-party verification. So it cannot be read as "every AI server will be 100 times faster."Company announcement on performance

Also, this demo used a 1GB DDR4 MRAM UDIMM. Large-scale AI needs much more capacity for checkpoints and caches, so capacity density, price, power consumption, write endurance, and yield are barriers to practical use. Other points also need testing: the latency added by passing through a CXL controller or fabric, whether OSes and AI runtimes can handle persistent memory correctly, and in what order data is committed during a power failure.

In addition, "world's first" is Everspin's own wording. It has not been confirmed by a third-party survey of the whole market or by independent certification. This news is an important demo that shows a technical direction, but keep in mind that it does not prove mass-production shipments or broad customer adoption.

Points to watch

  • Whether products or roadmaps that scale beyond 1GB are announced.
  • How much checkpoint save times and restart times are shortened in real AI training and inference.
  • Whether independent benchmarks measured under the same conditions are published.
  • Whether Linux, hypervisors, and AI frameworks can handle CXL-connected MRAM as persistent memory.
  • Whether price, power consumption, endurance, and supply reach realistic levels compared with DRAM and SSDs.

This CXL-connected MRAM is less a finished product that replaces DRAM and more a demo that points toward adding layers to the memory hierarchy of AI servers. Combining MRAM's non-volatility with CXL's sharing and expansion features may reduce the burden of moving data around repeatedly. Going forward, it is important to check four things needed to move from demo to mass-produced product: capacity, price, software, and independent evaluation.
