What happened? An update to a platform for analyzing semiconductor manufacturing data

On September 9, 2026, PDF Solutions announced "Exensio Aurora," a new architecture for Exensio, its analytics platform for semiconductor manufacturing data. The company says it is designed for petabyte-scale data analysis and for the use of agentic AI in manufacturing and supply chains. This news is an announcement of an analytics software design and a release plan. It is not an announcement that performance or quality gains have been proven at customer fabs. Official announcement

The dates also need to be kept apart. September 9 is the date of this press release, but the Aurora name and the background of its development had already been introduced in an official blog post dated August 18. We can sum it up this way: the earlier technical explanation came first, and this announcement added performance targets and a release schedule. August 18 explanation of the development background, September 9 announcement

Why does it matter? To use data across process steps

The existing Exensio product organizes different kinds of data, such as equipment monitoring, testing, assembly, and packaging, and connects it to analysis and machine learning. The company describes two ways to use it: in the cloud, and on-premises, run inside a company's own facilities. The key point is not only storing large amounts of data, but also making it possible to link information from different process steps. Official description of Exensio

The key to understanding how this works is the "semantic model." This is a framework that organizes what data represents and how it relates to other data. For example, if equipment logs and test results are simply stored as separate files, the people doing the analysis have to figure out how the two are related. It helps to think of a semantic model as a shared foundation for handling this content and these relationships in analysis. The existing Exensio is described as a platform that organizes different types of data onto this shared foundation. Official description of the common semantic model

Given this background, Aurora's significance can be read as more than just adding storage. It aims to strengthen the link from analyzing information across process steps to using the results on the factory floor. However, these materials do not tell us how much it will reduce defects or how much it will shorten root-cause investigations. The developer's explanation of linking analysis and the factory floor

Sorting out the technical terms: capacity and analysis speed are separate challenges

A "petabyte" is a unit of data. In decimal terms, 1 petabyte is 1,000 terabytes. But having a large storage space is different from being able to quickly analyze the information you need inside it. What PDF Solutions describes for Aurora is a design meant for analyzing data at this scale. It should not be read as a track record of achieving petabyte-scale processing in customer environments. Official announcement on design scale

A "distributed analytics engine" is a system that splits analysis calculations across multiple computing resources. The idea is like dividing a job of going through a huge pile of documents among several people. But splitting the work and combining the results also take effort, so adding more resources does not always speed things up by the same proportion. For Aurora's distributed analytics engine, the company has set a design target of about 25 times faster performance at a similar hardware cost. Distributed analytics engine and performance target

This "about 25 times" is not confirmed performance that users can expect as is. The announcement does not give details on which version it was compared with, the type of processing, the dataset, the hardware setup, or any third-party verification. It cannot be stretched to mean that every analysis will be 25 times faster, or that a whole fab's productivity will rise 25 times. To evaluate it, we need to know which task was compared under which conditions. Announcement text with the performance target

From manufacturing data analysis to learning about quality and generative AI

When you read news about manufacturing data analysis, keep three things separate: storing data, speeding up analysis, and improving process quality. Experience matching inspection values with lot and equipment histories is a point of contact with quality and process jobs. General generative AI learning can serve as a starting point, for example by trying it out to summarize public information or draft reports. Do not put a fab's confidential data into outside services, and check analysis results against the original records.

Development background: from storage and ingestion to analysis and AI

According to the official explanation on August 18, earlier major updates focused mainly on expanding data storage and ingestion. In developing Aurora, the focus widened from there to analysis, automation, and AI. The challenges the company lists are not only data volume but also differences in data types and formats. The concern is that even at the same volume, data with different content and structure needs different organizing and processing before it can be analyzed. Technical background behind the development

The same explanation also describes a plan to link analysis of stored data after the fact with data collection and control on the factory floor. For example, the idea is to make it easier to check for missing or inaccurate data, and to feed models built through analysis back into floor control. The design philosophy is not to stop at collecting data, but to carry analysis results into the next round of operations. However, the size of that effect has not been confirmed. Explanation of linking the factory floor and analysis

"Agentic AI," which appears in this announcement, refers to a way of using AI that carries out multiple tasks toward a goal. Aurora is designed with this use in mind, but that does not mean it has achieved fully automated fab operations. The development background also stresses reproducible results and a clear view of how outputs are produced when using large language models (LLMs), which generate text and other content. To use AI output in manufacturing, being able to verify the results matters as much as getting them quickly. Design intended for AI use, Explanation of challenges in using LLMs

What to watch next: what the limited beta and public demo will show

The initial release is planned as a beta program for a small number of early adopters during September 2026. A beta is a trial and evaluation stage before wide release. Based on information available as of September 12, we could not confirm that it has started, and the specific start date, customer names, general availability date, and price are unknown. Announced plans and actual use need to be viewed separately. Limited beta release plan

The first public demo is planned for PDF Solutions CONNECT 2026, scheduled for October 15–16, 2026, in San Francisco. Going forward, beyond confirming the start of the beta, it will be worth watching the data volume, type of analysis, computing resources, and the way results are verified in the demo. The company's announcement and technical explanations show the direction, but there is not yet any independent evidence of an edge over competitors or of quality improvements. The next thing to judge is whether the targets turn into measured results with specific conditions. Public demo announcement, CONNECT 2026 event information
