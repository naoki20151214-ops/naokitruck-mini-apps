Inside semiconductor manufacturing equipment, five systems keep things running: vacuum, gas, plasma, transfer, and temperature. This article is a map that sorts the main parts into three groups and summarizes their roles and how they relate. From any part you are curious about, you can go on to a detailed explanation.

What you will learn in this article

  • Which groups the main parts inside the equipment fall into
  • What role each part plays in the equipment as a whole
  • Which article to read first when something goes wrong

1. Parts that create and maintain vacuum (pumping, sealing, measurement)

Many processes take place in a vacuum, where almost all the air has been removed. A stable process needs all three: parts that create the vacuum, parts that protect it, and parts that measure its condition.

  • Turbomolecular pump (TMP) | A pump that creates high vacuum. It works after rough pumping
  • Vacuum valves and gate valves | Connect and separate the chamber, the pump, and the transfer chamber
  • O-rings and vacuum seals | Seal the joints between parts so air cannot get in
  • Vacuum gauges | Measure how strong the vacuum is. The measurable range depends on the type
  • Helium leak testing | Finds where a vacuum leak is by using a helium signal

2. Parts that control gas and plasma

In processes that add thin films or etch material away, gas flows in set amounts, and plasma is created when needed. Used gas contains hazardous substances, so it must be treated.

  • Mass flow controller (MFC) | Keeps the gas flow rate constant
  • RF matching network (matcher) | Delivers high-frequency power to the plasma without waste
  • Abatement systems (scrubbers) | Make the exhaust gas used in the process harmless

3. Parts that hold and transfer wafers and keep the temperature steady

Wafers are moved around the fab, held in place inside the equipment, and kept at a set temperature. If this is disturbed, film thickness and the shape of the processed features will drift.

  • Electrostatic chuck (ESC) | Holds the wafer with static electricity and helps control its temperature
  • Chiller (temperature control) | Keeps the cooling water temperature constant and removes heat from the equipment
  • EFEM, load port, and FOUP | Hand wafers over from their carrier to the equipment
  • Load lock chamber | Moves wafers in and out without opening the vacuum chamber
