What happened? An expanded partnership to protect 800V DC power distribution

On September 9, 2026, Infineon and SolarEdge announced they would expand their partnership into developing solid-state circuit breaker (SSCB) technology for 800V DC power distribution in AI and large-scale data centers. SolarEdge will lead the breaker design, and Infineon will provide the core SiC JFET technology. September 9 is the date the expanded partnership was announced, not the date mass production or shipments began.Infineon announcement

The target is the distribution section from a device called an SST, which converts voltage, to the racks that hold computing servers. The aim is to quickly disconnect the electrical path when abnormal current flows there. This announcement is an expansion of a development partnership. It does not show that a finished product has gone on sale or been installed in customer facilities.SolarEdge announcement

Why does it matter? Stopping DC current takes special care

A circuit breaker is a protection device that cuts off electricity when something goes wrong. A solid-state circuit breaker stops current by controlling semiconductor switches instead of opening mechanical contacts. To understand this partnership, the key point is that power distribution equipment needs not only technology to deliver electricity efficiently, but also technology to disconnect it when a fault occurs. The two companies are trying to put that protection function into the 800V DC path.Joint announcement on DC distribution protection

In alternating current (AC), the direction of current changes periodically, and the current passes through zero along the way. Direct current (DC) has no such natural "current zero." According to the two companies, when DC is interrupted mechanically, dealing with an arc, a discharge that continues between the contacts, is a challenge. Electricity may keep flowing even after the contacts separate, so simply opening the contacts does not solve the problem.SolarEdge explanation of DC interruption

The SSCB is described as being designed to interrupt fault current in a few microseconds. One microsecond is one millionth of a second. However, this is a design description by the companies, not a measured value with test conditions for the jointly developed device. It cannot be read as "a finished product that always protects within a few microseconds now exists."Company description of interruption speed

What is a SiC JFET? A part that balances everyday losses and fault toughness

SiC is a semiconductor material called silicon carbide, and a JFET is a type of device called a junction field-effect transistor. Here, the SiC JFET is the core of the semiconductor switch that lets current flow or stops it. Keeping the SSCB, which refers to the whole device, separate from the SiC JFET, the semiconductor device used inside it, makes their roles easier to see.Division of roles in this partnership, SiC JFET technical materials

A protection switch does not work only at the moment of a fault. It carries current for long periods during normal operation, too. The energy lost to the device's electrical resistance during this time is conduction loss. "On-resistance" is the resistance when the switch is on; for the same current, lower resistance means less of this loss. Infineon's technical materials list normal-operation losses and robustness during faults as key evaluation points for protection devices.Explanation of SiC JFETs for protection circuits

The company also describes linear operation that limits current and tolerance to avalanche when high voltage is applied. In other words, when choosing a device, it must not only conduct electricity easily but also withstand electrical stress during faults. However, this general component description does not tell us the losses, temperature rise, or the size of current the jointly developed device can interrupt.Technical materials on SiC JFETs for protection uses

Background: from a 2025 power conversion partnership to protection

On November 5, 2025, the two companies announced a partnership on the joint design, optimization, and validation of SSTs. SST stands for solid-state transformer, a device that uses semiconductors to convert voltage. The plan at the time set development targets of converting medium voltage to 800 to 1500V DC with efficiency above 99%. These were development targets at that time, not proven performance of the new breaker.SST partnership announcement of November 5, 2025

An SST converts voltage, and an SSCB stops current during faults. This announcement can be seen as extending the earlier power conversion partnership to the distribution protection that comes after it. Its significance is that the companies now handle the power path supporting AI data centers from both the conversion and protection sides. Conversion efficiency and interruption performance are separate evaluation items, so the SST's "over 99%" cannot be used as the SSCB's efficiency.Plan for the earlier conversion technology, Scope of the new protection technology

Limits and what to watch next: check the test conditions for the whole device

Using fast semiconductors alone does not complete a protection device. Infineon's general SSCB explainer lists a current sensor, a control microcontroller, and gate drivers that operate the switches as components, in addition to the power switches. A whole system is needed to measure faults, decide, and operate the switches. This material explains general technology and is not the confirmed specification of this device.Technical explainer on SSCB components

The first thing to watch in future announcements is under what conditions "a few microseconds" is measured. The current joint announcement does not specify the current value, temperature, measurement starting point, or definition of completed interruption. Knowing whether it includes the time to detect a fault, and how large a fault current it can stop, would make it easier to evaluate its protection performance as a device.Published design description

Also, this announcement does not confirm specific part numbers used, rated current, certification of the finished product, price, mass production and shipping timing, or customer installations. Both companies' news releases are based on the same joint announcement, so their publication by two companies cannot be treated as third-party performance verification. At this stage, it makes sense to take this as news that a development direction for protecting 800V DC distribution has been shown, and to follow upcoming test results and product information.Infineon announcement, SolarEdge announcement
