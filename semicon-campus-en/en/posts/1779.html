On October 1, 2026, Infineon announced that it had officially opened a new back-end semiconductor manufacturing site in Samut Prakan, Thailand. The new site handles not only assembly and testing but also wafer testing. It is part of an effort to expand, in Thailand, the manufacturing network for power semiconductors related to automobiles, industrial equipment, energy infrastructure, and more. That said, opening a site is different from carrying out every future plan. The confirmed facts here are the opening of the site and the functions it has, not specific production volumes or effects on supply stability.Infineon's opening announcement

What opened

The semiconductor "back-end process" is the stage that finishes chips as products, after the front-end process that forms circuits on the wafer. In general, it separates chips from the wafer, puts them into packages, and performs electrical tests and more. The new site has assembly and test equipment, a cleanroom, and wafer test capabilities. The opening report from a service linked to Thailand's Board of Investment (BOI) describes it as a factory that combines wafer testing, power module assembly, and IC testing at one site.

Infineon's site description covers power modules, discrete devices, wafer testing, and R&D. The company says the site had about 350 employees at the time of opening, and expects that number to grow to about 1,000 as Module A ramps up. This is the company's expectation, not a figure showing actual staffing after the increase.

Technical terms to know

Wafer testing

Wafer testing is the step that checks the electrical characteristics of chips on the wafer before they are cut out and put into packages. It differs in timing and role from the final test done after packaging. The materials say the new site also handles testing at this wafer stage, but they do not disclose product-by-product volumes or capacity.

Power modules

Power semiconductors are semiconductors that control electric power, such as letting electricity flow, stopping it, or converting it. They are used in electric vehicles, industrial inverters, solar power equipment, power infrastructure, data centers, and more. A power module combines multiple power semiconductors and related parts into a single product. In uses that handle large amounts of power, managing heat, insulation, and reliability is as important as electrical performance.

From the 2025 groundbreaking to the 2026 opening

This facility was not suddenly completed in October 2026. On January 14, 2025, Infineon announced it would build a new back-end factory in Samut Prakan, citing the goals of meeting demand for power modules and diversifying its manufacturing sites. The original plan was to start operating the first building in early 2026. Then, on October 1, 2026, the official opening was announced. This shows the project went through the stages of planning, construction, and opening.2025 groundbreaking announcement

Infineon says it has operated manufacturing and assembly sites in Thailand since 1989. The new site is positioned to expand back-end and related R&D functions, building on this existing regional base. However, the public materials do not confirm which products or processes will be moved from existing sites.

What supply chain diversification means, and its limits

Infineon positions the new site as a facility that diversifies its global manufacturing network and gives customers more supply flexibility. When semiconductor processes and equipment are concentrated in a particular region, disasters, power outages, logistics disruptions, trade restrictions, and similar events can have wide effects. On the other hand, setting up sites in different regions does not always make backup production possible right away. This is because of conditions such as product specifications, certifications, equipment, materials, and customer approval.

The materials do not show which products will be made in what volumes, how work will be shared with existing factories, or whether production can be substituted across multiple regions. For this reason, supply chain resilience should be treated as the aim and assessment stated by Infineon and the Thai government, and we cannot say its real effects have been verified.

How to read the expansion plan and investment amount

The new site starts with Module A and is said to be designed to expand to up to five modules in the future. However, five modules is not a completed result at the time of opening, but a future plan stated by the company and the government. The BOI-linked announcement describes the investment as $1.4 billion, or 48 billion baht. On the other hand, the text of Infineon's opening release does not mention an investment amount. So this amount should be treated separately, as a figure based on the Thai government's announcement rather than a figure announced by Infineon.

The BOI-linked materials also mention a larger cleanroom if the site expands to all five phases, and a plan to employ more than 5,000 Thai workers. These are future expansion and hiring plans, not results as of October 1, 2026. They also need to be read separately from the company's figures of about 350 people and about 1,000 after Module A ramps up, since they refer to different stages.

Environmental aspects and points to check next

For Module A, Infineon highlights 100% green electricity, water reuse, rainwater collection, solar power equipment, and more. A separate company page sets a water recycling target of 25%. These describe facility policies and targets, not actual figures showing the annual power mix, water use, or emissions reductions.

Going forward, we need to check when Module A will be in full operation, whether product-by-product production capacity, utilization, and shipment results will be published, and when expansion to up to five modules will be decided. It is also important whether the hiring plans of about 1,000 and more than 5,000 people turn into actual results, and whether environmental targets are backed by measured data.

This news is not just about one more factory in Thailand. It is an investment that aims to build up manufacturing capabilities in Southeast Asia by combining back-end processing, wafer testing, power modules, R&D, and talent development. However, the confirmed fact of the opening needs to be viewed separately from future expectations about scale-up and effects on the supply chain.
