What is film thickness measurement?

Film thickness measurement means measuring the thickness of thin films formed on a wafer. Semiconductor manufacturing forms many kinds of films again and again: oxide films, insulating films, metal films, barrier films, resist films, and more. If a film is not at its target thickness, it affects the next process and the final chip performance.

For example, if an insulating film is too thin, leakage may increase in places where current should not flow. If it is too thick, the dimensions of the next process or the electrical properties may shift. In CMP, where films are polished away, film thickness measurement is also needed to check for leftover film or over-polishing.

Put simply for beginners, film thickness measurement is "a health checkup for thin films."

It is not just about measuring thickness. It is used to see whether the process is running as usual, whether there is unevenness across the wafer, and whether the wafer can safely move to the next process.

Film thickness measurement closes the loop on the process

Film thickness measurement does not end with producing a number. You form a film with deposition, polish it with CMP, and then measure its thickness. If it turns out too thick, too thin, or too uneven, you go back and review conditions such as gas flow, processing time, temperature, polishing conditions, and equipment maintenance.

Key points of the figure
  • Measure thickness after deposition or CMP
  • Look at the distribution, such as center vs. edge and tool-to-tool differences
  • If abnormal, go back to adjusting conditions or maintenance

This idea also connects to SPC. If you look at film thickness measurements lot by lot, it becomes easier to notice changes such as the process slowly drifting thicker, only one tool running thin, or unevenness growing only at the wafer edge.

Three abnormal patterns in film thickness

The abnormal patterns seen in film thickness measurement fall into three broad types: "too thick," "too thin," and "uneven." The key point is not to feel safe based on the average alone. Even if the average thickness is close to target, a poor distribution across the wafer can lead to performance variation between chips and lower yield.

Key points of the figure
  • Too thick: dimensions and electrical properties in the next process shift
  • Too thin: the insulating or barrier role becomes weaker
  • Uneven between center and edge: suspect equipment conditions or the CMP state
Too thickSuspect a deposition time that is too long, a reaction that is too strong, leftover film, and so on.
Too thinSuspect insufficient deposition, etching or CMP going too far, film damage, and so on.
UnevenSuspect the temperature, gas distribution, polishing state across the wafer, or the equipment condition.

Common methods for film thickness measurement

There are several methods for measuring film thickness. Common ones include reflectometry, which uses reflected light; ellipsometry, which looks at changes in polarization; and XRR, which uses X-rays. In real fabs, the method is chosen based on the film type, its thickness, whether it is transparent or opaque, whether it is single-layer or multilayer, and whether it needs to be measured quickly during volume production.

Some films are easy to measure with light, while others need different methods

What matters in film thickness measurement is not "which method is the most impressive," but choosing "which method fits which film." For films that interact well with light, such as oxide films, nitride films, resist films, and interlayer dielectric films, reflectometry and ellipsometry are commonly used. Their strength is that they do not damage the wafer and can measure quickly on a production line.

On the other hand, for metal films such as copper, aluminum, and tungsten, or for complex multilayer films, optical measurement alone may not be enough. In those cases, methods suited to the purpose are combined, such as XRR using X-rays, estimating thickness or film condition from sheet resistance, and cross-section observation. In other words, film thickness measurement is a job that changes the measuring method to fit "the film material," "the thickness," and "the purpose of the process."

Key points of the figure
  • Reflectometry: reads film thickness from reflected light
  • Ellipsometry: reads film thickness and optical properties from changes in polarization
  • XRR / X-ray: evaluates thin and multilayer films with high precision
  • Mapping: shows the film thickness distribution across the wafer

KLA, Onto Innovation, Nova, and Semilab are well-known companies that offer thin-film measurement and metrology equipment. KLA describes thin-film reflectometers that measure film thickness, refractive index, and more, and Onto Innovation and Nova also offer metrology products for semiconductors.

Connect film thickness measurement and post-maintenance quality checks to real work and further study

In post-maintenance checks, separate "the equipment runs" from "the process results have returned to the expected state." Experience comparing not just average film thickness but also in-wafer distribution and lot-to-lot changes against the equipment history connects to recovery checks and process improvement. Record measurements under consistent conditions, and organize what you handed over to the quality team. Learning the basics of metrology and quality control helps you explain the results.

Why people who understand film thickness measurement are valued in the job market

The value of someone who understands film thickness measurement is not just being able to operate the measuring tool. It lies in being able to look at the measurements and think about which way the process is drifting, which conditions to go back to, and what the possible causes of a yield drop are.

A deposition process engineer looks at film thickness uniformity and lot-to-lot variation and adjusts temperature, pressure, gas flow, and processing time. In CMP, they look at over-polishing and leftover film and think about polishing conditions, slurry, and pad condition. In quality evaluation and inspection jobs, people check when the measurements started changing by combining them with SPC and yield data.

What words appear in job postings?

In job postings, film thickness measurement may appear as is, but it may also appear as "thin-film evaluation," "metrology," "optical measurement," "within-wafer uniformity," "process control," "SPC," or "yield improvement." These keywords are common in postings for deposition, CMP, inspection and metrology, quality evaluation, and process engineer roles.

Metrology roles make good use of lab, analysis, and quality experience

Jobs that specialize in measurement like film thickness measurement may appear in job postings under names such as "metrology engineer," "measurement and evaluation," "thin-film evaluation," or "analysis and evaluation." They are less visible than design or process development roles, but they are important roles that support quality and yield on the production line.

People with experience in science lab work, chemical analysis, physical measurement, instrumental analysis, quality evaluation, or tester measurement can easily describe it as "experience taking measurements, looking at variation, and thinking about possible causes," even with no semiconductor experience. When reading job postings, look not only at equipment names but also at phrases such as "connect measurement data to process improvement," "check within-wafer uniformity," and "manage variation with SPC."
