What was announced on September 16, 2026

On September 16, 2026, NVIDIA published its first MLPerf Inference v6.1 preview submission results for its next-generation AI system, Vera Rubin NVL72. The same day, MLCommons, the organization that runs the benchmark, released the official results and listed Vera Rubin NVL72 as a platform in the "Preview" category. These are measurement results published on September 16, not a preview of a future announcement.NVIDIA announcement, MLCommons official announcement

According to NVIDIA, compared with the previous-generation GB300 NVL72, throughput was up to 3.7 times higher on Qwen3-VL, a model that handles vision and language, and up to 2.5 times higher on the reasoning model DeepSeek-R1. The results are in the MLPerf Inference v6.1 Closed Division, and the comparison submission IDs are listed as 6.1-0106 and 6.1-0074. However, both are maximum values under specified conditions. The announcement does not say the same multiple applies uniformly to every use or every scenario.NVIDIA's explanation of the results

"Up to 3.7x" does not mean 3.7x faster responses

The key technical term is throughput. It means the amount of work that can be processed in a given time. For a road, it is like "how many cars can pass in an hour." In AI inference, it is used to see how many requests and generated tokens the whole system can handle. Even if throughput is 3.7 times higher, the time it takes for one user to receive an answer, that is, the individual response latency, does not necessarily drop to 1/3.7.

MLPerf Inference is a benchmark that measures, under common rules, the performance of "inference," where a trained AI model is given input and generates results. For data centers, there are scenarios such as Offline, which processes large batches of work together; Server, which handles requests arriving continuously; and Interactive, which assumes stricter response times. The "up to 3.7x" here is the maximum multiple across these target scenarios for Qwen3-VL. It does not mean every scenario was 3.7x.MLPerf Inference submission guide, NVIDIA announcement

The Closed Division is a category that uses the same models and reference settings to improve comparability. It is easier to compare than tests with free conditions, but the results are limited to the specified model, precision, software, and system configuration. It does not rate price, power consumption, ease of deployment, or the performance of every real service with a single number.MLCommons official guide

Why compare the whole rack instead of a single chip?

Vera Rubin NVL72 is a rack-scale system that tightly connects 72 Rubin GPUs through NVLink Switch. NVIDIA's technical page lists sixth-generation NVLink bandwidth of 3 TB/s per GPU and 216 TB/s for the whole NVL72. However, the page labels these as preliminary specifications, not final values for production products.NVIDIA NVLink technical page

NVLink is an interconnect technology that lets multiple GPUs exchange data at high speed. Think of it as a wide, dedicated highway linking the GPUs. When a huge AI model is spread across many GPUs, even if each GPU computes quickly, the whole system has to wait if the data exchange in between gets congested. So the real capability of AI chips depends not only on the compute units but also on memory capacity, memory bandwidth, and GPU-to-GPU communication.

Text generation can be roughly split into prefill and decode. Prefill is the stage that reads the input text or images all at once, and decode is the stage that generates answer tokens one by one. The first relies on computing large inputs in parallel; the second relies on low-latency processing while repeatedly reading data from memory, so their bottlenecks differ. Assigning the two to separate groups of GPUs is a way to use the whole rack efficiently.

NVIDIA cites factors behind the performance gain such as Tensor Cores and the Transformer Engine, the low-precision format NVFP4, separating prefill and decode, expert parallelism, and sixth-generation NVLink. Expert parallelism, used in Mixture-of-Experts models, spreads the processing of the expert networks chosen for each input across multiple GPUs. However, the public materials alone do not show how much of the 3.7x or 2.5x each technology contributed.Rubin GPU technical blog, NVIDIA's explanation of the results

Why these results matter

In the AI inference race, what matters is not just a single chip's peak compute but how many users a large model can serve at once. These results show that NVIDIA is combining GPUs, memory, GPU interconnect, low-precision compute, and inference software at the rack level. The inclusion of the vision-language model Qwen3-VL and the compute-heavy DeepSeek-R1 also reflects how communication and memory will matter in future generative AI systems.NVIDIA announcement

On the other hand, this is not a comparison of a single Rubin GPU against a single GB300 GPU. The Qwen3-VL submission used vLLM and NVIDIA Dynamo, and DeepSeek-R1 used TensorRT-LLM. The performance gap is the result of the whole system, including the chips, NVLink, memory, numeric precision, distribution method, and inference software.NVIDIA's description of the submission setup

NVIDIA says higher throughput leads to serving more users and lowering the cost per token. However, this is the company's outlook, not independent proof of the economics. Actual costs vary with equipment price, power, cooling, networking, software, utilization, and service fees. Also, what MLCommons confirms is the v6.1 results, submission categories, participating platforms, and so on. The specific comparisons of up to 3.7x and 2.5x versus GB300 NVL72 are based on NVIDIA's announcement.MLCommons official announcement, NVIDIA announcement

What "Preview" means and what to watch next

The biggest caveat is that Vera Rubin NVL72 is in the Preview category, not "Available." In MLCommons' official guide, Preview is the category for systems expected to become available by the next submission round. This submission is the result of a configuration planned for future release, and it does not mean the real-world performance of a mass-produced system you can buy today has been confirmed. Volume shipment timing, price, cloud availability dates, and deployment costs also cannot be confirmed from these materials.MLCommons results announcement, Official explanation of the Preview category

Going forward, we need to check formal submissions in the Available category, full figures for each scenario, response latency, power consumption, and final system specs. Other points to watch are verification that aligns hardware and software differences with the previous generation, and cost-effectiveness under loads close to real services. An accurate summary of this news is: "In a comparison using a Preview system, NVIDIA announced that Qwen3-VL throughput was up to 3.7 times that of GB300 NVL72, and DeepSeek-R1 up to 2.5 times." You cannot say that "all AI processing or response speed became 3.7 times faster."
