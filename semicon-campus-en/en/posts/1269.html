On September 23, 2026, Synopsys, a leading semiconductor design software company, announced an expanded collaboration with TSMC and new tools to support AI chip design. The scope covers design flows for TSMC's A14, agentic AI, advanced packaging that combines multiple chips, optical interconnects, and semiconductor IP for N2P. This news is easier to understand not as the launch of a new single-chip product, but as a stronger design foundation that handles advanced process, design software, IP, and packaging together. Synopsys official announcement

What was announced?

According to Synopsys, it is advancing certification of digital and analog design flows for TSMC's A14 and has built in agentic AI features. The announcement covers migrating existing analog designs to the new process, analyzing digital designs using LLMs, and co-optimizing chiplet floorplans with 3DIC Compiler.

Agentic AI is an automation approach that does more than answer questions: it carries out multiple steps in a row, such as exploring, analyzing, fixing, and verifying, based on a designer's instructions. For example, it can help try several layout options based on design conditions, compare the results, and find problem areas. However, what we can confirm this time is a company announcement that Synopsys has built this feature into its design flows. It does not show how many percent design time was shortened or how much performance or power improved in actual customer products.

CoWoS and power design matter for AI chips

In AI chips, it is becoming common to combine multiple compute dies and high-bandwidth memory such as HBM into one package, rather than using just one large chip. TSMC's CoWoS is a 2.5D packaging technology that places multiple compute dies and HBM on a wiring substrate called an interposer. Splitting up chips increases flexibility in design and manufacturing, but wiring, power, heat, and signal quality must all work at the same time.

Synopsys said it will provide an environment in 3DIC Compiler for designing and simulating integrated voltage regulators (IVRs) in multi-die designs on CoWoS. An IVR places the voltage-converting circuit close to the circuits that use the power, in some cases inside the package or near the die. This makes it easier to deliver power over a short distance, but it can also add heat, noise, and area, so it is not a technology where you only need to think about power.

In official materials from April 2026, TSMC said it is producing CoWoS products at 5.5 reticle size. It also plans volume production in 2028 of a 14-reticle-size version that can integrate about 10 large compute dies and 20 HBM stacks. This is a future plan and does not mean that spec is already in volume production as of 2026. TSMC official technology symposium material

What do optical interconnects and CPO solve?

In AI data centers, moving data between chips and between servers, not just between compute chips and memory, uses a lot of power and time. CPO is a technology that places optical engines near electrical switches or compute systems, or in the same package. Compared with long-distance, high-speed communication using only electrical signals, it is expected to help reduce the power and latency needed for connections.

Synopsys said it will continue a CPO design and verification flow for TSMC's COUPE that combines 3D packaging, co-design of optical and electronic circuits, multiphysics analysis including heat and electromagnetics, and optimization. If optical parts, electronic circuits, and packaging are designed separately, performance or heat problems may only be found at the final stage. Handling them in the same design environment is the key meaning of this collaboration.

On the other hand, the statement in TSMC's official material that it "plans to start production of COUPE on substrate in 2026" is a statement of plans. It does not mean that, as of the September 23, 2026 announcement, COUPE volume production or broad adoption in customer products had been confirmed. TSMC's explanation of COUPE

What IP for N2P and UCIe mean

Synopsys announced that it has reached enablement or tape-out milestones for TSMC's N2P in IP such as PCIe 7.0, HBM4, LPDDR6/5X/5, 224G Ethernet PHY, UCIe, OTP, and PVT monitoring. IP is a design asset for a functional circuit that can be built into a chip. It reduces the burden of designing every circuit from scratch in-house and makes it easier to use standardized communication and memory interfaces.

UCIe is a standard for connecting different chiplets inside a package. It serves as a common interface when AI accelerators, CPUs, I/O circuits, and so on are designed as separate dies and combined. Synopsys also claims silicon demonstration of UCIe-A 32G and 40G using a TSMC N3P test chip and a CoWoS-S interposer, and completed tape-out of 64G UCIe IP at 2nm and 3nm.

What to note here is the difference in stage each term indicates. "IP enablement" means the IP is ready to use in designs, "tape-out" means the design data for manufacturing has been finalized, and "silicon demonstration" means operation has been confirmed on a prototype chip. All are important progress, but none automatically guarantees volume adoption, volume-production yield, or the performance of real products.

Limits of this news and points to watch

From this announcement, we cannot conclude that AI chip design will immediately speed up or that product power or performance has improved. No independently verified figures are given, such as the reduction in design time from agentic AI, PPA (performance, power, area) comparisons, customer names, the number of volume adoptions, or yield. A14 certification, IP for N2P, and UCIe demonstrations are also results based on Synopsys's official explanation.

Going forward, checking the following points will make it easier to judge how effective the announcement really is:

  • How much schedule, power, performance, and area changed in actual designs using agentic AI.
  • Whether the N2P IP and UCIe are adopted in customers' volume-production chips.
  • Whether the planned start of COUPE production becomes reality, and whether yield and reliability of optical interconnects are shown.
  • Whether the large CoWoS plan proceeds on schedule and can be produced stably as a system including HBM, heat, and power.

TSMC describes A13 as a direct shrink of A14, with backward compatibility with A14 design rules and volume production planned for 2029. This is also a plan on the roadmap. This news shows that competition in AI chips is expanding beyond scaling to the combined strength of design automation, chiplets, power, optical communication, and packaging.
