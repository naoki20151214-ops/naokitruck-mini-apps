On September 24, 2026, Microchip Technology announced the PAC1761 and PAC1861, digital power monitors for 48V power systems. Both chips measure voltages up to 65V. They monitor not only voltage, current, and power, but also energy accumulated over time and sudden load changes. They are positioned as parts that give a detailed view of power conditions in equipment with rising power density, such as AI data centers, automotive, communications, and industrial equipment.Microchip official announcement

What was announced

The PAC1761 is a 12-bit digital power monitor, and the PAC1861 is a 16-bit one. The bit count is a rough guide to how finely a measured value can be divided into steps when it is digitized. For the same measurement range, 16 bits generally handle smaller changes more easily than 12 bits. However, real accuracy is not decided by bit count alone. It also depends on the sensing circuit, noise, board layout, calibration, and more.Microchip product portfolio

For both products, Microchip describes measurement up to 65V and spike protection up to 75V. A spike here is a sharp, temporary rise in voltage on a power line. It can happen from switching in motors or power converters, or when loads are connected or disconnected. Protection up to 75V shows a margin for events that briefly go beyond a normal 48V system. It does not tell us how long or against what waveforms it protects, or how robust the whole circuit is. This is the manufacturer's specification, and the materials we checked contain no independent third-party test results.Official release

The products are also said to support programmable alerts when voltage, current, or power exceeds a set value, step limits that detect sudden load changes, and thresholds for accumulated energy. The PAC1861 product page lists I2C communication, accumulation, bidirectional measurement, alert support, a high-side current sensor, and "in production" status.PAC1861 official product page

How is "power" different from "energy"?

Power is a value that shows how much work is being done at a given moment. For example, how many watts a server uses at a certain point in time is power information. Energy, on the other hand, is power added up over a period of time. It is similar to the watt-hours used on electricity bills, and it shows consumption over a period, not just short peaks.

In servers with AI accelerators, the load can change suddenly depending on the workload. Looking only at instantaneous power can make it hard to evaluate short swings or repeated load patterns. With accumulated-energy registers and threshold alerts, a microcontroller or management software can use consumption over a period, not just single overvoltage or overcurrent events, as an input for power control and condition monitoring. However, this means you can get more monitoring data. Simply adding the IC does not guarantee better efficiency or fewer failures.Microchip product information

Why 48V power shows up in the AI era

When delivering the same amount of power, a higher voltage means less current is needed. Losses from the resistance of wires and busbars are roughly proportional to the square of the current. So raising the voltage from 12V to 48V makes it easier to deliver the same power with less current. This helps reduce wiring losses and the current flowing through conductors.

However, as AI accelerators get denser and rack power rises, even 48V systems face high currents, heat, sudden load changes, and protection coordination during faults. The Open Compute Project (OCP) explains that, against this backdrop of long-term power growth, Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and others are developing common requirements for 800VDC. 800VDC is not a plan to replace existing AC equipment all at once; a gradual adoption path that works with existing equipment is also expected.OCP explanation

Even if 800VDC spreads, not every device will run at the same voltage. A Renesas white paper describes a setup that uses DC/DC conversion from 800V to 48V and keeps using the existing 48V ecosystem. In other words, future data centers may have high-voltage distribution and 48V systems near racks and boards existing side by side for a while. In that case, power needs to be measured and problems detected at each voltage level.Renesas white paper

The technical meaning of these products

This news is not about changing the 48V distribution voltage itself. What matters is that it adds time-based information to existing or transitional 48V power systems. If instantaneous voltage, current, and power, plus accumulated energy, sudden load changes, and over-limit conditions can be sent to the control side, power management software can use more information for control.

For example, power converters or system management could respond when a sudden load change is detected, or energy use over a period could be monitored and used for operating decisions. However, how much benefit you get depends on the combination of shunt resistor, board layout, power converter, firmware, protection circuits, and cooling design. The uses Microchip lists, such as AI data centers and automotive, are target fields. They do not show that a specific customer has adopted the chips or built them into a production design.Microchip official announcement

Once you understand power monitor ICs, record voltage, current, power, and time-accumulated energy separately. It is important to tell measurement resolution apart from real accuracy and to keep evaluation conditions the same. Already on sale, but with limits to check

Microchip says the products are available at launch. It lists specified MSOP-10 versions from US$0.56 each in 10,000-unit quantities, and the EV12R33A evaluation board at US$49. The official PAC1861 product page also says it is in production. This shows the products are not just planned but can be bought and evaluated. It does not guarantee stock or lead times for every part number. The price is also a starting price that varies by version, quantity, region, and supply terms.Pricing and availability

Also, the materials we checked do not confirm measurement accuracy, bandwidth, alert latency, the specific time conditions for 75V spike protection, the scope of automotive qualification, or the size of efficiency gains on real hardware. Performance in manufacturer announcements needs to be kept separate from performance verified independently by third parties. Deciding on actual adoption requires checking the electrical characteristics in the datasheet, measuring on the evaluation board, testing under temperature and load changes, and confirming supply stability.

What to watch next

The first thing to watch is whether concrete evaluation results or design examples using the PAC1761 and PAC1861 are published. Especially important are detection performance during sudden load changes, error in accumulated energy values, stability in noisy environments, and how the chips work with power converters and protection circuits.

Second, we need to see how fast voltage architectures in AI data centers change. Even if 800VDC standardization moves forward, 48V rack distribution and board power after conversion will not necessarily disappear soon. Monitoring may be needed at several voltage levels: the high-voltage side, the 48V side, and lower voltages. These products will likely be judged as parts that give a detailed view of 48V systems during that transition.

In conclusion, the PAC1761 and PAC1861 will not solve the power challenges of the AI era on their own. But the idea of passing not just the moment-to-moment state of power, but also transients and time-accumulated energy, to the control side is becoming more important in equipment with rising power density. It is important to follow future design examples while separating the products' availability (announced and in production) from independent benchmarks and adoption records that are not yet confirmed.
