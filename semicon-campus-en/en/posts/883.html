What was announced: process development aimed at lower optical loss

On September 9, 2026, Xanadu and ASML announced a collaboration to develop lithography processes for Xanadu's photonic quantum hardware. The goal is to control the shapes of patterns formed on the chip more precisely and to explore processing methods that lead to structures with less light loss. This is an announcement of technology development aimed at lower loss, not a report that lower loss has been achieved.Xanadu official announcement

The company release was posted at 7:00 a.m. U.S. Eastern Time on September 9. That date can be confirmed as when the collaboration was made public, but it does not tell us when the contract was signed or when experiments began. Also, the articles on the official site and GlobeNewswire are the same company announcement, not materials in which separate organizations verified the technical results.Original company release

Why roughness in the "light path" is a problem

The first term to know is the optical waveguide. It is a structure that guides light within the chip, and beginners can think of it as a "path for light." It should be distinguished from metal wiring that carries electricity. The shape of that path and the roughness of its sides after processing are important factors in how well light can pass with little loss.Research on roughness and loss in photonic devices

The specific issue the announcement named is LER (line edge roughness). It refers to fine, irregular bumps along the outline of a line. Even if you design a line as if drawn with a ruler, the actual outline wavers when you look closely. Despite the name, it does not mean a defect only at the end of a line. Xanadu explains that this roughness can affect light loss in photonic quantum processors.Xanadu official announcement

However, LER, measured on the pattern outline, and "sidewall roughness," measured on the sides of the waveguide after processing, cannot be treated as the same measurement. It is important to connect the outline control in the announcement with the sidewall quality in the background research while keeping in mind the differences in where and at what step they are measured.Collaboration announcement, Sidewall roughness research

Lithography is not only about making things smaller

Lithography (pattern formation using exposure) is the technology that transfers designed patterns for chip manufacturing. However, the shape in the design does not appear on the material exactly as drawn. According to ASML's technical explanation, the transferred image is distorted by diffraction, where light spreads, and by the physical and chemical effects of the light-sensitive resist layer. To think about outline quality, this deviation has to be controlled.ASML technical explanation

One countermeasure is computational lithography. It tunes a manufacturing model with data from tools and test wafers, predicts distortion, and corrects patterns such as those on the mask, which serves as the master. Think of it as adjusting the original shape in advance, expecting it to warp when copied. This is a general technique described by ASML, not a method announced for use in this collaboration.ASML computational lithography

With this background, what to watch here is "how precisely the intended outline can be formed," not just shrinking dimensions. It can be seen as applying semiconductor patterning technology to the challenge of processing quality in structures that carry light. However, this is an interpretation based on the announcement and general technology, not experimental results showing the size of the improvement.Development plan of the collaboration, Technical background

Ongoing research: loss is not decided by lithography alone

The link between processing roughness and light loss is not a new issue. In a study first released on May 24, 2021, Samantha Roberts and colleagues compared roughness from DUV lithography, electron-beam writing, and different etching processes in silicon nitride photonic devices. Etching is processing that selectively removes material to create shapes. The second version, currently posted, was released on July 3, 2022.Abstract and submission history

The study's loss prediction model uses waveguide width and bend radius in addition to sidewall roughness. Bend radius is a dimension showing how gentle a curve in the light path is. This study serves as background showing that, when thinking about loss, you need to look at the shape of the structure as well as roughness. On the other hand, it is not a result of this collaboration, and its materials and results cannot be applied directly to Xanadu's chips.Research on roughness, shape, and loss

What is still unknown and what to watch for next

The release does not state any improvement in light loss, measurement conditions, specific lithography tools, exposure wavelength, or mass-production start date. Working with ASML alone does not tell us whether EUV, High-NA EUV, or DUV will be used. Target materials, waveguide dimensions, and roughness targets also cannot be confirmed from this announcement.Disclosures in the official announcement

When reading future announcements, checking the following three points will make it easier to judge how far the process development plan has turned into results. These check items are based on information not yet disclosed and on background research.Collaboration announcement, Technical background on loss evaluation

  • What was made: Are the materials, waveguide shapes, and lithography and processing conditions disclosed?
  • What improved: Are there results on outline roughness and light loss, with comparison baselines and measurement conditions?
  • What stage was reached: Are process exploration, prototype evaluation, and the start of mass production clearly distinguished?

Lower loss and future manufacturing and commercialization should, for now, be read as the companies' goals and expectations. This collaboration matters because it spells out a concrete manufacturing challenge, but it does not prove that the light-loss problem has been solved, that error correction has been achieved, or that the timeline for practical use has moved up. The next focus is whether measurement results follow the development plan.Company announcement and forward-looking statements
