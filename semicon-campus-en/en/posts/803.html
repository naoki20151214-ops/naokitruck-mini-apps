Kioxia and SanDisk to invest about ¥5 trillion in Japan, boosting flash memory output for AI demand

Kioxia and US-based SanDisk have announced a plan to invest a total of about ¥5 trillion (more than US$31 billion) by 2032 in their flash memory business in Japan.

The investment centers on the Yokkaichi Plant in Mie Prefecture and the Kitakami Plant in Iwate Prefecture. The companies will keep strengthening production equipment, related infrastructure, and technology development to meet growing flash memory demand driven by AI and data-intensive applications.

The key point is that this is not just a factory expansion.

It is a large investment to strengthen, over the long term and inside Japan, the supply of NAND flash that stores the ever-growing data of the AI era.

What happened?

On August 27, 2026, Kioxia and SanDisk announced that they expect to invest a total of about ¥5 trillion (more than US$31 billion) in Japan by 2032.

The plan assumes government support. It will strengthen production equipment, related infrastructure, and technology development at the Yokkaichi and Kitakami plants.

The two companies have jointly developed and produced flash memory for more than 25 years, and have so far made about ¥9 trillion (more than US$50 billion) in capital investment in Japan.

The new ¥5 trillion is a large plan to expand that partnership further into the 2030s.

The key points

  • About ¥5 trillion invested in Japan
  • The period runs through 2032
  • Two sites strengthened: Yokkaichi and Kitakami
  • The background is growing AI and data demand
  • The investment assumes government support

Why invest about ¥5 trillion now?

The background is the growth in data volume driven by the spread of AI.

As generative AI, AI agents, and on-device AI spread, the world needs not only high-performance GPUs and CPUs but also huge amounts of storage for models, training data, inference data, logs, and more.

In AI infrastructure, the roles are divided like this:

Semiconductors that compute
↓
DRAM and HBM that pass data quickly
↓
NAND flash that stores large amounts of data

That is the division of labor.

With this announcement, Kioxia and SanDisk made clear their intention to strengthen flash memory production capacity and technology over the long term to support the growth of AI and a data-driven society.

Image note: AI drives storage demand

A new K3 building is also getting started in Kitakami

On the same day, August 27, Kioxia announced that it had begun land preparation at the Kitakami Plant to build its third fab building (K3).

K3 will be built south of the existing K2 building, with the goal of starting operations during fiscal 2029.

Kioxia expects the flash memory market to grow over the medium to long term as agentic AI, physical AI, on-device AI, and similar technologies are put into real-world use.

However, the company plans to decide the timing of K3 construction and specific investment amounts for production equipment while watching market trends.

Image note: New K3 in Kitakami

Strengthening two sites in Japan: Yokkaichi and Kitakami

This ¥5 trillion investment is not only about Kitakami.

Kioxia and SanDisk say they will keep strengthening production equipment, infrastructure, and technology at both the Yokkaichi and Kitakami plants.

The Yokkaichi Plant has grown over many years into Kioxia's main flash memory production site.

At the Kitakami Plant, K2 began operating after K1, and production of 10th-generation 3D flash memory started there in 2026.

If K3 is added, Japan's flash memory production capacity could become even stronger over the medium to long term.

Image note: Two sites in Japan strengthened

Why are Kioxia and SanDisk investing together?

The two companies are not just business partners.

For more than 25 years, Kioxia and SanDisk have carried out joint development and joint investment in NAND flash.

With each new generation of flash memory, both manufacturing equipment and R&D become enormously expensive.

So the two companies have jointly built up

  • Technology development
  • Production equipment
  • Production capacity

and have increased their competitiveness while sharing the investment burden.

This ¥5 trillion investment is an extension of that long-term partnership.

Image note: More than 25 years of joint development

It also means Japan-US semiconductor cooperation

On August 27, 2026, Kioxia President Hiroo Ota and SanDisk CEO David Goeckeler visited the Prime Minister's Office and paid a courtesy call on Prime Minister Takaichi.

In their joint announcement, Kioxia and SanDisk explained that this investment is also in line with the Japanese government's economic policy goals and the strengthening of Japan-US relations.

This large investment is not just capital spending by two companies. It is also drawing attention on the policy side, in terms of Japan's domestic semiconductor supply and Japan-US cooperation.

Why does this matter for Japan's semiconductor industry?

In recent years, TSMC Kumamoto and Rapidus Hokkaido have drawn a lot of attention in Japan's semiconductor investment.

But Kioxia and SanDisk's ¥5 trillion investment shows that investment continues so that Japan remains a global production base in memory as well.

This is not a temporary production increase.

As a long-term plan through 2032, they intend to keep strengthening

  • Production capacity
  • Manufacturing infrastructure
  • Technology development
  • Next-generation 3D flash

on an ongoing basis.

When looking at Japan's semiconductor industry, you need to look not only at logic chips but also at memory and storage.

Image note: Strengthening Japanese memory

How far will NAND grow in the AI era?

When people talk about AI chips, NVIDIA's GPUs and HBM tend to get the attention.

But across an entire AI system, SSDs and NAND flash that store huge amounts of data are also essential.

Going forward, as

  • AI data centers
  • AI agents
  • On-device AI
  • Self-driving cars and robots
  • High-capacity enterprise storage

expand, the amount of data to store will also grow.

In July 2026, Kioxia and SanDisk announced the start of 10th-generation 3D flash memory production at Kitakami Plant K2.

This ¥5 trillion investment can be seen as groundwork for capturing this growing AI and data demand over several years.

Image note: NAND is also a star in the AI era

Three points to watch going forward

1. How quickly the ¥5 trillion is carried out

This plan is a long-term investment through 2032.

Depending on government support, market conditions, and memory demand, the actual timing and size of capital investment could change.

2. Whether Kitakami K3 starts on schedule

K3 aims to start operations during fiscal 2029.

What matters is when equipment installation begins and which generation of BiCS FLASH it will mass-produce.

3. How far storage demand for AI will grow

How much AI investment boosts demand not only for GPUs and HBM but also for NAND and SSDs will determine the medium- to long-term return on investment.
