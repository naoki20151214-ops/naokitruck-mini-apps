On October 9, 2026, Kioxia announced the "KIOXIA LD4 Series," an SSD for data centers. It is the company's first product to use the E1.L form factor, and it carries QLC NAND based on 8th-generation BiCS FLASH. At the time of the announcement, samples were shipping in 15.36TB and 30.72TB capacities, and Kioxia explained that its validated architecture can support configurations of up to 122.88TB.Kioxia official announcement

What was announced

The LD4 Series targets high-density 1U servers and "read-intensive" storage uses, where most of the work is reading data. It complies with NVMe 2.0e, and Kioxia's announcement says it supports the OCP Datacenter NVMe SSD Specification v2.6. For the interface, the announcement lists "PCIe 5.0 (Gen 4 x4)," with a note of up to 16GT/s x4.Product specifications and announcement

However, the important point here is that not every capacity is already in mass production or on sale. The 15.36TB and 30.72TB units are samples for functional evaluation, and their specifications may change in mass production. Also, 122.88TB is the capacity ceiling described as "possible with the validated architecture." No mass production date, general sales date, or price has been announced for a 122.88TB model.

Understanding QLC and E1.L for beginners

What is QLC?

QLC is a method that stores 4 bits of data in each memory cell of NAND flash memory. Because it increases the amount of information each cell can hold, it makes it easier to build higher-capacity SSDs in the same size. In environments where stored data is growing quickly, such as AI training data, inference data, logs, and backups, higher capacity density is a big advantage.

On the other hand, using QLC alone does not decide a product's performance or lifespan. The controller, firmware, spare area, access patterns, and cooling conditions also matter. This announcement positions LD4 for read-focused uses, but it does not give specific write performance, endurance, power consumption, IOPS, or price. So you cannot judge from the word "QLC" alone that it is fast and durable for every use.

What is E1.L?

E1.L is the long version of EDSFF, an SSD form factor for data centers. A form factor is a design standard that covers an SSD's outer shape, how it connects, how it is mounted, and so on. E1.L was developed around the idea of using its long shape, which fits in a 1U server, to make it easier to place storage in the limited space at the front. Compared with traditional 2.5-inch or U.2 drives, the aim is to make it easier to use the server's overall space for more capacity.Form factor explainer by Solidigm

NVMe is a set of specifications that lets host software communicate with non-volatile memory over PCIe and similar links. E1.L, which is the SSD's physical shape, and NVMe, which is the communication method, are separate concepts, but they are used together in data center SSDs.Official NVM Express specification

Why it matters for AI data centers

In AI systems, it is not only GPUs and CPUs that matter. Storage for training data, models, inference caches, and generated results is also important. If more storage devices can fit in a 1U server, the amount of data handled in the same rack space may increase. LD4 stands out because it combines QLC for high capacity with E1.L for mounting density, and it is offered for read-focused uses.

The OCP data center NVMe SSD specification sets requirements for E1.L, including at least PCIe Gen4 x4, activity and error LEDs, protection against NAND block and plane failures, and requirements for temperature, SMBus, and power control. In other words, E1.L is not just a shape. It is tied to data center implementation requirements that include operation, maintenance, and thermal design.OCP Datacenter NVMe SSD Specification v2.7

However, adopting E1.L does not automatically increase the capacity of the whole rack. The actual number of drives you can install depends on the server chassis, front I/O, power, cooling, redundancy, storage controllers, and more. In exchange for higher capacity density, handling power density and heat also becomes a design challenge.

What we still don't know from this announcement

What we can confirm now is the product direction and the fact that samples are shipping. Independent benchmarks have not proven that it is faster than other companies' SSDs, or that it always gives more capacity in the same rack. Also, the 122.88TB that Kioxia mentions is a configuration the company says it can support, and it should be kept separate from the capacity of mass-produced models you can buy today.

  • When samples and mass production of the 122.88TB configuration will start
  • Usable capacity, random and sequential performance, IOPS, and throughput
  • Write endurance, warranty terms, power consumption, and cooling requirements
  • Price, regions of general sale, and compatibility with server makers
  • How fully it meets each OCP v2.6 requirement, and whether it has third-party certification

For this reason, the current assessment is "a product announcement that shows a direction toward higher capacity density in AI data centers." To judge its real value for deployment, we need to wait for sample evaluations, spec sheets for the mass-production version, real-world benchmarks, and warranty and endurance data.

What to watch next

Kioxia says it will exhibit LD4 at the OCP Global Summit, scheduled for October 12–15, 2026, in San Jose, USA. This is a planned exhibit, not a mass production or sales start date. At the exhibit, the key points are how much Kioxia explains about mounting in real chassis, cooling, management features, and capacity configurations.Planned exhibit at the OCP Global Summit

Going forward, it will be important to check whether the 122.88TB configuration is offered as an official product, and what performance the 15.36TB and 30.72TB samples show. In storage competition in the AI era, you need to look not only at the maximum capacity number, but also at usable capacity per rack, read performance, power, cooling, endurance, and price together. The LD4 announcement is news that gives us a chance to think about these evaluation points.
