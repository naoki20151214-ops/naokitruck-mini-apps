An equipment engineer is "the person who keeps equipment running"

  1. Start up: Bring new equipment into the fab and adjust it until it runs
  2. Protect: Prevent breakdowns through inspections, parts replacement, and preventive maintenance
  3. Fix: When something goes wrong or the tool stops, isolate the cause and restore it
  4. Improve: Review downtime, failure frequency, and work procedures
  5. Connect: Work with manufacturing, process, quality, and equipment makers

Learn through a conversation between Naoki and Professor Owl

Naoki

A job posting says "equipment engineer." Does that mean a designer who builds equipment?

Professor Owl

Sometimes it leans toward design, but in job postings it is usually work involving the start-up, maintenance, troubleshooting, and improvement of manufacturing equipment. It's easiest to think of it as a technical job that keeps equipment running in the fab.

Naoki

How is it different from a process engineer? Both seem to use equipment.

Professor Owl

They work in very similar settings. But a process engineer looks at process conditions, meaning "how to make it." An equipment engineer looks at "whether the equipment runs stably." In cooking terms, process is like the recipe, and equipment is like checking the condition of the oven and cooking tools.

What does an equipment engineer do?

Semiconductor manufacturing uses a great many tools, such as deposition, lithography, etching, cleaning, CMP, and inspection equipment. This equipment does not run just by turning on the power. It combines temperature, pressure, vacuum, gases, chemicals, wafer transfer, sensors, software, safety mechanisms, and more.

Equipment engineers do start-up, maintenance, inspection, failure response, and improvement to keep this equipment running stably. If you work for an equipment maker, the job may be close to that of an FSE who goes to customer fabs to support the equipment. If you work for a semiconductor maker, it is closer to equipment maintenance and equipment improvement inside the fab.

1. InstallationBring the equipment into the fab and check piping, power, communications, and safety conditions
2. Start-upRun the equipment and check that it processes wafers to specification
3. MaintenanceReplace consumable parts, inspect, clean, and calibrate
4. RecoveryIsolate the causes of unexpected stops and errors, and restore the tool quickly and safely
5. ImprovementReduce failures and raise uptime and ease of work

What to check before applying

For equipment engineers and FSEs, the way you work varies greatly by job. Before applying, check not only the duties but also the work schedule and whether business travel is involved.

  • What equipment will you handle? Deposition, lithography, etching, cleaning, CMP, inspection, etc.
  • Is it on the semiconductor maker side or the equipment maker side?
  • Is there business travel or work at customer sites?
  • Is there night shift, shift work, or on-call duty?
  • Training programs, tool and safety training, and whether cleanroom work is involved
  • Which is emphasized: mechanics, electrical, controls, vacuum, gases, or chemicals?

Duties

A job that checks the condition of manufacturing equipment, isolates the causes of abnormalities, and supports maintenance, process control, and improvement. Job postings use various titles, such as equipment engineer, line engineer, and machine keeper.

May suit you if

May not suit you if

It may be a burden for people who want to avoid sudden callouts or shift work, or who do not get along with equipment noise or the cleanroom environment.

Summary: equipment engineers support the systems that keep semiconductor fabs from stopping

Equipment engineers start up, protect, fix, and improve semiconductor manufacturing equipment.
