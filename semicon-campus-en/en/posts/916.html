What was announced

Researchers from Kyoto University, the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, and NEC reported that the size and direction of "spin-orbit torque," which moves magnetization, change depending on the crystal orientation of CrSb thin films. The announcement by Kyoto University's Institute for Chemical Research and the first online publication of the paper in the journal Advanced Functional Materials both took place on September 10, 2026. This is the announcement and publication date, not the date the experiments were done.University announcement, The paper

The main point is that design depends not only on the material name but also on the orientation of the atomic arrangement and on the "interface," the boundary with the neighboring material. The authors mainly explain their measurement results by crystal and interface symmetry. This is a clue for material design toward MRAM, but it does not show a finished memory product or the start of mass production.The paper, University announcement

A simple way to think about MRAM and spin torque

MRAM is a nonvolatile memory that stores information using the state of magnetization. Nonvolatile means it keeps information even when the power is off. If you picture magnetization as the needle of a tiny compass, writing information means controlling which way that needle points. This research concerns how an electric current can move that direction.University announcement and glossary

"Spin torque" is the effect in which the spin angular momentum carried by electrons is transferred to a magnetic material and tries to change the direction of its magnetization. The "force" here is less about pushing an object to move it and more about turning its direction. So what matters is not only how strong the effect is but also which direction it tries to turn the magnetization. The spin-orbit torque in this study is one type of torque involved in this magnetization control.University announcement and glossary

Another term is "epitaxial growth." This is a way of growing a thin film so that it matches the crystal arrangement of the substrate. If you picture a lattice of regularly arranged atoms, the direction that lattice faces is the crystal orientation. By making films with aligned orientations, you can compare cases where the material is the same but the relationship between the atomic arrangement and the current differs.University announcement and glossary

What changed between the two crystal planes

The researchers compared the (1−100) and (11−20) planes of epitaxial CrSb. These numbers are labels showing which direction of crystal plane is used. For the measurements, they used "spin-torque ferromagnetic resonance," or ST-FMR. In this method, a high-frequency current is applied, and the resonance of the magnetization in the neighboring ferromagnetic layer is read as an electrical signal.The paper, Research explainer

The paper's abstract reports that an x-polarized damping-like torque component appeared, and that the sign of the y-polarized component reversed between the two crystal planes. x and y are coordinates for telling directions apart, and a sign reversal means that, using the same coordinates, the component acts in the opposite direction. The key point is that the difference observed was not just an increase or decrease in strength but a difference in torque direction.The paper

The authors explained this angular dependence with crystallographic torques allowed by a symmetry class called "C2v." Symmetry is the property of a structure looking the same after certain rotations or mirror reflections. Here, the rules of the atomic arrangement relate to which directions of torque can be produced. The university's materials cite, among other things, that no systematic change was seen when the film thickness was varied, as evidence for an interface origin. This is the authors' interpretation of the mechanism based on their measurements.The paper, Research explainer

Earlier research has used crystal orientation in design

The idea of controlling torque with crystal symmetry has a long research history. In a study of WTe2/ferromagnet stacks first submitted on May 9, 2016, an out-of-plane anti-damping torque was reported to appear or not appear depending on which crystal axis the current flowed along. It is an earlier example showing that the relationship between atomic arrangement and current affects not only torque strength but also which components can be produced.Earlier study by MacNeill et al.

With this background, the focus of the new work can be summed up as comparing different crystal planes in CrSb and separating out the mechanism that produces the observed response. However, WTe2 is a different material. The out-of-plane torque found in the earlier study cannot be treated as if it were also confirmed in CrSb here.Earlier study, The paper

"Because it is an altermagnet" is not enough of an explanation

CrSb is treated as an altermagnet, but the material's classification and the mechanism behind the torque actually observed need to be read separately. According to the university's explainer, in these samples no clear contribution was confirmed from the magnetic spin Hall effect or the spin splitter effect, which are specific to altermagnetism. The authors conclude that crystal and interface symmetry play the main role in this response.Research explainer, The paper

This conclusion is limited to these samples and measurements. It cannot be generalized to say that altermagnet-specific effects are small in every CrSb structure. Rather than assuming how a material behaves from its name, the way to read research that leads to applied design is to check which structure was used, what was measured, and which mechanism explains it.The paper, Research explainer

What it means for MRAM and what to watch next

The university says the highest damping-like torque efficiency reached a level comparable to heavy metals such as Pt and Ta. However, this is a torque efficiency comparison presented by the researchers. It cannot be directly converted into a power reduction rate for MRAM products, and it does not prove improvements in product write speed or endurance. The significance at this stage is that it provides grounds for treating crystal planes and interfaces as variables in material design.Research explainer, University announcement

The researchers plan to extend the work to other altermagnets and to control strain using piezoelectric substrates and element substitution. Strain refers to stretching, shrinking, or deforming the crystal lattice. These are future research plans, with no deadline given. The next thing to watch is how changing the structure changes the size, direction, and mechanism of the torque. Commercial adoption, mass production, and release timing cannot be confirmed from this announcement.Research explainer
