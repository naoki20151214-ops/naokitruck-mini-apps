What happened: announced September 7, applied from September 8

On September 7, 2026, China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) published its preliminary ruling in an anti-dumping investigation into imports of dichlorosilane (DCS) originating in Japan. The notice requires Chinese importers to provide cash deposits starting the next day, September 8. This change affects the trading terms for a material used in semiconductor manufacturing, but it is classified as a provisional measure in the form of cash deposits. It is not an announcement that a definitive anti-dumping duty rate has been set by a final ruling. Source: MOFCOM Notice No. 37

The company-specific deposit rates are 99.2% for Shin-Etsu Chemical, 80.8% for Denal Silane, and 99.2% for other Japanese companies. The party that provides the deposit to Chinese customs is the importer; it is not a system in which Japanese manufacturers directly pay a penalty at the same rate. Also, September 8 is the start date stated in the notice; actual payments have not been confirmed. Source: MOFCOM Notice No. 37

What is dichlorosilane? A "film material" for making chips

Dichlorosilane is a silicon compound with the molecular formula SiH2Cl2. It is not a finished semiconductor chip but a material used in the manufacturing process. MOFCOM describes its main use as thin-film deposition in semiconductor manufacturing, and Shin-Etsu Chemical's product page also classifies DCS under semiconductor wafer applications. These two sources confirm that the measure affects trade in a semiconductor manufacturing material. Source: MOFCOM notice, Shin-Etsu Chemical's product description

Thin-film deposition means forming a thin layer of material on the surface of a wafer or similar. It helps to think of it as the process of building a film with the needed properties on top of the base layer. The MOFCOM notice lists silicon nitride films, silicon oxide films, polycrystalline silicon films, epitaxial films, and others as uses of DCS. DCS is positioned as a raw material fed in to make such films. Source: MOFCOM notice

Another term is insulating film. This refers to a film that does not conduct electricity well. The key point is that it is not just a surface coating but a layer with an electrical role. Shin-Etsu Chemical explains that its high-purity silane gases are used for insulating films, epitaxial films, and more. However, this explanation covers the whole product group, and it cannot be read as uses of DCS alone or as adoption by specific customers. Source: Shin-Etsu Chemical's product description

What does 99.2% represent? It is not a price increase rate

The deposit formula given in the notice is customs value set by customs × company-specific deposit rate × (1 + import-stage VAT rate). The 99.2% is a rate used in this calculation; it does not show that import prices or semiconductor manufacturing costs rise by 99.2%. Because VAT is also included in the calculation, the actual deposit amount is not determined by the company rate alone. Source: MOFCOM Notice No. 37

To understand the mechanism, suppose the customs value is 100. For goods subject to 99.2%, the deposit would be 99.2 multiplied by "1 + the import-stage VAT rate." This is an example calculation using the notice's formula, not an actual transaction price. What we can tell is that the measure increases the funds needed when importing the covered product. How much of that burden is passed on to selling prices cannot be known from this notice alone. Basis for calculation and interpretation: MOFCOM notice

Background: an investigation launched in January, and how to read the authorities' provisional findings

Tracing the history, the investigation began on January 7, 2026. MOFCOM says it started the investigation in response to an application from China's domestic industry. It then published the preliminary ruling on September 7 and set September 8 as the start date for the provisional measures. You need to keep the launch of the investigation, the provisional finding, and the application of measures separate. Source: MOFCOM's official Q&A, MOFCOM notice

MOFCOM says it provisionally found dumping, material injury to China's domestic industry, and a causal link between the two. This is a judgment by Chinese authorities, and it should not be taken to mean that the calculation of company rates or the injury analysis has been independently verified. The ministry says it will continue the investigation and issue a final ruling based on the results. The final ruling is a future step. Source: MOFCOM's official Q&A

Limited scope: not all Japanese semiconductor materials

The measure covers DCS originating in Japan, and the product description in the notice includes a purity condition of over 99%. It clearly states that other products under the same tariff code, 28539090, are excluded. So you cannot generalize that 99.2% applies to all semiconductor materials imported from Japan. The country of origin and the product scope need to be checked together. Source: MOFCOM Notice No. 37

For customs procedures, General Administration of Customs Notice No. 133 requires that DCS be declared under commodity code 28539090.61 from September 8. What we can confirm is an official repost on the Hunan Provincial Government website; the repost was published on September 8, and the document itself is dated September 7. This is additional information about the declaration code, not a correction to the company-specific deposit rates. Source: Hunan Provincial Government repost of the customs notice

Why it matters, and what to check next

For the semiconductor industry, the significance is that the financial terms at import change for a material used in manufacturing. On the other hand, the deposit rate alone cannot be linked to supply disruptions, fab shutdowns, or higher prices for finished chips. The deposit obligation set by this notice and any actual effects on shipments, prices, or production need to be viewed separately. Basis for interpretation: MOFCOM notice

As background on material supply, Shin-Etsu Chemical cites its purification technology, quality control, and container delivery management as strengths. However, this is a company product description, not evidence of performance differences from competitors, the time needed for alternative sourcing, or supply shortages on the Chinese side. Judging whether suppliers can be switched requires separate, specific information. Source: Shin-Etsu Chemical's product description

The points to watch next are the final ruling after the continued investigation and how it is reflected in the covered transactions. Measuring the impact on companies will also require official statements from manufacturers and importers, plus information on trade volumes, price pass-through, inventory, and alternative supply. At this stage, the key to understanding this news is not to confuse the start of provisional measures with unconfirmed business impacts. Source on next steps: MOFCOM's official Q&A
