On October 12, 2026, Lumentum, Astera Labs, and Corning announced a joint demonstration of "PCIe over Optics," which carries PCIe signals over light inside the racks that hold AI servers. The announced exhibition period was October 12 to 15, and the demo was planned for Astera Labs' booth B34. The key point is that the three companies announced an optical interconnect configuration and a demonstration; it does not mean a volume-production product has launched or that it has already been widely deployed. Lumentum's announcement

What was announced?

The announced system combines Astera Labs' Scorpio Smart Fabric Switch, Lumentum's 1060nm VCSEL-based optical interconnect, and Corning's multimode fiber. A VCSEL is a semiconductor laser that converts electrical signals into laser light and sends it. On the other end, a photodetector receives the light and turns it back into an electrical signal. In other words, the idea is to keep the PCIe communication standard used by CPUs, AI accelerators, and so on, while replacing the transmission medium in between with optical fiber instead of electrical cable. Configuration described in the joint announcement

According to the three companies, the design uses multiple 32Gb/s NRZ optical channels to show an error-free PCIe 5 link. Lumentum says it places VCSELs and photodetectors in dense two-dimensional arrays and uses advanced packaging that includes backside lens technology. However, the announcement alone does not let us confirm the actual total number of optical channels, link distance, test duration, or error criteria. "Error-free" is a description of the demo given in the joint announcement; it does not mean an independent organization has reproduced the same result. Lumentum's announcement

Basics of PCIe 5.0 and optical interconnects

PCIe is a high-speed I/O standard that connects CPUs, GPUs and AI accelerators, storage, and more. According to PCI-SIG, PCIe 5.0 supports 32.0GT/s per lane, described as 128GB/s of effective raw bandwidth in each direction with 32 lanes. The PCIe 5.0 specification was released on May 22, 2019. With each new generation, more data moves in the same amount of time, so signals are more likely to weaken on cables and boards or be affected by reflections and noise. PCI-SIG official FAQ

A "lane" here is the smallest unit for sending and receiving data in PCIe. Bundling multiple lanes creates wider connections such as x16 or x32. "NRZ," meanwhile, is a modulation method in which each signal change represents one of two values, 0 or 1. This announcement showed a configuration that uses multiple 32Gb/s NRZ optical channels in parallel. Picture building a high-bandwidth connection by splitting it across several optical channels instead of carrying all the bandwidth on just one. Announcement on optical channels

Why is optics drawing attention in AI racks?

AI processing connects many accelerators, memory, and switches at high speed. As racks grow larger, connection distance, number of cables, signal quality, power, and thermal design can all become problems at once. Longer electrical wiring requires measures against signal loss and noise, and more cables make cabling work and cooling design harder. Because optical fiber does not send the electrical signal itself through a long conductor, it can serve as an alternative connection in these places. The significance of this demo is that it showed an implementation that switches the medium inside the rack to optics without greatly changing the PCIe protocol. Lumentum's description of the challenges

However, switching to optics does not always reduce power consumption or heat. Transmitters, receivers, switches, packages, and power circuits are needed, and there are more parts for optical conversion. This announcement does not give a power comparison with copper configurations, a latency comparison, the amount of heat reduction for the whole rack, prices, or timing of volume production. So we cannot go as far as saying "it has been proven to outperform copper and use less power."

What to check on the fiber side

Corning describes multimode fiber as being for short-distance, high-data-rate transmission inside data centers, and presents OM2, OM3, OM4, and others as suited to 850nm VCSEL-based uses of up to about 100m. Multimode means transmitting light along multiple paths inside the fiber. It is easy to use for short connections inside a facility, but wavelength, fiber standard, loss, connectors, and bending conditions must be evaluated together. This Corning material is mainly general background on 850nm configurations, and it does not directly prove the reach or loss of this 1060nm configuration. Corning's description of multimode fiber

How should we evaluate this announcement?

The Scorpio Smart Fabric Switch is described on Astera Labs' official product page as a fabric switch for AI scale-up, and a 64-lane configuration supporting PCIe 5 is also listed. This demo showed a direction of building optical interconnects into connection systems that include switches like this. It is one example showing that optical interconnects are no longer just lab components but something to build into PCIe-based rack designs. However, announcing a demo and completing a volume-production system that can run long term are different stages. Astera Labs' Scorpio product page

Also, on October 8, Astera Labs announced its Aries 7 signal conditioning product family for PCIe 7, including copper and optical interconnects. This is roadmap material showing that the company has optical interconnects in view for future generations, but it is not a performance result of this PCIe 5 demo. Sampling and availability plans also need to be treated as company announcements and cannot be confused with a confirmed start date for volume production. Astera Labs' Aries 7 announcement

Points to watch going forward

  • Whether the actual link distance, number of optical channels, test duration, and bit error rate for the 1060nm configuration are published.
  • Whether measured values for power consumption, latency, heat, and cable density compared with copper configurations are shown.
  • Whether standards compliance and interoperability of the 1060nm VCSEL, the fiber used, and connectors are confirmed.
  • Whether it moves from an exhibition demo to volume-production products, pricing, maintenance methods, and standard adoption in AI servers.

This news shows a concrete direction for tackling connection challenges in AI racks by bringing in optics while keeping PCIe. On the other hand, what we can confirm only goes as far as the demo configuration and design intent announced by the companies. To judge commercialization, performance advantages, cost savings, or heat reduction for the whole rack, we need to wait for independent measurements and operating data after volume production.
