102.4Tbps and CPO explained for beginners

Tbps is a unit of communication speed that shows how many terabits of data can be handled per second. 102.4Tbps represents the ability to process a total of 102.4 terabits per second of traffic inside the switch. However, it does not mean every server in the network can always communicate at that speed. Actual performance also depends on optical modules, cables, NICs, software, traffic patterns, and whether there is congestion.

CPO (Co-Packaged Optics) is a packaging approach that places the switch ASIC and the optical engines, which convert electrical signals into optical signals, close together in the same package. With conventional pluggable optical modules, electrical signals travel across the board from the switch to the module. The higher the speed, the more this electrical signal path and the burden of signal conditioning become a problem. CPO aims to improve power and signal stability by shortening the electrical distance and switching to optical connections sooner.

In a product announcement on October 8, 2025, Broadcom described TH6-Davisson as its third-generation CPO Ethernet switch. The published specs are 102.4Tbps of switching capacity, 200Gbps links, and sixteen 6.4Tbps Davisson optical engines. Broadcom also claims that, using optical engines and multi-chip packaging based on TSMC's COUPE technology, it can cut optical interconnect power by 70% compared with conventional pluggable approaches. This figure comes from Broadcom's product description, and the materials we checked do not show independent verification results. Broadcom's TH6-Davisson announcement

Why does the network matter in the AI era?

In AI training, many GPUs and AI accelerators frequently exchange data and computation results. Even if the compute chips are fast, slow communication between chips increases waiting time and lowers overall processing efficiency. That is why, in AI data centers, network bandwidth, latency, congestion control, link stability, and power consumption matter, not just computing performance.

Scale-up is the idea of densely connecting accelerators within the same server or rack, or close to each other. Scale-out, on the other hand, refers to a configuration that extends connections to multiple servers and racks so more nodes can share the processing. Broadcom is also promoting a vision of expanding AI networks in stages, including scale-across, which extends across an entire giant cluster.

An Ethernet switch is a device that forwards data in packets to their destinations. A NIC (Network Interface Card) connects a server to the network and handles part of the data transfer. Thor Ultra, an 800G AI Ethernet NIC, lists features such as compliance with the UEC specification, PCIe Gen6 x16, 100G and 200G PAM4 SerDes, per-packet multipath, out-of-order delivery, selective retransmission, and congestion control. Products in volume production and products not yet confirmed

What to note here is that "the Tomahawk 6 family is shipping in volume" and "the TH6-Davisson CPO version is shipping in volume" are not the same information. On March 12, 2026, Broadcom announced that the standard Tomahawk 6 family, with 102.4Tbps, 100G and 200G SerDes, load balancing, congestion management, and more, is shipping in volume for AI scale-up and scale-out uses. This is a company announcement about shipments of the standard family. Broadcom's volume shipment announcement

Meanwhile, materials from October 2025 said TH6-Davisson samples were being provided to early-access customers and partners. From the materials we could check as of October 11, 2026, we cannot determine when the CPO version will ship in volume, shipment volumes, or production deployment by customers. Exhibition, sample supply, volume shipment, and production operation are each different stages.

What this announcement means and its limits

The big trend we can read from this announcement is an effort to tackle communication bottlenecks in AI data centers by optimizing not just switch ASICs but optical interconnects, NICs, PCIe, racks, and network control together. The larger AI clusters get, the more it becomes necessary to manage power and signal quality while keeping many links running stably, which is why packaging technologies like CPO are drawing attention.

However, the 102.4Tbps capacity and 70% power reduction cannot be translated directly into the speed of actual AI training jobs or the power consumption of an entire data center without checking the comparison conditions and measurement environment. The official materials we checked do not show independent verification of the CPO version's production scale, production deployment by customers, power or latency in real operation, or long-term link stability. So for now, it is appropriate to evaluate these as Broadcom's announced figures and exhibition plans.

What to watch going forward

The first thing to check is whether the live demos and keynote at OCP Global Summit took place as planned. Next, watch whether the start of volume production for TH6-Davisson CPO, actual customer adoption, power and link stability in operation, and differences in use from the standard Tomahawk 6 are shown. For 800G NICs such as Thor Ultra, it also matters whether they move from sampling to volume production and what communication efficiency they achieve in AI clusters.

Broadcom's announcement shows that AI Ethernet is no longer just a race for "fast switches" but a system race that includes how optical and electrical connections are made, server-side NICs, congestion control, and rack configuration. Going forward, we need to evaluate not only the maximum values the company promotes but also independent measurements, customer production cases, and production volumes together.
