China's CXMT Starts Mass Production of LPDDR6, Set for Xiaomi's New Phone: Is Chinese DRAM Closing In on the Global Leaders?

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), a Chinese DRAM maker, has started mass production of "LPDDR6," the latest generation of low-power memory.

There are also reports that this LPDDR6 will be used in Xiaomi's next-generation smartphone.

This is not just news about "a new smartphone part."

It is important news that suggests China's DRAM industry may be narrowing the technology gap with global leaders such as Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, and Micron.

When reading this news, it helps to think about "the LPDDR6 standard," "DRAM as a memory product," and "Xiaomi's smartphone" separately. LPDDR6 is not the processor that does the computing. It is a memory-side technology that temporarily holds the data a processor works on inside a mobile device, and reads and writes it when needed.

That is why a smartphone's performance is not decided by memory alone. The processor design, the connection to memory, the software, and the balance with heat and battery all combine to create the real user experience. The focus this time is that CXMT is trying to move into mass production and customer adoption of the latest generation in the memory part of that picture.

What happened?

CXMT has started mass production of LPDDR6 and is moving forward with supply to Xiaomi. A Reuters report, based on CXMT's announcement and confirmation from Xiaomi, says LPDDR6 will be supplied for Xiaomi's new foldable smartphone.

CXMT has so far offered DRAM such as LPDDR5X, and it has now moved on to the next generation, LPDDR6. CXMT's official product page also confirms that the company offers DRAM products including LPDDR5/5X.

What matters is that this is not just a research announcement. It has moved to the stage of "mass production."

In semiconductors, there is a big gap between announcing a new technology and producing it in volume for use in real products.

This news means CXMT has moved into the phase of bringing its latest-generation DRAM to the real market.

However, the start of mass production here does not mean it will be built into every smartphone at once. Which products it goes into, in what quantity, and for how long depend on the customer's design and qualification and on CXMT's production plans. We cannot state the supply scale or market share beyond what can be confirmed from the published news.

What's so great about LPDDR6?

LPDDR is low-power DRAM used in smartphones and other mobile devices. DRAM is memory that temporarily holds data while a device is running, and LPDDR is a standard within it for mobile devices that emphasizes things like low-voltage operation. SK hynix's official explanation also describes LPDDR as low-power DRAM for smartphones and tablets.

As more AI processing happens on smartphones, these become more important:

  • Transferring more data at high speed
  • Keeping power consumption down
  • Keeping heat down

All of these matter.

The LPDDR6 generation aims to achieve both higher data transfer performance and better power efficiency than previous generations.

In a smartphone, the faster data moves from memory to the processor, the more you can sometimes cut waiting time. On the other hand, just raising speed leaves battery and heat problems unsolved. That is why each new LPDDR generation is judged on both moving data quickly and saving power by activating only the paths that are needed. LPDDR6 is positioned as a next-generation standard aiming to balance performance and power for mobile devices.

For example, SK hynix is also developing LPDDR6 and is working on higher speed and better power efficiency compared with LPDDR5X. SK hynix's official announcement describes the performance and power efficiency of its 1c LPDDR6 compared with LPDDR5X.

However, these are performance figures for SK hynix's products. They do not mean CXMT's LPDDR6 has the same performance.

The key point is that CXMT has not just announced a new product; it is moving toward adoption by a major smartphone maker.

Xiaomi is one of the world's leading smartphone makers.

Xiaomi is also developing its own processors. A Reuters report shares information about Xiaomi's Xring O3 and its manufacturing by TSMC.

What does this mean for Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron?

It is too early to think that this start of mass production alone will quickly change the global DRAM landscape.

DRAM competitiveness is decided by a combination of factors such as

  • Performance
  • Power consumption
  • Yield
  • Reliability
  • Production capacity
  • Cost
  • Customer qualification

and more.

However, if CXMT achieves stable mass production of the latest-generation LPDDR and builds a track record with major customers, competitive pressure will increase in the DRAM market, where Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron are strong.

Another important question is whether CXMT can expand beyond smartphones into servers and automobiles. Reuters also reported that CXMT has shipped LPDDR6 samples to customers for mobile devices, servers, and smart cars.

If it gets that far, this could affect the entire global DRAM market, not just memory for Chinese smartphones.

On the other hand, if adoption stays limited to one device and does not expand to stable supply and customer qualification, we cannot say the global market landscape has changed. Rather than deciding winners and losers from headlines alone, it is important to check, one by one, whether mass production continues, whether it spreads to other customers, and whether reliability is confirmed in real products.

Common misconceptions

Does starting LPDDR6 mass production alone mean CXMT is as competitive as the world's biggest makers?

Not necessarily. It has to be judged including the stability of mass production, yield, reliability, supply capacity, cost, and customer qualification.

Can SK hynix's LPDDR6 performance figures be applied to CXMT too?

No. SK hynix's announcement describes its own products. It does not show the performance of CXMT's LPDDR6.

Does this news mean Chinese DRAM has taken over the global market?

We cannot say that yet. What can be confirmed this time is the start of mass production and moves to supply Xiaomi. We need to follow future adoption by other companies, expansion into servers and automobiles, and competitive conditions.
