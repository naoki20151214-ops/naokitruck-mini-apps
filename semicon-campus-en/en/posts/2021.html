On October 12, 2026, Navitas Semiconductor announced a 24kW power board for AI data centers, developed in collaboration with NVIDIA. The announcement describes a design that converts 800VDC to 12VDC in a single stage, eliminating the traditional 50V intermediate bus. As rising power in AI servers makes power wiring and cooling a challenge, the news here is that 800VDC AI data center power has been shown as a concrete board. However, this is an announcement and a future exhibition plan, and it does not mean mass production or shipments have begun. Navitas announcement

What was announced

The board Navitas showed is a DC-DC power supply that takes 800VDC as input and outputs 12VDC. The announcement describes a configuration that can deliver 24kW, a 400mm × 60mm form factor, and placement close to the xPU on the compute board. The main power stage uses sixteen 650V, 18mΩ NV6034 GaNFast FETs, with a stacked half-bridge and dual-side-cooled DFN 8×8 packages. The 24kW here is the delivery capability announced this time. It does not mean the same as a guaranteed rating for a mass-produced product or a specification already verified long-term in customer environments. Specifications in the announcement

The performance Navitas cites is a target efficiency of 97% at full load and a target power density of over 2,000W/in³. These are target values announced by the company. From these materials alone, they cannot be called guaranteed values for mass-produced products, measurements from real operation, or numbers independently verified by a third party. Measurement conditions such as input voltage, temperature, load changes, and cooling method also need to be checked in the future.

Why is 800VDC getting attention?

For the same power, a higher voltage means a lower current. Losses from wire resistance are roughly proportional to the square of the current, so distributing power at high voltage makes it easier to reduce wiring losses and heat. It may also reduce the amount of copper needed in conductors and the burden on busbars and cables.

NVIDIA explains that with traditional 54V distribution, high current, copper use, cabling, and power density tend to become constraints as AI computers use more power, and it presents 800VDC as the power distribution architecture for next-generation AI factories. 800VDC is the voltage used to distribute power from the facility to racks and compute nodes. It does not mean the GPU's internal circuits run directly at 800V. NVIDIA's technical description of 800VDC

What does "single-stage conversion" cover?

Single-stage conversion here means handling the conversion from the board's 800VDC input to its 12VDC output in one conversion stage. In traditional setups, one approach was to place an intermediate bus converter (IBC) that first lowers 800VDC to around 54V or 50V and then converts that to 12V. This design moves toward eliminating that 50V intermediate bus. In its 2025 explanation, Navitas also described a power path that converts 800VDC to 54V or 12V and then to the voltage the GPU needs. Navitas announcement from October 2025

However, 12V is not the xPU's core voltage. After the 12V stage, there are voltage regulators called VRMs (Voltage Regulator Modules) that convert it to the even lower voltages the processor needs. So it is wrong to understand this as "powering the GPU core directly from 800V in a single step."

Reducing intermediate stages and placing the power supply near the xPU may reduce the number of converters, wiring distance, and board space used. On the other hand, how much overall efficiency and reliability improve is not decided by GaN FETs alone. A system-level evaluation is needed, including magnetic components, insulation, control circuits, protection circuits, VRMs, wiring, thermal design, and real load conditions.

Why GaN is used, and the technical background

GaN (gallium nitride) is a semiconductor material well suited to high-speed operation as a power supply switch. Faster switching makes it easier to shrink the magnetic components used in power circuits, which may improve power density by packing more power into a limited volume. The use of GaNFast FETs on this board likely aims to make the 800V-class conversion circuit small and dense.

However, high-speed switching brings challenges such as electromagnetic noise, insulation, parasitic inductance, heat, and control stability. Even if high power density is achieved, the space-saving effect for the whole data center is limited if the system, including cooling equipment, becomes larger. Even at a target efficiency of 97%, delivering 24kW means the remaining losses turn into heat, so cooling design is important.

Don't confuse an announcement with mass production

Navitas says it plans to show this board in the NVIDIA AI Factory MGX Wall partner exhibit area at OCP Global Summit 2026. This is a future exhibition plan. As of October 14, 2026, no mass-production start date, price, shipment volume, general availability date, or confirmed mass-production adoption in NVIDIA MGX has been announced. Being exhibited and being operated long-term in a data center are also separate facts. Announcement on the exhibition plan

This announcement builds on NVIDIA's 800VDC vision and the collaboration Navitas described in 2025. The May 2025 announcement explained the current and copper constraints of 54V distribution and the plan to move to 800V HVDC. This 24kW power board can be seen as taking that vision a step further toward a concrete power implementation, but we cannot infer from previous reports that NVIDIA's mass-production adoption is confirmed. Points to check next

  • Whether it is actually exhibited at OCP Global Summit 2026.
  • Under what input voltage, temperature, load, and cooling conditions the 97% efficiency and over-2,000W/in³ figures were measured.
  • Whether the timing, price, and supply volume for moving from exhibition prototype to mass-production specification are disclosed.
  • Whether effective efficiency including VRMs and the whole rack, thermal design, and long-term reliability are confirmed by third parties or in customer environments.
  • Whether safety standards, insulation, and maintenance procedures for handling 800VDC can be applied in data centers.

As of October 14, 2026, no new primary information correcting or withdrawing the main figures in this announcement has been found. However, the specifications and plans may be updated through future exhibitions, technical documents, and customer adoption announcements. This news is one example showing that competition over AI chip performance is spreading beyond the chips themselves to the infrastructure that delivers power safely and densely.
