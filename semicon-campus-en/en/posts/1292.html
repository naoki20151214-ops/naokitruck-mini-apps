On September 24–25, 2026, the "ASEAN Semiconductor Forum 2026" was held in Pattaya, Thailand. According to official announcements from Thailand's National Science and Technology Development Agency (NSTDA) and others, participants included ASEAN member states, the ASEAN Secretariat, governments, universities, research institutes, industry, and stakeholders from Japan, China, the United States, the Netherlands, and elsewhere, who discussed regional cooperation in semiconductors. The news release was published on September 24, but the meeting itself ran for two days, September 24–25. NSTDA official announcement

What does this news mean?

The theme stated in the official announcement was building a "future-ready ASEAN semiconductor ecosystem" through science, technology, and innovation cooperation and global partnerships. Here, "ecosystem" does not mean only factories. It refers to the whole network of companies, universities, research institutes, people, and systems involved in designing semiconductors, preparing materials and equipment, manufacturing wafers, packaging and testing, and building chips into final products.

Semiconductors are not a product where a single country needs to have every process at the same scale. If one country is strong in design and research and another is strong in assembly, packaging, and testing, linking them with reliable logistics, quality standards, and talent exchange can create one supply chain across the region. This forum can be read as pointing toward connecting different strengths, rather than having each ASEAN country build duplicate facilities. It is significant as a policy discussion, but it is not an announcement that a new fab has been completed or that the supply chain has improved.

Four pillars of cooperation

The NSTDA announcement suggests four main areas of cooperation. All of them were presented as directions or proposals. No agreement with a set budget, implementing body, start date, or numerical targets was announced. Official explanation of the areas of cooperation

  • Common skills and talent development: A plan to align skills and competency frameworks and promote training, internships, researcher exchanges, and student mobility.
  • Connecting research infrastructure: The idea of sharing each country's research centers, institutes, and lab facilities, leading to joint research and researcher exchanges.
  • Regional value chain: Linking each country's different industrial strengths so that design, manufacturing, back-end processes, and more complement each other within the region.
  • Technology partnerships with the world: Advancing talent, research, shared facilities, technology transfer, and university-industry collaboration with partners outside the region too.

An important term here is value chain. It means the flow in which raw materials and design information pass through multiple processes until they become a final semiconductor product. For example, circuit design, wafer manufacturing, packaging, and electrical testing each need different equipment and specialized skills. Even if one process is strong, products cannot ship unless it connects with the other processes.

Technical terms beginners should know

Wafer manufacturing is the process of forming fine circuits on a substrate such as silicon to make many semiconductor chips. It involves a long series of steps, such as lithography to draw circuits, deposition to add thin films, etching to remove unwanted parts, and inspection to check for particles and dimensions. In advanced manufacturing, equipment, materials, process conditions, and quality control must be run as one system.

Packaging is the process of connecting finished chips to external terminals and substrates, protecting them, and putting them in a form that can efficiently take in and send out power and signals. There are also technologies that combine multiple chips in one package, and in AI computers this has become an important part that affects performance and power consumption. The "advanced packaging" mentioned by the Thai side in the official announcement is a policy area aiming to upgrade these back-end processes. However, announcing a policy is different from a mass-production line starting operation.

Another key term is research infrastructure. This is the foundation for research, including not only research buildings but also measurement equipment, prototyping facilities, data, specialist engineers, and usage rules. If expensive equipment can be shared rather than each country owning one, universities and companies get more chances to prototype and evaluate. On the other hand, unless booking, costs, intellectual property, export controls, and handling of confidential information are standardized, the problem remains that facilities exist but are hard to use in practice.

Why regional cooperation is needed in the AI era

Semiconductors for AI and data centers involve many technologies beyond computing performance, such as power efficiency, high-speed memory connections, heat dissipation, and reliability. It is hard for a single company or country to complete design, manufacturing, packaging, and testing alone, so researchers, facilities, component companies, and educational institutions need to be combined widely. Against the backdrop of demand in the AI era, this cooperation plan is an attempt to strengthen ASEAN not as a group of separate countries, but as a region where processes are connected.

However, this is a policy implication that can be drawn from the meeting. The official announcement alone does not confirm how much investment will increase, how much exports will grow, or how much resilience to supply disruptions will improve through cooperation. The figures in the announcement on market size and ASEAN's export share also lack a stated calculation method or source on the same page, so they should not be treated as definitive without independent verification.

Historical background: policies linking research and industry

In Singapore, research and innovation policy has been built up since the founding of the National Science and Technology Board in 1991, and in 2010 "Innovation and Enterprise," which links research results to business and industry, was also built into the policy. The current RIE2030 covers 2026 to 2030 and is a framework for turning research, talent, and capabilities into real use in the economy and society. Explanation by Singapore's National Research Foundation

This experience is useful for understanding semiconductor cooperation. Simply handing out research funding does not produce technologies or products that companies can use. University research, company development, prototyping facilities, student training, and on-site internships need to be connected over a long period. Singapore's Economic Development Board also describes semiconductors as an industry made up of a chain including design, wafer manufacturing, packaging, and testing, and lists joint training and internships by universities, companies, and the government as elements of talent development. Look at plans and results separately

What we can confirm is that the forum was held and that the direction of cooperation and the areas discussed were officially presented. The Thai side also explained a plan to integrate policy, investment, technology, talent, and the industrial ecosystem through its Semiconductor Board, expanding into semiconductor design, advanced packaging, photonics, power devices, research, and highly skilled talent. However, this is the Thai side's policy direction and plan. It is not proof that the facilities have been completed, mass production has started, or goals have been achieved. Official announcement on Thailand's policy direction

Points to check going forward are: first, whether intergovernmental agreements or memorandums are signed; second, whether budgets and implementing bodies for joint research are made clear; third, who can use the research facilities and under what conditions; and fourth, whether common skills certification and student and researcher mobility programs actually get underway. It is also important whether company projects moving from prototyping to mass production, investment by process, and products or patents from joint research appear. Only once these are confirmed can we judge that this "cooperation design" has moved toward a real semiconductor ecosystem.

Summary

This news about ASEAN semiconductor cooperation does not report the completion of a new fab. It is a policy discussion about connecting talent, research facilities, technology transfer, and industrial processes inside and outside the region. Given the division of labor in semiconductors, common skills, shared use of research infrastructure, and exchange between companies and universities are an important foundation. On the other hand, signed agreements, budgets, specific shared facilities, selected projects, and mass-production results have not been confirmed. Whether the regional supply chain for the AI era really becomes stronger will become clear by following how the plans are institutionalized and carried out after the forum.
