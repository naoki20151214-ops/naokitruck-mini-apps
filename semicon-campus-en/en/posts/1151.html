On September 21, 2026, OMNIVISION announced the "OAX7700," an AI companion ASIC for always-on monitoring using a vehicle's exterior cameras. It is designed to watch the surroundings even while the car is parked and to fully wake the vehicle's central computer only when it detects an event involving a person, object, or the like. Samples are already available, but volume production is planned for the first quarter of 2027; this is not news that volume production has started or that it has been installed in any vehicle model. OMNIVISION's announcement

What was announced?

The OAX7700 is a dedicated chip that processes video from automotive exterior cameras close to the camera. The official description lists AI tasks such as detecting human presence, detecting people and objects, and measuring distance. During always-on monitoring it runs at low power, and when an event occurs it switches to normal mode and records 5 to 10 seconds of "pre-roll" video so the vehicle's ECU can verify the event.

Pre-roll is video saved from just before a detection happens. If you only record after detection, you may not know what happened right before someone approached or a collision occurred. Keeping video from before and after makes it easier to see how a person or object approached. However, the actual recording time and storage method also depend on the vehicle's design.

The product page lists a 60-pin BGA package with stacked PSRAM and flash, up to 720p, and an operating temperature of -40 to +125°C. The package measures 5.5mm × 3.5mm. OMNIVISION says customers can run their own AI algorithms on the NPU and that it supports OTA updates. OAX7700 product page

Why does lower power matter?

If a parked vehicle constantly sends camera video to a high-performance central ECU and keeps running AI inference and recording there, the processor, memory, video links, and storage all run continuously. In cars that use an auxiliary battery, power consumption affects how long the car can stay parked, and even in electric vehicles it is important to keep the whole vehicle's standby power low.

This is where the idea of "edge AI" comes in. Edge AI analyzes data on a chip placed near the camera before sending it to a distant server or a large central processor. If a low-power chip like the OAX7700 identifies people and objects first, the central ECU may not need to stay on during times when nothing is happening. Lattice Semiconductor's automotive monitoring material also describes a design that checks events at the edge and wakes the central processor only when a threat is confirmed. This is general technical background; the numbers and configuration in that material do not prove the OAX7700's performance. Lattice solution brief

How to read the "97% lower power" figure

OMNIVISION claims that parking monitoring systems using the OAX7700 use 97% less power than existing market solutions. It is a striking number, but for now it should be read as a comparison claim by the company.

The official announcement does not let us confirm the product it was compared with, the number of cameras, the resolution or frame rate, the ratio of time spent in standby versus event states, whether the central ECU and sensors are included, or how it was measured. So this 97% cannot be treated as a reduction in the whole vehicle's power consumption, an extension of battery life, or a number that applies to every parking monitoring system. The absolute power consumption, AI model accuracy, and false-detection rate are also not clear from the official announcement alone.

Connection to SDVs and technical background

Another key point of the OAX7700 is that, although it is a dedicated chip, it is designed for customers' own AI algorithms and OTA updates. OTA stands for "Over the Air," a way to update software over a network connection. Detection rules and supported features may be updated even after the vehicle is purchased. However, the specific update procedure, security authentication, and what can be updated have not been disclosed.

This direction ties into the idea of software-defined vehicles, or SDVs. In SDVs, vehicle functions are expanded not only through fixed mechanical parts but also through updates to software and in-vehicle computers. NXP also lists edge AI, NPUs, low-power modes, and OTA updates as background for SDVs. However, this is not an announcement that the OAX7700 has been adopted in a particular SDV platform or production vehicle. NXP S32N7 product page

Announced plans and what is still unknown

Separating the facts and plans we can confirm this time gives the following:

  • Fact: The OAX7700 was officially announced on September 21, 2026.
  • Fact: The official materials list an NPU, stacked PSRAM and flash, up to 720p, 5 to 10 seconds of pre-roll, and more.
  • Fact: Samples are said to be available.
  • Company claim: 97% lower power than existing solutions.
  • Company plan: Volume production planned for the first quarter of 2027.
  • Unconfirmed: The start date of volume production, customers adopting it, installation in production vehicles, automotive certification, and the actual effect on the battery.

In particular, the date of January 1, 2027 in the input data is an estimate based on the start of the first quarter, not a confirmed start date for volume production. Also note that planned volume production does not mean manufacturing results or the start of sales.

Points to watch going forward

Going forward, we would like to confirm, first, the specific start date of volume production and the vehicle models adopting it; second, absolute power consumption and measurement conditions; third, the accuracy of person and object detection and the false-detection rate; and fourth, automotive certification and security measures for OTA updates. In real vehicle conditions, handling night, rain, backlight, snow, dirty lenses, and similar situations also matters.

The OAX7700 is a product that shows the direction of automotive edge AI: instead of constantly sending camera video to the central ECU, it makes AI decisions near the camera and wakes the vehicle's large computing resources only when needed. It may lower power consumption, but it is important not to confuse the 97% figure or the production plan with independent measurements or real-world installations.
