On September 22, 2026, Qualcomm Technologies announced the "Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6" and the "Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6" for premium smartphones. For the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6, the main focus of this article, Qualcomm says it combines a 2nm process, its custom-designed Oryon CPU, an Adreno GPU, and a Hexagon NPU. The announcement presents plans and specifications for new products. It does not mean smartphones with the chip are already finished and on sale in stores. Qualcomm's announcement

What was announced?

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 is an SoC (System on a Chip), the core of a smartphone. An SoC brings together not only the CPU but also several functions that run the device, such as image processing, graphics, AI inference, and communications, on one semiconductor platform. The CPU mainly handles general computing for the OS and apps, the GPU handles games and display, and the NPU handles AI computing.

Qualcomm positions both platforms for next-generation premium devices that focus on AI, gaming, cameras, and connectivity. They are expected to appear in devices from HONOR, Motorola, OnePlus, OPPO, vivo, Xiaomi, and others. However, the announcement we can confirm does not settle each model's release date, price, or regional specifications. In other words, what we can confirm now is that "compatible products are expected to appear," not that specific products are finished or have sales records. Announced device makers and product plans

What does "2nm" mean?

2nm does not mean that every dimension of the transistor is literally 2nm. It is a process node name that semiconductor makers use to indicate a generation of manufacturing technology. In general, as generations advance, designs aim to fit more transistors in the same area or improve the balance between performance and power use. However, you cannot directly calculate actual battery life or processing speed from the node name alone.

As background on manufacturing technology, TSMC describes N2 as its first-generation nanosheet transistor technology and says it began mass production in the fourth quarter of 2025. A nanosheet transistor is a structure in which the gate wraps more fully around the channel where current flows. By making current easier to control precisely, it aims to improve performance, power consumption, and transistor density. TSMC also describes plans to expand the N2 platform to AI, HPC (high-performance computing), mobile, and other areas. TSMC's N2 technology overview TSMC's official April 2026 announcement

A point to note here is that Qualcomm describing its use of 2nm is different from being able to determine the foundry or manufacturing site from these materials alone. TSMC's N2 fits as technical background, but the materials we reviewed do not state which TSMC line or which variant will be used to make the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6.

The roles of the Oryon CPU and on-device AI

Qualcomm says the new-generation Oryon CPU reaches 5GHz, with the company optimizing the CPU microarchitecture and implementation in-house. It also introduces "Flex Cache," which lets different types of CPU cores access a shared cache pool.

A cache is a fast storage area that temporarily holds data the CPU uses again and again. If each CPU core uses only its own separate cache, there may be more cases where another core has to read the needed data from memory again. A shared cache is a mechanism that makes it easier for multiple cores to use the same data. However, a shared cache does not always make apps faster. Results vary with the workload, memory design, and limits on heat and power. Explanation of the Oryon CPU and Flex Cache

Another focus is on-device AI. On-device AI is the idea of running AI inference on the smartphone's CPU, GPU, NPU, and other parts instead of sending all input data to the cloud. It may reduce data traffic, keep some processing running where there is no connection, and keep personal data on the device. On the other hand, running large AI models requires enough memory, model compression, cooling, battery capacity, and support from the OS and apps. The SoC specifications alone do not mean every device will offer the same AI experience.

How to read the claim of models over 30 billion

In an additional explanation on September 24, 2026, Qualcomm claimed that the Extreme Gen 6 can run Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models with more than 30 billion parameters on the device. Parameters are the numbers an AI model gains through training, and they are generally used as a rough measure of model size. MoE is a design that does not run all the expert parts of the model every time, but picks some experts to compute based on the input. The aim is to keep the capability of a large model while reducing the amount of computation used per run.

However, the figure of over 30 billion is Qualcomm's product description, and these materials do not show independent benchmark results. Actual speed, answer quality, power use, and heat vary with the model type, quantization, memory capacity, software, and the device's cooling design. So at the time of the announcement, it should be taken as "the maker's claim that it is supported" and compared with hands-on testing of real devices after launch. From on-device AI to choosing software and a learning setup

When you read the Snapdragon announcement, think separately about processing that runs on the device, processing sent to the cloud, and the test conditions. If you re-read public materials and try small exercises, go back to the system requirements of the software you use. Choosing a home PC is different from buying or adding this smartphone SoC. Learning that uses generative AI to summarize public information or write text can also be compared as a separate next step.

Checklist before comparing: supported CPU and GPU, OS, and memory for the exercise software, and the cost difference compared with using the cloud. Separate what you can try with your current setup from what needs upgrading, then choose your options.

Why this announcement matters

This move matters because it presents advanced logic manufacturing, a custom CPU, GPU, NPU, and on-device AI together as one generational change in a smartphone SoC. Until now, attention on AI features has tended to focus on cloud-side performance. If the range of processing that can be done on the smartphone grows, the design of cameras, voice, search, summarization, app control, and more may change.

However, moving to 2nm alone does not automatically give a smartphone high performance and long battery life. Circuit design, operating voltage, manufacturing yield, memory, cooling, the OS, and app optimization all work together. For on-device AI in particular, it is important how models are put on the device, how personal information is handled, and how the accuracy of results is checked.
