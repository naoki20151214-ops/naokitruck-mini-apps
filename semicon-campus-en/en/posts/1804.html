On October 2, 2026, Qorvo announced an RF front-end configuration for X-band AESA radar. At its center is the "QPX0252," a 4-channel CMOS beamformer IC covering 7.9 to 12GHz. This announcement is not a performance demonstration of a finished radar. It shows a set of components that combines several RF functions placed near the antenna and can be used for prototyping and evaluation. This article is based on Qorvo's announcement and the product page.

What was announced?

QPX0252 is described as handling four antenna elements and supporting dual-beam receive and single-beam transmit. Phase and gain can be controlled with 6 bits, and the product information also lists SPI mode selection, telemetry, and sequencing functions. The product document's revision date is September 28, 2026, but the news announcement date is October 2. Because these dates differ, the document date and the announcement date should be read separately.Qorvo QPX0252 product page

According to Qorvo, QPX0252 has enough transmit output to drive a compatible FEM directly without an external driver amplifier. FEM stands for "front-end module." It is a component near the antenna that handles power amplification on the transmit side, low-noise amplification on the receive side, and switching between transmit and receive. A major feature of this configuration is that it may reduce external parts and RF wiring.Qorvo news release

However, QPX0252 alone does not provide all the functions of a GaN power amplifier. In the announced configuration, other Qorvo devices handle gate and drain bias control for the GaN PA and RF switch control. So rather than full integration into a single chip, it is more accurate to see this as an X-band solution that combines a CMOS beamformer, a GaN-compatible FEM, bias control ICs, and RF switches.Qorvo news release

Why this matters for AESA radar

AESA stands for "active electronically scanned array." It adjusts the phase and amplitude of radio waves for each of many antenna elements, changing the beam direction without mechanically rotating the whole antenna. It can scan quickly without relying on mechanical parts. On the other hand, beamformers, FEMs, power supplies, bias circuits, and control lines must be packed densely near each element.Qorvo product information

A beamformer is an IC that gives different phase shifts and gains to the signal of each antenna element. This strengthens radio waves in a specific direction, or makes it easier to pick out signals from a specific direction when receiving. QPX0252 handles four elements and shows a configuration with two beams for receive and one beam for transmit. This supports electronic scanning suited to the radar's application.

X-band generally refers to the frequency range of about 8 to 12GHz. Using higher frequencies makes it easier to get fine spatial resolution for a given antenna aperture. However, it becomes harder to fit element spacing, wiring, packaging, and thermal measures into a small space. QPX0252 can be seen as a component aimed at simplifying beam control and the connection to FEMs, giving more design room inside the array. However, how much actual board area or weight is reduced depends on the design of the whole radar.

The relationship between GaN and thermal design

GaN is a semiconductor material that uses gallium nitride. It is used in RF power amplifiers that require high power density. It may make it easier to handle high power on the radar's transmit side. However, adopting GaN alone does not automatically achieve smaller size or longer range.

In RF power amplifiers, the heat generated affects output, lifetime, reliability, and equipment size. DARPA materials related to THREADS also explain that thermal resistance inside transistors and heat dissipation are important limits for RF devices. For this reason, evaluation needs to check not only the semiconductor material but also the package, heat dissipation structure, power supply, bias control, and variation between channels.DARPA THREADS introduction, DARPA program description

Company claims vs. verifiable facts

Based on its own system analysis, Qorvo claims up to 20dB higher cascaded input P1dB compared with other products on the market. P1dB is a measure of the point where, as the input to an amplifier gets stronger, the output falls 1dB below the ideal proportional relationship. A higher value can indicate more headroom for handling strong signals, but results change with conditions such as frequency, temperature, connection setup, and what it is compared with. Detailed conditions and independent verification cannot be confirmed from the announcement alone, so this cannot be treated as an established advantage across the whole market.Qorvo news release

Qorvo says samples and evaluation kits for QPX0252 are available, and that compatible gate bias, drain bias, and RF switch control devices are also sampling. This shows a stage where designers can start prototyping and evaluation. It does not mean mass production shipments, adoption in customer designs, or actual radar deployment. What can be confirmed from the announcement is the product specifications and sample availability, not production volumes, prices, customer names, or the detection range of a finished radar.Qorvo news release

What to watch next

Going forward, it is important to see whether measured values using the evaluation kit are published. In particular, we would like to check transmit and receive performance across 7.9 to 12GHz, power consumption, temperature rise, thermal resistance, and phase and gain errors between multiple channels. Another point for judgment is how much the number of parts, board area, and RF wiring are reduced compared with conventional configurations when QPX0252, FEMs, and bias control ICs are combined.

As of October 4, 2026, within the scope of Qorvo's news release and product page that could be checked, no new primary update correcting or withdrawing the October 2 announcement was found. However, this does not rule out future specification changes or mass production plans. This news is not an event that proved the performance of a finished radar. It is best seen as a component announcement that brings together the RF functions needed in the limited space of an AESA and moves toward evaluation with real samples.
