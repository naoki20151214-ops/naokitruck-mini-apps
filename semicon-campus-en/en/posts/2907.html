In Lesson 1, we saw that the basic idea of cleaning is "using liquids with different properties in order." In this article, we look at what is inside those liquids.

Two liquids are used: "SC-1" and "SC-2." And there is a clear reason you must not swap their order.

Yellow highlight = key points that appear on this course's tests

What you will learn in this article

  • How SC-1 and SC-2 are mixed and used (ratio, temperature, time)
  • Which contaminants each one removes
  • Why SC-2 is used after SC-1

1. SC-1: an alkaline liquid that removes organics and particles

SC-1 is a mixture of ammonia water, hydrogen peroxide, and pure water. According to KTH lecture materials, the mixing ratio is ammonia water : hydrogen peroxide : pure water = 1:1:5 to 1:2:7, the temperature is 70–80°C, and the time is about 10 minutes. The liquid is alkaline (pH above 7).

Source

KTH (Royal Institute of Technology, Sweden) lecture "Wafer Clean and Wet Processing" (Mikael Östling, 2012). It summarizes SC-1 as oxidizing organic films, complexing metals, slowly dissolving and re-oxidizing the native oxide, and making particles easier to remove.

SC-1 does four jobs.

  • Oxidizes organic films (the power of hydrogen peroxide)
  • Turns some metals into a form that dissolves easily in the liquid (complexes) (cobalt, nickel, copper, zinc, and others)
  • Slowly dissolves the native oxide and re-oxidizes the surface
  • Makes particles easier to lift off the wafer
Let's work with numbers

A 1:1:5 ratio adds up to 1 + 1 + 5 = 7. To make 700 milliliters, use 100 of ammonia water, 100 of hydrogen peroxide, and 500 milliliters of pure water.

The share of pure water is 5 ÷ 7 ≈ 71%. Strong chemicals are used after being diluted quite a lot. (With a 1:2:7 ratio, the total is 10, and pure water is 70%.)

Check quiz: Is the SC-1 liquid alkaline or acidic?

Answer: Alkaline (pH above 7).

2. SC-2: an acidic liquid that removes metals

SC-2 is a mixture of hydrochloric acid, hydrogen peroxide, and pure water. In KTH lecture materials, the ratio is hydrochloric acid : hydrogen peroxide : pure water = 1:1:6 to 1:2:8, the temperature is 70–80°C, and the time is about 10 minutes, and it is acidic (pH below 7). It removes ions such as aluminum, iron, and magnesium, and metals such as gold.

Why is SC-2 used after SC-1?

According to the KTH materials, in the SC-1 liquid, aluminum, iron, and magnesium ions and others form hydroxides that do not dissolve in ammonia. In other words, SC-1 alone may not fully remove these metals, and they can remain (or stick). So the acidic SC-2 is used to dissolve them and remove them as a finishing step.

Let's work with numbers

With 1:1:6, the total is 1 + 1 + 6 = 8. The share of pure water is 6 ÷ 8 = 75%.

Both SC-1 and SC-2 take about 10 minutes, so the two liquids alone take 10 + 10 = about 20 minutes. Rinsing and drying time is added on top. From the time side, too, you can see why repeating cleaning dozens of times matters.

Check quiz: In the SC-1 liquid, which metals end up as hydroxides that do not dissolve?

Answer: Ions such as aluminum, iron, and magnesium.

3. Ultrasound is also used for particles

Combining ultrasound with RCA cleaning improves particle removal. The KTH materials state that the ultrasound frequency is 20–50 kilohertz, or about 1 megahertz. The explanation is that sound waves push liquid in around the particles, and the particles separate from the wafer and float in the liquid.

Let's work with numbers

1 megahertz is 1,000 kilohertz. Compared with 20–50 kilohertz, that is about 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20 times to 1,000 ÷ 20 = 50 times higher in frequency.

4. When RCA cleaning alone is not enough

  • When there is a lot of organic material: A mixture of sulfuric acid and hydrogen peroxide (piranha solution) is used. The KTH materials explain that with sulfuric acid : hydrogen peroxide = 2:1 to 4:1, it turns photoresist into carbon dioxide and water.
  • When removing the native oxide: Diluted hydrofluoric acid (HF) is used (about 1:50, as we saw in Lesson 2).

The KTH materials also introduce the "cleaning method using more dilute liquids" from IMEC, a Belgian research institute. For example, it uses ammonia water : hydrogen peroxide : pure water at 0.05:1:5, with the idea of cutting the amount of ammonia to about 1/20 of RCA. It is a way to remove particles, organics, and metals while using fewer chemicals.

Let's work with numbers

In standard SC-1, ammonia water is "1." In the IMEC method, it is "0.05." Since 1 ÷ 0.05 = 20, the amount of ammonia water is about 1/20.

Key points of this lesson

RCA cleaning goes in the order of alkaline SC-1 (organics, particles), then acidic SC-2 (metals). Both run at 70–80°C for about 10 minutes. SC-2 removes the metals that remain after SC-1, so the order has meaning.

Check quiz: What are the temperature and time for SC-1 and SC-2?

Answer: Both are 70–80°C for about 10 minutes.

Word review

  • SC-1: An alkaline cleaning liquid of ammonia water, hydrogen peroxide, and pure water.
  • SC-2: An acidic cleaning liquid of hydrochloric acid, hydrogen peroxide, and pure water.
  • Complex: A metal in a form that dissolves easily in a liquid.
  • Ultrasonic cleaning: A method that uses the power of sound waves to make particles easier to remove.

Common misunderstandings

Q. Does SC-1 alone remove all contamination?

No. In the SC-1 liquid, some metals become hydroxides that do not dissolve. That is why acidic SC-2 is also used.

Q. Can SC-1 and SC-2 be used in either order?

The basic order is SC-1 first, then SC-2. This is so that SC-2 can remove the metals left after SC-1.
