On September 22, 2026, semiconductor substrate maker AT&S announced that it would expand its collaboration with Marvell Technology and increase its supply capacity for advanced IC substrates for next-generation AI and cloud infrastructure. The site involved is Kulim in Kedah, Malaysia. This announcement also revealed that the additional customer AT&S had described in June 2026 as "another leading technology company" is Marvell. AT&S official announcement

What was announced this time?

According to AT&S, the Kulim expansion includes equipping the existing Plant 2 and building a new manufacturing facility for IC substrate cores and advanced packaging. The goal is to increase capacity for the larger, more complex packages used in high-performance semiconductors for AI and the cloud. However, product part numbers, volumes, prices, allocation within the plant, and the supply start date for Marvell have not been disclosed. Details of the announcement

This news does not mean that specific Marvell products are already being mass-produced in Kulim. What we can confirm is that AT&S announced an expanded collaboration and capacity increase. Construction of production facilities, finalization of contract terms, and actual mass production and shipments each need to be confirmed separately.

What does an IC substrate do in AI semiconductors?

An IC substrate is a high-density wiring base that connects the package holding a semiconductor chip to the circuit board in a data center. It delivers power to the chip and carries signals for computation results and data to the outside. In semiconductors that handle large amounts of power and high-speed signals, such as AI accelerators, wiring length and density, electrical losses, and how heat is released affect performance and stability.

A particularly important term is chiplet. This is a design that splits functions such as processing, input/output, and memory control into multiple small semiconductor dies and combines them in the same package. Compared with putting all functions on one large die, it makes it easier to combine dies made with different manufacturing processes. On the other hand, the wiring that connects the dies at high speed, power delivery, and thermal design become harder.

Another term is heterogeneous integration. This is the idea of integrating chips and components with different materials, manufacturing generations, and functions into one package, and it applies when placing AI compute chips and high-speed memory close together. As packages get larger and the number of connection points grows, IC substrates need more layers, finer wiring, high dimensional accuracy, and stable mass-production capability. This is general technical background. It has not been confirmed that this agreement will be used for a specific chiplet product.

The technical role of the Kulim site

According to AT&S's site information, Kulim produces high-end IC substrates for high-performance processors and began high-volume production in early 2025. Technologies the company lists include ABF substrates, Anylayer with up to 26 layers, 5μm line width and spacing, Embedded Component Packaging (ECP), and FCBGA and FCLGA. AT&S Worldwide

ABF is a type of insulating material used in semiconductor package substrates. It makes it easier to form fine wiring in many layers and is used to connect high-performance processors. "5μm line width and spacing" means the width of the wires and the gap between them can each be made as fine as about 5 micrometers. Smaller numbers make it easier to fit more connections into a limited area, but they also raise manufacturing difficulty and challenges in inspection and yield.

The Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) lists high-performance computing, AI, servers, cloud, and networking among Kulim's target sectors, and explains the local electrical and electronics workforce, logistics, and power and water infrastructure as reasons for the location. Investment plans, and what cannot be confirmed yet

In its June 15 announcement, AT&S said the planned investment for the Kulim expansion was €1.5–2 billion. The plan was described as backed by long-term customer commitments, but the related contracts are subject to final negotiation and signing. In its August 4 update, the company said the expansion was progressing on schedule, but explained that contributions to revenue and profit would be recognized as the project progresses. So this investment amount is a plan presented by the company. It does not mean the full amount has already been spent or that the capacity has been completed. June expansion announcement August progress update

AT&S explains that demand for large, high-density, multilayer substrates will grow as AI semiconductors become larger and move toward chiplets and heterogeneous integration. This is the company's view of the industry. These materials alone do not allow independent verification of total demand in the AI market, the scale of supply shortages, or how much the Marvell collaboration will contribute to market growth.
