On September 21, 2026, MediaTek announced the Dimensity CX C10 Max, the first product under its new PC brand, "Dimensity Compute Experiences (CX)." It is an SoC that combines TSMC's 3nm process, an 8-core CPU, an 11-core GPU, and an NPU with up to 55 TOPS. MediaTek describes it as the flagship SoC for upcoming premium, ultra-thin Googlebooks, but this does not mean laptops with the C10 Max were already on sale at the time of the announcement. MediaTek's official announcement

What was announced

The C10 Max CPU consists of 1 Arm Cortex-X925 core, 3 Cortex-X4 cores, and 4 Cortex-A720 cores. In the product specifications, it is described as an "all big core" design in which all 8 cores are high-performance cores. The GPU is the 11-core Arm Immortalis-G925 MC11, and the chip includes the NPU 890 for AI processing. According to MediaTek, the NPU 890 delivers up to 55 TOPS (dense) of AI compute performance. MediaTek's product specifications

This SoC is designed to combine the CPU, GPU, and NPU on a single semiconductor. The CPU mainly handles general tasks such as the OS and document editing, the GPU handles screen rendering, video, and parallel computing, and the NPU handles AI inference using neural networks. It is also said to support LPDDR5X memory at up to 9600 Mbps, UFS 4.0, 4K60 video processing, and the Imagiq 1090 ISP for cameras up to 32 MP. These are specifications published by MediaTek, not measurements from finished products. MediaTek's product specifications

What does "3nm" mean?

"3nm" in semiconductors does not mean that every part of the chip is uniformly 3 nanometers. It is mainly a name for a manufacturing process generation. TSMC describes N3 as a FinFET-based 3nm technology and positions it as a process aimed at improving performance, power consumption, and chip area. TSMC also says N3 moved into high-volume production in 2022. TSMC's explanation of 3nm technology

In general, if the same functions can be built on a smaller process generation, designers may be able to use that for better performance, power efficiency, and chip area. However, a real laptop's battery life and heat are not determined by the semiconductor process generation alone. The cooling system, power settings, display, battery capacity, OS, and app optimization also have a big effect. Even if MediaTek promotes the chip as making it easier to design thin, light, fanless devices, that is a design goal, not a third-party measurement proving the battery life of real devices.

Does up to 55 TOPS mean AI is always fast?

TOPS is a metric showing how many trillions of operations can be processed per second. The C10 Max's "up to 55 TOPS" represents the upper limit of AI compute that the NPU can deliver under certain conditions. However, this number alone cannot tell you the overall speed of a laptop, the quality of generative AI answers, or how smoothly apps run.

Real AI processing depends on model size, computing precision, memory bandwidth, software, power limits, cooling, and OS and app support. For example, even with the same model, speed, memory use, and the tendencies of the results change depending on whether it runs at INT8, INT16, FP16, or BF16 precision. MediaTek says the NPU 890 supports these precisions and is designed to run generative AI and agentic AI on the device. "On-device AI," which runs on the device itself, can be easier to use in places without a connection and may avoid sending data to external servers. On the other hand, which AI apps actually support it and how fast they run need to be verified with real products and software. MediaTek's product specifications

How much of the link to Googlebook can be confirmed?

In an official article dated the same day, September 21, 2026, Google describes Googlebook as a new laptop category that combines Android technology with the ChromeOS desktop platform. It also emphasizes Gemini Intelligence and integration with Android smartphones. However, the processors named for the first models in that article are from Intel and Qualcomm; the MediaTek C10 Max is not mentioned. Google's official Googlebook article

Also, the planned in-store sales date in Australia mentioned in Google's article is October 5, 2026. This is an announced future plan, not a date showing actual sales of devices with the MediaTek C10 Max. MediaTek's announcement also says the C10 Max will go into "upcoming" Googlebooks, so the specific manufacturers, model names, prices, launch regions, and release dates cannot be confirmed from the sources we checked.

Turning an AI laptop announcement into requirements for home use

Once you understand laptop SoCs, first check the supported OS, required memory, and GPU or NPU requirements of the apps you use. A chip announcement alone does not guarantee its features will work with all software. Deciding whether you need portability or whether a space-saving desk setup is enough lets you compare laptops and mini PCs by use. Do not assume a general PC retailer is where the announced product will be sold; check the specs, warranty, and return terms of the target model before choosing upgrade candidates.

Checklist before comparing: portability, supported OS, memory, GPU/NPU requirements, and ports. Separate what you can try with your current setup from what needs upgrading, and then choose your candidates.

What this announcement means, and its limits

This announcement can be read as a move to extend Arm-based CPUs, GPUs, and NPUs, which developed in smartphones, into a new category: AI laptops. The Cortex-X925 is IP that Arm describes as a high-performance CPU core, and Arm says it also prepared physical implementations for 3nm. However, this background material does not show the C10 Max's measured performance or how it compares with competing products. Arm's explanation of the Cortex-X925

What can be confirmed at this stage is that MediaTek announced the specifications and plans to use the chip in devices. Shipment volumes, the number of manufacturers adopting it, market share, prices, independent benchmarks, and real battery life cannot be confirmed. So it is too early to conclude that "it has successfully entered the AI laptop market in earnest" or that "it is faster than competitors because it has 55 TOPS." Both MediaTek's official announcement and its product page list up to 55 TOPS, and within what we checked, we found no official update correcting this figure.

What to watch from here

  • The manufacturers, model names, prices, launch regions, and release dates of Googlebooks that actually use the C10 Max
  • Performance in real apps that use the CPU, GPU, and NPU, especially processing speed for generative AI, transcription, and summarization
  • The measurement conditions for 55 TOPS, supported models, precision formats, memory bandwidth, and power settings
  • Heat during long use, whether there is a fan, and battery life
  • The range of software actually available, such as ChromeOS, Android apps, and Windows apps

The figures 3nm, 8-core CPU, 11-core GPU, and up to 55 TOPS are important building blocks of an AI laptop. However, you cannot read the usability of a finished product directly from a semiconductor spec sheet. To judge the value of the C10 Max, we need to wait until devices with it go on sale and prices, software support, and third-party measurement results are available.
