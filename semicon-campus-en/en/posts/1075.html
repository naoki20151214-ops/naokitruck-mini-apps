What was announced: a product concept, not the start of mass production

Taiwan's QBit Semiconductor published a roadmap for its "QB88XX" series for robots and drones in a release posted on September 17, 2026. At its core is a design that combines AI processing and servo control on one chip, aiming to support up to 32 motors. This is the company's description of its design and capabilities, not an announcement of mass production or confirmed measured performance. QBit announcement

The dates need attention. The release describes what was shown at SEMICON Taiwan 2026, but the show ran from September 2 to 4. September 17 is the date the release was posted, not the date the products were shown at the venue. The materials we could check do not tell us which day during the show QBit made its presentation. QBit announcement, SEMI opening announcement

How do the CPU and NPU share the work?

The configuration QBit showed for QB88XX combines a four-core Arm Cortex-A78 and an NPU with a Cortex-M7 for servo control, a TGEN, and another NPU. An SoC is a chip that brings multiple processing functions together. The key point here is the plan to split work across different types of compute circuits: higher-level processing on one side and adjusting motor movement on the other. QBit configuration description

A CPU is a circuit that runs a wide range of tasks according to a program. Even among Arm designs, roles differ: Arm describes the Cortex-A78 as a CPU focused on high performance and power efficiency. The Cortex-M7, meanwhile, has DSP functions for processing signals as numbers and floating-point functions for calculations with decimals, and its listed uses include motor control and industrial automation. However, these are general CPU characteristics, not performance measurements of QB88XX. Arm Cortex-A78 overview, Arm Cortex-M7 overview

An NPU is a dedicated circuit for AI calculations that use neural networks. Servo control refers to control that adjusts the gap between actual movement and the target, for example when moving a robot joint to a target angle. QBit also highlights AI-based servo tuning. This setup can be read as a design that brings "judging the situation" and "smoothing the movement" closer together, but integration alone does not prove better response speed or power savings. QBit AI and servo control description

What "up to 32 motors" means and its limits

Beyond support for up to 32 motors, the company lists PCIe, USB, CAN Bus, and camera interfaces. These are connections for exchanging information with peripherals, cameras, and other devices. For a robot with many joints, a setup that can handle many motion commands and sensor signals matters, not just AI computing power. This concept can be seen as a move to expand the role of chips into physical AI, meaning AI involved in the movement of real machines. QBit supported features

However, the number "up to 32" alone does not tell us under what conditions 32 motors can run at once. The control cycle (how often commands are updated), timing variation, and the accuracy of moving multiple axes together also need to be checked. Also, the computing that controls motors and the circuits that supply power to them are separate roles. The announcement does not show that external motor drivers or power circuits become unnecessary. The company's "cerebellum" wording is a design metaphor and does not mean ability equal to the human cerebellum has been proven. QBit announcement and scope of disclosure

Bringing a record in image processing and precision control to new fields

According to QBit's official history, the company was founded in 2016 and has focused on image processing and precision motion control. It states that QB63xx entered mass production in 2020 and QB66xx in 2023, with QB66xx using TSMC's 28nm process. The move into robots and drones can be understood as an effort to extend its technology for handling images and motion. However, the mass production record and manufacturing process of older products cannot be applied directly to QB88XX's maturity or manufacturing conditions. QBit company overview and history

From a multi-axis control concept to learning control and measurement

After reading an announcement about the maximum number of motors, check not just the number of axes but also motion synchronization, communication, stopping during faults, and evaluation conditions. It is important to read concept descriptions separately from a proven track record in real operations. Experience inspecting control panels and drive units, checking operation, and keeping measurement records can be an entry point to equipment and maintenance jobs. Combine relearning electrical and control topics with technical career consultations to sort out what you can handle.

Drones in two stages, and a tracking demo reported by the company

The drone plan has two stages: rolling out QB77XX in 2026–2028, then moving in 2029 or later to a dedicated QB88XX that integrates flight control and mission computing. If you think of flight control as the processing that keeps the aircraft flying, and mission computing as the processing for tasks such as tracking, the goal of integration becomes clear. "2029 or later" is a future plan and does not mean a dedicated product has shipped. QBit drone roadmap

For QB77XX, the company reports that tracking was maintained even when the target was blocked, noting conditions of a relative speed of up to 60km/h and a flight time of 10–15 minutes. The 10–15 minutes does not indicate how long the target stayed blocked or the product's maximum flight time. These are not independent test results either, so comparing performance would require detailed information such as blocking conditions and tracking success rates. A multi-object tracking demonstration is planned for Q4 2026, and as of September 20 it is treated as a future plan. QBit tracking demo description

What to watch next: supply schedule and measurement conditions

To judge whether QB88XX can be adopted in real designs, an official datasheet, sample and mass production schedules, power consumption, and the conditions for running 32 motors will be important. The materials we checked also do not confirm price, customers, manufacturing process, CPU frequency, or NPU performance. For AI servo tuning, the next things to check are the training method, safety measures, and measured results compared with conventional methods. QBit's published roadmap

The scope of security validation also needs to be separated. The U.S. NIST A6932 record covers a firmware implementation called "QB77 Secure Crypto 1.0," with an initial validation date of May 15, 2025. It lists items such as ML-DSA signature verification in Cortex-A76 and Cortex-M33 environments. Signature verification is the process of checking a digital signature attached to data, and this record cannot be used to say that all of QB88XX or a finished drone has been certified as secure. It is important to read design concepts, measured performance, and validation of specific functions separately. NIST A6932 validation record
