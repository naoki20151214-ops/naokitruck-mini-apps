On September 22, 2026, MicroVision announced an "ELSFP (External Laser Small Form Factor Pluggable)" module for AI data centers, developed by its wholly owned subsidiary Scantinel. The idea is to move the laser light source into a small, replaceable external module for an optical connection approach called CPO (Co-Packaged Optics). The announcement featured technology built around Scantinel's two-dimensional photonic integrated circuit (PIC). MicroVision's announcement

What was announced?

According to MicroVision, Scantinel's ELSFP is a module for next-generation CPO systems used in AI data centers. It centralizes the light source and makes maintenance and replacement easier. In optical communication, a laser light source is needed to convert electrical signals into optical signals. Instead of fixing that laser inside the package of a compute chip or optical engine, ELSFP treats it as an external, pluggable part. Full text of the distributed primary announcement

The announcement date was September 22, 2026. Scantinel reportedly unveiled the technology during the week of an optical communications event held in Málaga, Spain. However, what we can confirm is a technology announcement and talks toward commercialization. It does not mean that volume shipments or customer adoption have started.

ELSFP and CPO explained for beginners

What is CPO?

CPO is the idea of placing optical communication parts close to the compute chip and combining the chip and optical engine into one unit. In AI servers, GPUs and dedicated AI accelerators exchange huge amounts of data. When electrical signals travel over long board traces, signal loss and power consumption tend to become problems. So designers are looking at converting electrical signals into optical signals near the chip and connecting them to high-speed optical links.

On the other hand, CPO has challenges with heat and maintenance. A laser is the part that creates light, and it generates heat when it runs. If the laser is built into the chip or package, cooling design becomes more complex, and when it fails, the chip side may also have to be replaced. The external laser approach separates the laser into a place where it is easier to manage. Lumentum's CPO overview also describes how external lasers help with thermal management, maintenance, replaceability, and sharing a light source across multiple optical engines.

What is ELSFP?

OIF's "OIF-ELSFP-02.0" defines ELSFP as a form factor for an external laser that supplies continuous-wave (CW) laser light to optical transceivers in a CPO system. A form factor is a standard shape and specification, such as a part's size and connection method, that lets it fit into equipment.

The OIF document also describes an ELSFP configuration that can be replaced in the field through a pluggable electro-optical blind-mate connection. Blind mate is a mechanism where electrical and optical connections are made simply by inserting the module, without taking the equipment apart and connecting fine wiring by hand. This could make it easier for data center maintenance staff to replace only the laser part. OIF-ELSFP-02.0

Scantinel's technology and what this means

MicroVision places Scantinel's two-dimensional PIC at the center of this ELSFP technology. PIC stands for "Photonic Integrated Circuit." It is an optical circuit that integrates functions such as guiding, splitting, and converting light onto a small substrate, much like a semiconductor. It is similar to integrating electronic circuits onto one chip, but a PIC handles optical signals.

The company says this PIC is the same core technology that supports Scantinel's FMCW LiDAR architecture and can also be applied to optical connections in AI data centers. FMCW is a method that measures distance and speed using light whose frequency changes continuously. On November 10, 2025, MicroVision announced a collaboration to acquire Scantinel's FMCW LiDAR business and related assets. This news can be seen as a move to extend photonics assets used in sensing fields such as automotive to optical connections in data centers. November 2025 acquisition collaboration announcement

In AI data centers, not only computing power but also the performance of the network that moves data between chips is important. If an external laser can be shared by multiple optical engines, it creates maintenance and thermal design options that differ from designs that mount a laser on each chip. However, this is a general advantage of the ELSFP approach. It does not show how much performance Scantinel's module has actually achieved.

What the announcement does not tell us yet

This release does not list wavelength, optical output, communication bandwidth, power consumption, insertion loss, thermal characteristics, or reliability test results. It also does not disclose conformance testing to the OIF specification, the start of volume production, pricing, customer names, or contract amounts. So the claim that it "improves power efficiency and economics" should be read, for now, as a design goal or expectation stated by the company.

MicroVision says it is in talks with several potential licensees and development partners to speed up commercialization. This means it is considering commercialization. It does not mean contracts have been signed, volume adoption has happened, or revenue has been generated. Also, the OIF document explains the standard ELSFP mechanism, but it does not prove that Scantinel's module meets every item in the specification.

What to watch next

The first thing to check is disclosure of concrete specifications. If optical output, power consumption, insertion loss, allowable temperature, number of replacements, and reliability test results are shown, the discussion can move from an announced concept to an evaluation of practicality. Next, announcements of joint development with companies making CPO systems or optical engines, evaluation samples, and standard conformance will be important.

We should also watch whether actual customer names, production timing, sales contracts, and revenue contributions are announced. In AI semiconductors, even a great technical idea will not reach large-scale adoption unless thermal design, optical fiber connection, manufacturing yield, maintenance support, and cost are all solved at the same time. This news shows a direction for applying Scantinel's photonics technology to AI data centers. Product performance and commercial success will be judged by future test results and customer adoption.
