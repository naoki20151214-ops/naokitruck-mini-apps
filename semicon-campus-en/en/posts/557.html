The short answer: "making" a semiconductor includes both design and manufacturing. A company that does not run its own wafer mass-production fab and instead focuses on chip planning, circuit design, and verification is called a fabless company. A contract manufacturer that takes a customer's design and builds the circuits on wafers is called a foundry.

In 30 seconds: why you can make AI chips without a fab

The 30-second answer

Fabless companies design an AI chip's purpose, circuits, performance, power, verification, software, and more. Foundries connect that design to manufacturing processes and build the circuits on wafers. Companies for EDA and IP, materials, manufacturing equipment, dicing, packaging, and testing also join in. Even a company without a fab can become "a company that makes AI chips" by leading the design and turning it into a product.

"Making" a semiconductor includes design and manufacturing

In everyday speech, we tend to lump everything into one word, "make." But with semiconductors, you need to think about at least two separate things: design, which decides what the chip will do, and manufacturing, which forms that circuit on a wafer.

DesignDecide the product's purpose, circuit structure, computing performance, power use, inputs and outputs, verification method, and connection with software.
ManufactureBuild circuits that follow the design rules onto wafers through steps such as masks, deposition, lithography, etching, cleaning, and wiring.

Design alone does not complete a chip, and manufacturing alone does not decide "what kind of AI computing the chip does." Only when the two sides go back and forth on design rules, verification results, manufacturing conditions, and ramp-up information do they get close to stably mass-producing the same design.

Don't get this wrong: Sending design data to a foundry does not mean the chip is finished automatically. The two sides need ongoing collaboration, including designs that fit the manufacturing process, rule checks, physical verification, prototypes, evaluation, and yield improvement.

What does a fabless company do?

Fabless is a business model in which a company does not run its own wafer mass-production fab and focuses on chip planning, design, verification, and productization. "Fab" means a semiconductor plant, and "less" means not having one.

Plan the productDecide which devices it goes in, what AI processing it does, and at what performance, power, and price range.
Design and verify the circuitsDesign the layout of logic circuits, memory, inputs and outputs, and compute units, and check that they work as intended.
Make the whole product workCoordinate software, packaging, testing, mass-production plans, and connection with customers' products.

A fabless company is not "a company that hands off all manufacturing and forgets about it." It is responsible for product specs and design quality, and it brings products to market while coordinating with manufacturing partners and back-end companies. The point of this division of labor is not only avoiding huge fab investments, but also being able to focus resources on design and software.

What does a foundry do?

A foundry is a company that manufactures semiconductors designed by its customers under contract. It combines manufacturing processes, fabs, equipment, materials management, and quality control to build the designed circuits into wafers.

Wafer manufacturing repeats steps many times, such as deposition to make thin films, lithography to transfer circuit patterns, etching to remove unneeded parts, and cleaning to remove dirt and residue. Tokyo Electron's official process guide also lists deposition, lithography, etching, and cleaning as major front-end processes.

However, a foundry's role is not just running equipment. It prepares the design rules and technical information that designers use, and shares prototype lot results, wafer tests, quality and reliability, and production status with customers. TSMC's official eFoundry description also presents collaboration on design, engineering, and logistics as part of its manufacturing service.

One-line summary so far: fabless companies design "what to make," and foundries handle "how to make it on wafers stably."

How do they work together from design data to wafers?

1. Decide product specs and circuitsThe fabless side turns goals for AI processing, performance, power, memory, inputs and outputs, and so on into circuits.
2. Design to fit the manufacturing technologyThe design is matched to the design rules and PDK provided by the foundry. A PDK is a set of design information covering the available devices, dimensions, wiring, verification conditions, and more.
3. Verify the design and tape outCheck not only logic behavior but also wiring, timing, power, and manufacturing rules, and turn it into design data that can be handed off for mask making.
4. Build prototypes and measureThe foundry manufactures wafers and checks test results and yield. The design side also evaluates performance and behavior on real chips.
5. Adjust and move to mass productionAdjust the design, manufacturing conditions, inspection conditions, packaging, and more to get closer to a state where it can be made repeatedly.

EDA, IP, materials, equipment, and the back-end process are also essential

The fabless company and the foundry do not provide everything by themselves. Semiconductors are made possible by layers of specialist companies that support design and manufacturing.

EDADesign automation tools that help with circuit design, placement and routing, timing analysis, power analysis, manufacturing rule checks, and more.
IPVerified design building blocks, such as CPU cores, communication circuits, and memory controllers, offered in a reusable form.
MaterialsSupply silicon wafers, photoresist, gases, chemicals, metals, package substrates, and more.
Manufacturing equipmentCarries out the steps that build circuits on wafers, such as deposition, lithography, etching, cleaning, and inspection / metrology.
Back-end processCuts wafers into chips, assembles them into packages, and makes them able to connect electrically to the outside.
TestingChecks electrical characteristics and functions at the wafer stage and after completion to see whether chips work to spec.

According to Synopsys's official description, EDA supports the design and verification of complex chips and the development of processes and models needed for manufacturing, and it is used together with the company's silicon IP. TSMC's Open Innovation Platform is also described as a system that connects EDA certification, IP, design services, and manufacturing and packaging technology.

After the wafer stage, chips are cut apart by dicing, assembled into packages, and tested. ASE's official information also lists testing services in several stages, such as wafer probing, final test, and system-level test.

Thinking through the flow to everyday AI devices

Taking a chip that goes into an AI server or a smartphone as an example, you can think of the roles flowing like this.

Decide the purpose of the AI processingDecide the needed calculations and use cases, such as image recognition, generative AI, or voice processing.
Design the circuits and softwareDesign the compute units, memory, data movement, and inputs and outputs, and make them usable from software.
Build the circuits on wafersThe foundry stacks many front-end process steps to turn the design into real circuits on silicon.
Cut, assemble, and testCut the chips apart, assemble them into packages, and test them electrically and functionally.
Install them in devicesCombined with boards, cooling, power, memory, the OS, and AI software, they become products that users can touch.

The actual division of roles varies by product: Sometimes one company handles a wide range from design to manufacturing, and sometimes manufacturing, packaging, and testing are split among several companies. A given company does not always use the same manufacturer.

From the map of the division of labor to your own area

Once you understand the difference between fabless and foundry, pick one area you are interested in or have experience with: design, manufacturing, equipment, materials, or assembly and testing. If you have hands-on experience in manufacturing, maintenance, or quality, writing down the processes you handled and the results you checked gives you material for job consultations. For manufacturing jobs open to people new to semiconductors, compare training programs and working conditions, and also keep a path open to relearn any gaps in mechanical, electrical, or quality skills.

What jobs does this division of labor lead to?

Once you understand the difference between fabless and foundry, you can see that "working at a semiconductor company" does not mean just one kind of workplace.

Fabless sideProduct planning, architecture, logic design, physical design, verification, embedded software, and mass-production and quality coordination.
Foundry sideProcess development, process integration, manufacturing, equipment, yield, quality, and customer technical support.
Support and back-end sideEDA/IP development, materials, equipment, dicing, package design, assembly, testing, and reliability evaluation.

Fab work is not the only semiconductor work, and design companies are not the only center of technology. The expertise you need changes depending on where you add value: design, manufacturing, materials, equipment, or the back-end process.

Summary: a single AI chip is built from the strengths of many companies

The conclusion of this article
  • "Making" a semiconductor includes both design and wafer manufacturing
  • Fabless companies do not own fabs and focus on planning, circuit design, verification, and productization
  • Foundries connect customer designs to manufacturing technology and build the circuits on wafers
  • Chips are not made automatically from design data alone; collaboration on PDKs, verification, prototypes, evaluation, and ramp-up is needed
  • AI chips are completed by linking all the way to companies for EDA, IP, materials, equipment, the back-end process, and testing

A company without a fab can make AI chips not because it "makes nothing." It is because companies that focus on design, companies that focus on manufacturing, and the specialist companies supporting them link their strengths together.
