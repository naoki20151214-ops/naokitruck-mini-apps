In AI chips, increasing the number of compute units or their speed alone does not always make overall AI processing faster. If the data needed for calculations cannot be delivered from memory fast enough, the compute units are left waiting. This constraint is commonly called the "memory wall." On October 13, 2026, Credo announced that, together with AMD, it would demonstrate at the OCP Global Summit a way to extend external memory to programmable-logic-based AI accelerators. However, what public materials confirm is the planned demo and the statement that a working emulation platform for evaluation has been built, not the completion of the demo at the venue or measured mass-production performance.Credo official news release

What was announced

The core of the announcement is a setup that combines Credo's OmniConnect Weaver with AMD's Versal AI Edge Series Gen 2 evaluation board. According to Credo, the two companies built an emulation platform that runs "memory disaggregation" with Weaver on Versal and plan to present it at the OCP Global Summit. The OCP Global Summit runs from October 12 to 15, 2026, but the news release was published on October 13. October 12 cannot be treated as the announcement date or the date the demo was completed.Credo official news release OCP official event information

According to Credo, LPDDR memory is connected to a programmable-logic-based XPU via VSR links, providing terabyte-class capacity and bandwidth. The setup is said to use a standard organic substrate without a silicon interposer. Credo also claims that because the extended memory appears transparently above the controller, it can run without changing application software. These are the company's descriptions of the setup, not facts confirmed unconditionally for all software and AI models.Credo official news release

Why the memory wall is a problem for AI inference

Decode and the KV cache

Generative AI inference has two phases: "prefill," which processes the input all at once, and "decode," which generates output tokens one at a time. During decode, the model's weights and the KV cache, which holds conversation history and similar data, are read repeatedly. The larger the model, the longer the context, and the more users handled at once, the more memory capacity and bandwidth are needed. As a result, the memory side that supplies data can determine processing speed more than the peak performance of the compute units. Credo also explains that in this kind of inference, memory, not just compute power, becomes a constraint.Credo technical blog

LPDDR and VSR

LPDDR is a low-power DRAM standard also used in smartphones and other devices. Weaver is described as a "memory fan-out gearbox" that combines an LPDDR5X PHY, 112G VSR SerDes, a lightweight framer, telemetry functions, and more. VSR is the name Credo uses for a connection technology that links memory and compute with high-speed serial links. If memory does not have to be fixed right next to the compute die and can instead be placed separately in the system, it may widen the options for capacity and board design.Credo product brief Credo product page

AMD's Versal and how to read the rated specs

AMD's Versal AI Edge Series Gen 2 is an adaptive SoC that combines programmable logic, integrated CPUs, AI Engines, memory controllers, and more. Unlike chips with only fixed functions, part of the circuitry can be configured flexibly for each use. AMD lists support for LPDDR5X 8533 and memory bandwidth of up to 170GB/s depending on device configuration. The data sheet gives a maximum physical interface speed of 8533Mb/s for single-rank LPDDR5X parts and 7500Mb/s for dual-rank parts. Actual performance depends on design, board, electrical analysis, characterization, and more.AMD product page AMD official data sheet

Meanwhile, Credo rates Weaver at up to 6.4TB of memory capacity, up to 16TB/s of bandwidth, 40ns round-trip latency, and 250mm reach. Here, you should not add AMD's maximum of 170GB/s to Credo's maximum of 16TB/s. The former is a rated value for the memory interface on the Versal side, and the latter is a rated value for the Weaver product. Their scope and conditions differ, and no material shows that both maximum values were measured at the same time on this evaluation board.Credo product page Credo product brief

How to evaluate this announcement

For now, it is best to see this announcement not as a benchmark of a mass-produced AI accelerator, but as a planned implementation demo showing that an external memory extension architecture can be integrated into a programmable-logic-based evaluation platform. The construction of an evaluation platform can be confirmed, but the announcement materials do not confirm completion of the demo, the specific Versal part number, the number of LPDDR chips connected, measured capacity, sustained bandwidth, measured latency, inference throughput, or power consumption. Nor do they give timing for mass-production shipments or adoption in customer systems.Credo official news release

Connecting external memory over high-speed links makes it easier to add capacity, but it also brings new challenges: wiring distance, link congestion, protocol processing, error correction, latency, power, heat, and board signal quality. In AI inference in particular, what matters is not just average bandwidth but also sustained performance when access is concentrated and latency when handling the KV cache. The claim that "no change to application software is needed" is also a description of the setup assumed here, and does not mean it holds regardless of OS, runtime, model, or memory access pattern.

Points to watch

Going forward, we first want to confirm whether the demo actually took place and whether its setup and measurement conditions are published. Then inference throughput with specific AI models, sustained bandwidth during long runs, effective latency, power and cooling conditions, error handling, and software compatibility need to be evaluated separately. Only when mass-production timing, price, customer adoption, and reliability testing are made clear can we judge that it has moved from a research implementation demo to product technology.

As of October 15, 2026, the official Credo, AMD, and OCP materials we checked show no correction or withdrawal of the news release in question. However, demo details and materials may still be updated during or after OCP. The key to understanding this news correctly is to track the announced plan, the company's rated values, and independent measured results separately.Credo official news release
