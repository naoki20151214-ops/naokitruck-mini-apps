Three entry points for finding semiconductor jobs

This page does not rank specific services. It explains the differences between job sites, recruitment agencies, and company careers pages. To decide which service to use, see the guide to choosing semiconductor job services, which is organized by experience.

1. Job sites: an entry point for collecting job posting terms

Search on your ownNumber of jobsCompare conditions

An entry point where you search with words like semiconductor, manufacturing, equipment, quality, inspection, materials, and FSE to get a sense of which job titles, work locations, and working conditions come up.

Strength: easy to see how many jobs there are. Weakness: there are many job titles, so beginners can struggle to tell which ones fit them.
2. Recruitment agencies: an entry point for translating your experience into job posting terms

ConsultationWork historyUnadvertised jobs

An entry point where you get advice on how to put experience in servicing, maintenance, quality, analysis, production engineering, and so on into the language of semiconductor job postings.

Strength: helps you organize how to present your experience. Weakness: strengths vary depending on the recruiter and service.
3. Company careers pages: an entry point for checking where you would be assigned and the workplace

Official company infoWork locationCheck the department

If there is a company you are interested in, check the work location, assigned department, job duties, required experience, and reason for hiring on its careers page.

Strength: you can see information published directly by the company. Weakness: you have to compare with other companies and judge the fit with your experience on your own.

If you are ready to choose a specific service

The entry points to check differ for people completely new to semiconductors, people with mechanical, electrical, or maintenance experience, and people with a science background or manufacturer experience. To choose based on your background, go to the main selection guide.

First, separate the three roles

Job sites: a place to collect terms

Collect the words that often appear in job postings, such as manufacturing operator, equipment maintenance, field service, quality assurance, evaluation, analysis, and materials development.

If you haven't decided on a job type yet, it is easier to use job sites not as "a place to decide where to apply" but as "a place to collect industry terms."
Recruitment agencies: a place to translate your experience

Get advice on which semiconductor job types your experience is closest to. Their role is to put experience in manufacturing, maintenance, quality, chemistry, data, machinery, and so on into job posting terms.

However, the recruiter may not know semiconductors well. If you narrow down your areas of interest a little before the consultation, the jobs you are introduced to will be a better match.
Company careers pages: a place to check the workplace

Check the work location, assigned department, training, the equipment and materials you would handle, whether there is shift work, and how much travel is involved.

It is important not to judge by company name alone, but to read which department and what kind of work it is.

The best entry point depends on your experience

Even among people new to semiconductors, the job posting terms to look for differ for people with factory experience, people who have worked with equipment or machinery, and people with experience close to quality or analysis. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's job tag also describes semiconductor manufacturing as work that includes operating and monitoring equipment, cleanrooms, and inspection. Semiconductor engineers, on the other hand, work in a wider range of technical areas, such as process, design, equipment, materials, quality, and yield improvement.

People with factory or manufacturing experience

Words to look for first: semiconductor manufacturing, manufacturing operator, inspection, cleanroom, shift work

Collect terms on job sites, then check the work location and work schedule on company pages. If you want to read something first, start with the roadmap for people new to semiconductors.
People with machinery or equipment experience

Words to look for first: equipment maintenance, maintenance, equipment start-up, field service, FSE

This is an area where an agency can easily translate your experience. Compare job postings from equipment makers, fab equipment departments, and engineering staffing firms.
People with quality or analysis experience

Words to look for first: quality assurance, reliability evaluation, measurement, analysis, defect inspection

The words in job postings can look difficult, so it helps to check the terms in the list of job posting keywords first.
People with chemistry or materials experience

Words to look for first: chemicals, gases, resist, slurry, materials evaluation, analysis

This entry point easily leads to materials makers and evaluation roles. It is easier to understand if you also read articles that link processes and materials, such as the one on CMP slurry.

Use job sites to "learn the terms" rather than to "search"

When people new to semiconductors look at job sites, the many unfamiliar words can be tiring at first. So change your first goal from applying to collecting terms. Semiconductor manufacturing, equipment maintenance, quality assurance, evaluation, analysis, materials, process, field service. As you see these words again and again, the entry points that suit you start to become clear.

On job sites, look at the "words describing the work" before the annual salary.

Annual salary and company names matter, but when you are new to semiconductors, the first step is to increase the number of job posting terms you can read.

An agency is not "a place to get answers" but "a place to get translation"

When using a recruitment agency, the key is not to just say, "I want to get into the semiconductor industry." Do you have manufacturing experience? Have you worked with equipment? Is your background closer to quality or analysis? Are you interested in chemistry or materials? Sharing this makes it easier for the recruiter to find jobs for you.

Employment placement services have rules for displaying information about jobs and job seekers accurately. Even so, you need to check for yourself whether the jobs introduced to you are a good fit. Judge each introduced job by its duties, work location, work schedule, training, and the experience you would gain for the future.

On company careers pages, look at the "assigned department," not the "company name"

When you look at semiconductor companies' careers pages, it is easy to focus on whether the company is famous. But in a career change, the company name is not everything. Which fab? Which department? Equipment or materials? Manufacturing or quality? Lots of travel? Shift work? Looking at all of this changes your picture of life after joining quite a bit.

Five things to check before signing up

1. Does it handle semiconductor jobs?Check whether you can find jobs in semiconductors, manufacturing equipment, materials, quality, inspection, process, FSE, and so on.
2. Are there jobs in your preferred location?Semiconductor jobs vary by region. Decide first whether you can widen your search area or will look locally.
3. Are there explanations for people new to semiconductors?Check whether training, onboarding support, application requirements, and preferred qualifications are described concretely.
4. Can you prepare information to share with the recruiter?Writing down your experience, interests, conditions, and concerns improves the quality of the meeting.
5. Can you still compare even if you feel rushed?Don't decide based on one company alone. Compare job duties, work style, work location, and training.

Note
This article does not urge you to sign up for any particular job service. Job availability, application requirements, compensation, and whether you can be introduced vary by company, region, timing, and experience. Before applying, always check each service's or company's official information, the job posting, and what was discussed in your meeting.

Summary: Compare in the right order

When comparing semiconductor job services, starting from your own entry point is less confusing than starting from service names. Collect terms on job sites, translate your experience with an agency, and check the workplace on company careers pages. In this order, even people new to semiconductors can gradually build up the information they need to decide.
