Think of a GPU as a "fast worker" and HBM as a "warehouse that stores materials." No matter how capable both are, if they sit far apart and are linked by a narrow road, the worker ends up waiting for materials. A key role of advanced packaging is to put the worker and the warehouse on the same site and connect them with short, multi-lane passages.

Advanced packaging is not just an "outer box"

Semiconductor packages have always protected fragile chips from moisture and shock and connected them electrically to external circuit boards. In advanced packaging, another role comes to the front: integrating multiple dies or chiplets at high density inside one package to unlock system performance.

Carrying signalsConnects the GPU and HBM with many short wires.
Delivering powerCreates paths that steadily supply large currents to each die.
Handling heat and stressMaintains reliability by considering heat dissipation, warpage, and differences in thermal expansion between materials.

Why connect the GPU and HBM up close?

Even if a GPU can run parallel computations quickly, its compute units have to wait if the data they need doesn't arrive. HBM is memory that delivers data to the GPU with wide memory bandwidth. To make use of that wide I/O, you need to build many short wires between the HBM and the GPU.

Shorter distances and higher density make it easier to improve energy per data transfer and signal quality, compared with routing the same connections long distances across an external board. However, placing them close together does not automatically make things faster. You need a balance that includes I/O design, power, heat, software, and the workload.

How 2.5D, interposers, and substrates relate

TermMeaning in this articleCommon confusion
2.5DAn integration method that places several active dies close together, mainly side by side, and connects them with high-density intermediate wiringNot the same as 3D, which stacks logic dies on top of each other
InterposerAn intermediate structure between the dies and the package substrate that handles fine wiring between diesNot always a full silicon layer; there are other methods such as RDL
Package substrateThe base that spreads the fine connections above out toward the circuit board below, and also handles power and mechanical supportNot the same part as the interposer
Advanced packagingA group of technologies that together achieve multi-die integration, high-density connections, power, heat, and reliabilityDoes not refer to one fixed layer structure

What does CoWoS refer to?

CoWoS is TSMC's advanced 2.5D packaging platform, named after "Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate." TSMC's official description shows it being used to integrate multiple SoCs and HBM stacks. There are versions such as CoWoS-S, CoWoS-L, and CoWoS-R, and the setup is not just one type: it can use a full silicon interposer, local silicon interconnects, or an RDL-based approach.

An important distinction: CoWoS is TSMC's own brand. "2.5D = CoWoS" is not true, and neither is "all AI GPUs use CoWoS." Other companies have different packaging technologies, and even products from the same company change structure depending on use, generation, cost, and required bandwidth.

What gets harder in exchange for high performance

HeatThe closer the GPU and HBM are, the more important it becomes to design for local heat generation and heat dissipation paths.
Yield and testingA defect anywhere in the multiple good dies, bonds, wiring, or final package affects the whole product.
Warpage, stress, and costDifferences in thermal expansion between materials, larger sizes, fine connections, and complex processes all need to be managed in mass production.

It is a field where materials, wafer processing, bonding and assembly, substrates, inspection, thermal design, equipment, and quality assurance work together. Frequently asked questions about advanced packaging

Q. Is a package just a case that wraps the chip?

Protection matters, but that's not all. It also handles external signal connections, power delivery, heat dissipation, mechanical support, and integration of multiple dies.

Q. Is 2.5D a structure that stacks HBM on top of the GPU?

In typical 2.5D, logic such as a GPU and HBM are placed side by side and connected through intermediate high-density wiring. Inside HBM, DRAM dies are stacked vertically, but that is a separate kind of stacking.

Q. Are the interposer and the package substrate the same?

No. The interposer is an intermediate structure that mainly handles fine wiring between dies, while the package substrate is the base that spreads wiring outward and handles power delivery and connection to the circuit board.

Q. Do all AI GPUs use CoWoS?

No. CoWoS is one of TSMC's technology brands. The structure of GPUs and accelerators differs by maker, product, generation, and use, and there are other 2.5D methods, local bridges, and different memory setups.

  2. In 2.5D, a typical setup places logic and HBM side by side and connects them with fine wiring such as an interposer
  3. CoWoS is TSMC's own platform, not a general name for all GPUs or all 2.5D
