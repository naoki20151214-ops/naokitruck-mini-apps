On September 30, 2026, MediaTek announced that its flagship mobile SoC (System on Chip), the Dimensity 9500, has been adopted in Samsung's new Galaxy Tab S12 Ultra and Galaxy Tab S12+. Samsung also said on the same day that both models use the Dimensity 9500 as their AP (application processor). What can be confirmed at the time of the announcement is the adoption and product launch announcement; actual sales are scheduled to start on October 7, 2026.MediaTek announcement, Samsung announcement

What is new?

The Dimensity 9500 is a flagship SoC for tablets that integrates the CPU, GPU, NPU, and more into one semiconductor package. MediaTek says it uses a TSMC 3nm-class process, and the product page specifies "TSMC N3P." A finer manufacturing process is the foundation for putting more transistors in the same area and improving the balance between performance and power. However, 3nm does not automatically mean better battery life or speed in a real device. Device cooling, software, the screen, battery capacity, and more also affect the result.MediaTek technical page

The CPU uses an "All Big Core" design. This means it is built mainly around high-performance cores rather than small power-saving cores. According to MediaTek, it has eight cores in total: one Arm C1-Ultra, three C1-Premium, and four C1-Pro. This makes it easier to handle app launches and multitasking with strength, but running high-performance cores tends to raise heat and power use, so power control and cooling design become important.

A simple guide to NPUs and on-device AI

An NPU is a dedicated circuit that efficiently handles the calculations in machine learning models. While the CPU handles a wide range of tasks and the GPU is good at drawing images and large amounts of parallel computing, the NPU aims to handle common AI calculations, such as speech recognition, image processing, and generative AI inference, with less power.

The Dimensity 9500 has an "NPU 990" and a second-generation "Gen-AI Engine." MediaTek describes faster token generation, generative and agentic AI, 4K-resolution text-to-image generation, and an eNPU using compute-in-memory. A token is a small unit that generative AI uses when processing and generating text. Faster token generation could shorten the wait before text or answers appear.MediaTek product description

On-device AI may let some processing continue where connections are unstable, may make responses faster, and may widen the range of tasks where input data does not need to leave the device. But having an NPU is not the same as every AI feature running fully offline. Samsung says some Galaxy AI features may require a Samsung account or an internet connection, and depend on region, language, OS, and service terms. Which features run on the device and which use the cloud needs to be checked feature by feature.Samsung Galaxy Tab S12 product page

What the GPU means for a large-screen tablet

MediaTek also highlights a 12-core GPU, hardware-accelerated ray tracing, and support for Unreal Engine 5.5+. Ray tracing is a technique that calculates light reflections and shadows to draw more realistic images. These specifications are aimed at heavy workloads such as games, 3D creation, and video editing. However, "support" means it can be used in supported apps and settings; it does not guarantee high frame rates or image quality in every game.MediaTek adoption announcement

Samsung positions the Galaxy Tab S12 Ultra and S12+ as products that bring productivity, Galaxy AI, creativity, and gaming together in one large-screen experience. The Ultra has a 14.6-inch screen and the S12+ a 12.6-inch screen. Both use the same Dimensity 9500, but they differ in screen size, weight, battery, connectivity specifications, and more. According to Samsung, compared with the Galaxy Tab S11 Ultra, the S12 Ultra improves NPU processing by up to 61%, CPU performance by 19%, and GPU performance by 17%. However, these are comparison figures from Samsung, not independent third-party measurements that include test conditions.Samsung Global Newsroom

Why this adoption matters

Until now, competition among high-performance smartphone SoCs has focused on peak CPU and GPU performance, plus camera and AI processing. This adoption is an example of MediaTek's flagship SoC being used to promote on-device AI and heavy workloads not only in smartphones but also in large-screen premium tablets. On tablets, people may write documents, join online meetings, edit images, watch videos, and play games at the same time. That makes it important to design not just the CPU, but the NPU, GPU, cooling, memory, OS, and AI services together.

In other words, the point of this news is not just the numbers for the NPU 990 or the 12-core GPU. It is the attempt to combine an SoC made on an advanced process with Samsung's large screens, cooling system, apps, Galaxy AI, and experiences like the S Pen and DeX, bringing AI closer to an everyday tablet feature rather than a special experiment. This is an interpretation of product strategy drawn from the announcements; it does not prove sales volume, market share, or an advantage over competing products.

Compare tablet AI by use case and learning setup

Once you understand how the CPU, GPU, and NPU share the work, pick one task you want to try, such as reading documents, writing, or spreadsheets. Separate on-device processing from cloud use, and first check what you can do with your current device. Learning to use generative AI to summarize public information and upgrading your home PC are separate decisions. Do not assume the chip in a tablet can be bought for a PC; compare equipment based on the supported OS and required performance of the software you will use.

Notes to check before comparing: supported OS, browser, required memory, ports, and budget. Separate what you can try with your current setup from what needs an upgrade, and then pick candidates.

Separate announcements from results

To sort out the dates: on September 30, MediaTek and Samsung announced the products and adoption. Samsung's notice for Japan says the devices can be tried at some stores from October 1, with sales starting October 7. October 7 is an announced sales plan; it does not mean that stock, prices, or regional rollout after launch had been confirmed at the time of research.Samsung Newsroom Japan

Also, the performance gains and AI feature descriptions from manufacturers are helpful when choosing a product, but they must be kept separate from independent benchmarks and long-term use results. Actual speed and battery life change with app optimization, screen brightness, network conditions, heat, and whether processing happens on the device or in the cloud.

What to watch next

  • After sales start on October 7, how real devices are rated for heat, battery life, gaming performance, and stability with multiple apps.
  • How far each Galaxy AI feature runs entirely on the device, and when it needs an internet connection.
  • Whether Samsung's stated NPU, CPU, and GPU gains hold up in third-party benchmarks and in real creative and work apps.
  • When using generative AI on a large-screen tablet, how privacy, pricing, supported languages, and available regions are designed, not just processing speed.

This announcement is one example showing that semiconductor competition is not just about "fast chips," but has become competition over the whole device experience, including AI processing, power, cooling, apps, and how work is split with the cloud. Going forward, it is important to look separately at announced specifications and verification results from real devices after launch.
