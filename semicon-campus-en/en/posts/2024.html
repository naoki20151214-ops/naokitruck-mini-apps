On October 12, 2026, semiconductor test companies FormFactor and Teradyne announced a technology collaboration covering the development, validation, and ramp to mass production of next-generation semiconductors. Teradyne has agreed to make a $300 million strategic investment in FormFactor common stock. However, the investment is subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approval, and the announcement does not say the investment was completed on October 12.FormFactor and Teradyne announcement

What was announced

The collaboration targets HPC, advanced packaging, HBM, and silicon photonics. The companies explain that FormFactor will bring expertise in probe technology, interfaces, test engineering, and device characterization, while Teradyne will provide automated test platforms.

In semiconductor manufacturing, there is a step that checks small chips on the wafer, and a step that checks chips after they are assembled into packages. This collaboration aims to connect these different steps from the development stage through mass production. The announcement promotes a test ecosystem "from wafer to data center" and expanded joint customer support, but these are the two companies' plans and expectations, not proof of performance or sales effects already achieved.

How semiconductor testing works, for beginners

What is a probe card?

A probe card is a component that presses many fine contacts onto chips on a wafer to send and receive electrical signals. Because it can check electrical characteristics before the chips are cut apart, it is used to keep defective chips from moving on to the back-end process. According to FormFactor, its HFTAP K32 supports speed testing at up to 3.2GHz and 6.4Gbps at the wafer stage, and is intended to check the performance and yield of HBM-related devices and interposers. It is said to support fine pitches down to 50 micrometers.FormFactor HFTAP Series

ATE and test cells

ATE (automatic test equipment) is equipment that applies set signals and power to a semiconductor and automatically judges whether it works correctly. In real manufacturing, ATE is not used alone. It is used as a test cell, combined with a prober that holds the wafer, a handler that moves finished products, contact parts, and so on. Teradyne says it provides integrated solutions that combine this group of equipment, from the design stage through mass production.Teradyne's test cell explanation

Why it matters for HBM and advanced packaging

In semiconductors for AI computing, designs that place compute chips close to large-capacity memory are becoming important. HBM stacks multiple memory dies, and advanced packaging combines multiple dies and interposers. If a defect is found after assembly, the entire expensive package, including several good parts, may be wasted.

That is why Known Good Die checks, which select good dies before assembly, are important. This is the idea of confirming that the dies sent to the back-end process have enough electrical performance. FormFactor's HFTAP promotes wafer-stage evaluation of HBM-related devices and interposers, and Teradyne explains that its Magnum 7H covers HBM base-die wafers, the stage after stacking but before singulation, and the stage after singulation.Teradyne Magnum 7H

So if the two companies' technologies work well together, it may become easier to use test data gathered at the wafer stage when designing tests after stacking and in mass production. However, this is a technical meaning drawn from published product descriptions and the collaboration details. It does not mean a joint product has been completed, or that benefits have been confirmed on a specific customer's mass production line.

What this announcement does not tell us

  • The number of shares, issue price, ownership percentage, and expected completion date of the $300 million investment have not been disclosed.
  • The specific equipment, software, probes and interfaces, and data-sharing specifications to be jointly developed have not been revealed.
  • No figures have been given for reductions in test time, yield improvement, time to ramp to mass production, or when customers will adopt it.
  • Which products in silicon photonics and advanced packaging will be jointly sold or jointly developed is also undecided.

So the phrase "integrated from wafer to mass production" works as a summary of the direction this collaboration is aiming for. But it cannot be stretched to mean that a single integrated product is already complete, or that results have been achieved on mass production lines.

What to watch from here

The first thing to check is the closing of the $300 million investment and regulatory approval. Next, the focus will be the names of equipment and software the two companies offer jointly, which process steps will share test data, and when customer adoption will begin.

On the technical side, it is necessary to check whether the same test approach can be applied from the HBM base die through stacking and after singulation, and how finer and faster probe contacts affect mass production yield. In the official news lists after the announcement, no correction or withdrawal regarding this collaboration had been found as of October 14, 2026. However, note that there has been no follow-up disclosure confirming completion of the investment or effects on mass production.Teradyne Newsroom FormFactor press releases

In the race for AI chip performance, what matters is not only the chip itself, but how reliably complex packages can be inspected so that only good parts are assembled. This announcement shows the possibility of connecting those test steps, but for now it is a collaboration agreement that includes a capital investment. To judge its real value, we need to follow the facts in order: deal completion, joint products, customer adoption, and mass production data.
