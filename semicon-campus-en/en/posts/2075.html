On October 13, 2026, chip design company Rambus announced a PMIC for "LPDDR6 SOCAMM2" for AI servers and said it had begun shipping samples to lead customers. Note that this does not announce the start of mass production of servers with LPDDR6 SOCAMM2. In Rambus's announcement, the target SOCAMM2 is described as currently in development. What can be confirmed is that a power management chip for a next-generation memory module has been designed and is being provided for customer evaluation.Rambus official announcement

What does the announced PMIC do?

PMIC stands for "Power Management Integrated Circuit." It is not the DRAM that stores memory data itself. It is a semiconductor that converts power supplied from the server into voltages that memory chips and circuits on the module can use. It handles stepping voltage down, controlling the order in which power is turned on, monitoring operating status, and protecting against faults.

A server's power supply does not run on just one voltage. DRAM, management circuits, signal circuits, and others each need different conditions, so power must be distributed stably on the module. Unstable voltage can lead to data errors or boot failures. Rambus lists power conversion, power sequencing, telemetry, and fault protection as functions of this PMIC. Telemetry is a mechanism that measures conditions such as voltage and current so the system can monitor them.Function description in the announcement

Why LPDDR and SOCAMM2 are getting attention in AI servers

LPDDR is a memory family that focuses on lower power consumption compared with typical DDR memory for servers. In AI servers, not only GPUs and CPUs but also large amounts of memory run for long periods. Even if the power savings per memory chip are small, the difference becomes large across a whole data center as the number of chips grows. Also, since power turns into heat, lower power consumption affects not only electricity costs but also the load on cooling equipment.

SOCAMM2 is a setup that treats low-power LPDDR as a replaceable module for data centers. LPDDR is sometimes mounted directly on the board to prioritize power savings, but making it a module can make maintenance and replacement easier. In other words, SOCAMM2 is an approach that tries to combine LPDDR's power efficiency with ease of handling as a server component. However, how much of that benefit is realized in the LPDDR6 generation will depend on actual products and measurement results.Rambus explanation of SOCAMM2

On the module, not only the PMIC but also the SPD Hub is important. The SPD Hub is a circuit that identifies information such as memory capacity and configuration and supports system management and telemetry. With the PMIC handling power and the SPD Hub supporting module information and management functions, the server can more easily recognize and operate the memory.

What is happening in the current LPDDR5X generation?

LPDDR6 is a next-generation candidate, but the earlier LPDDR5X SOCAMM2 is also moving forward. On March 3, 2026, Micron announced that it had shipped customer samples of a 256GB SOCAMM2 using LPDDR5X. This product uses a monolithic 32Gb LPDDR5X design and is said to enable 2TB of LPDRAM per 8-channel CPU. This is a product trend from a different generation from this LPDDR6 PMIC.Micron 256GB SOCAMM2 announcement

Micron claims that under specific comparison conditions, SOCAMM2 can cut power and footprint each to one-third of a comparable RDIMM. However, this comparison is based on Micron's internal tests and calculations. Results change if the server configuration, capacity, bus width, or access patterns change, so it cannot be applied directly to the performance of LPDDR6 SOCAMM2 or this Rambus PMIC.Micron SOCAMM2 product page

What this announcement tells us, and what is still unknown

Rambus describes this PMIC as part of a roadmap that extends the chipset it announced for LPDDR5X SOCAMM2 to the next-generation LPDDR6 SOCAMM2. Sample shipments show that customer evaluation has started, but they do not mean mass production has begun. Customer names, sample quantities, product part numbers, supported voltages, current ratings, manufacturing process, and mass production timing have not been disclosed.

The announcement also has no independently verified figures from performance measurements of real servers with LPDDR6 SOCAMM2, or on AI inference speed, power consumption, or cost-effectiveness. The contribution to AI infrastructure power efficiency and "tokens per watt" that Rambus describes should be read as the product's aim or the company's explanation. The PMIC is an important foundational component, but it is not a chip that directly increases DRAM storage capacity or the compute performance of AI accelerators.

Points to check going forward

  • Whether the final standard specifications for LPDDR6 SOCAMM2 and the supporting CPUs and server platforms are announced.
  • Whether Rambus's PMIC moves from the sample stage to mass production, and whether product part numbers and electrical specifications are disclosed.
  • How memory capacity, bandwidth, power consumption, heat, and serviceability are measured in actual servers.
  • Whether the comparison results shown for the LPDDR5X generation are reproduced in the LPDDR6 generation, and whether they are verified by third parties and in multiple configurations, not just under manufacturer-specific conditions.

This news shows that the progress of AI servers is not determined only by GPUs and CPUs. It also extends to the power management and module design that support memory. On the other hand, at this point we have not seen finished LPDDR6 SOCAMM2 products or mass-produced servers. Going forward, checking sample evaluation results, progress on standardization, supported platforms, and measured data in turn will be the key to telling plans apart from results.
