On October 14, 2026, Microchip Technology announced the "POL-05-20S-T," a radiation-tolerant GaN point-of-load (POL) converter for high-performance processors used in space. The company says it supports up to 20A of continuous output, comes in a 25g surface-mount package, and is available in production quantities. This announcement supports better performance for computers on satellites through smaller power supplies and radiation protection. However, the announced specifications do not mean flight heritage on a specific satellite or guaranteed operation in every orbit.Microchip official announcement

What was announced?

A POL converter is a power component that converts the voltage supplied from a satellite's power bus into the lower voltage needed near devices such as FPGAs, ASICs, and microcontrollers. Because a processor's current changes quickly depending on its workload, the power supply must handle sudden load changes while keeping the voltage stable. A POL, which converts power close to the load, makes it easier to reduce losses and voltage swings caused by power wiring.Microchip's guide to space DC-DC converters

For the POL-05-20S-T, Microchip gives an input voltage range of 4.75 to 13.2V and an adjustable output voltage of 0.7 to 5V. The package measures 1.51×1.51×0.45 inches, weighs 25g, and has an operating temperature range of minus 55 to plus 100°C. Features listed include synchronous buck conversion, current-mode control, remote sensing, undervoltage and overvoltage protection, soft start, enable, and power good. Meeting the size and power limits of satellite processors through small size and high power density is the company's design intent. No third-party measurements showing how much smaller or more power-efficient a whole satellite can become were found.

Why GaN and POL matter

GaN (gallium nitride) is a semiconductor material used in power switches. It works well with power supply designs that switch at high frequencies, and in some cases it can help shrink surrounding parts such as inductors and capacitors and achieve high power density. Satellites have limited weight, volume, and available power, so the more the onboard computer's performance increases, the more it matters to keep the power supply compact.

However, adopting GaN alone does not determine efficiency, heat, or reliability. The control method, switching frequency, surrounding parts, mounting, heat dissipation, electromagnetic noise, input conditions, and load changes must be evaluated together. This official announcement does not include efficiency curves, switching frequency, or actual satellite adoption cases. It is too early to say that "because it is GaN, it is always better than conventional power supplies."

How to read the space radiation specifications

Two important terms for space semiconductors are total ionizing dose (TID) and single-event effects (SEE). TID is a phenomenon in which long-term exposure to radiation changes conditions inside a semiconductor, gradually shifting characteristics such as threshold voltage and leakage current. It is easiest to think of it as cumulative degradation that progresses over time.

SEE are temporary or permanent faults that occur when a single particle, such as a heavy ion or high-energy particle, passes through a semiconductor. Sometimes the result is only a minor malfunction, and sometimes it can destroy the device. NASA broadly divides the effects of space radiation into TID, displacement damage in materials, and SEE. It also explains that SEE range from recoverable faults to destructive failures.NASA Goddard explanation NASA technical bulletin

Microchip says that for the POL-05-20S-T, it specifies electrical characteristics before and after irradiation up to a TID of 50krad, and has characterized SEE up to LET 43 MeV·cm2/mg. LET is a measure of how much energy a particle deposits per unit distance as it passes through a material. However, 50krad and LET 43 are numbers showing the range of tests and evaluations confirmed for the part. They do not guarantee that it will never fail in a specific orbit, shielding thickness, or mission duration, nor do they guarantee the radiation tolerance of a whole satellite.

Significance and limits of the announcement

What is new here is that Microchip has stated that a high-current GaN POL power supply, with radiation evaluation aimed at space processing systems, is available in production quantities. Putting high-performance FPGAs and ASICs on satellites requires managing not only computing performance but also radiation, heat, electromagnetic noise, and power conversion at the same time. Smaller, higher-current power supplies could widen the room for that design work.

On the other hand, a product announcement, a supply plan, and independent test results are not the same thing. Saying it is available in production quantities indicates a sales plan or supply guidance, but it does not confirm inventory, lead times, customers, or spaceflight heritage. Efficiency curves, particle conditions for SEE testing, failure modes, sample counts, testing organizations, and specific electrical values after irradiation were also not found in the materials checked this time.

As NASA's design knowledge base shows, radiation risk is judged by estimating the environment from the orbit and shielding, then reflecting part-level TID and SEE evaluations in fault countermeasures at the circuit, system architecture, and software levels. For this reason, a satellite's lifetime or reliability cannot be determined from this product's numbers alone.NASA Spacecraft Systems Resilience and Integrity Knowledge Base

What to watch next

  • Whether detailed power supply data, such as efficiency curves, switching frequency, and response to sudden load changes, is published.
  • Whether the particle conditions for SEE testing, test sample counts, failure modes, and performance changes after irradiation are made clear.
  • Whether adoption in actual satellites or spacecraft, flight heritage, and mission durations can be confirmed.
  • Whether the statement about production supply leads to actual inventory, lead times, and qualification processes.
  • Whether GaN's advantages of small size and high power density are confirmed at the whole-system level, including thermal design and EMI countermeasures.

As of October 15, 2026, the main information is Microchip's own product announcement. So at this stage, the right way to sum it up is: "Microchip announced it is starting supply of a radiation-tolerant GaN POL converter for space in production quantities." If datasheets, independent tests, and adoption results are published in the future, its value in actual spacecraft design can be evaluated more concretely than from the announced specifications.
