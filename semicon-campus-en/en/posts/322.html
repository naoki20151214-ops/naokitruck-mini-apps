What is defect inspection?

Defect inspection is the step that examines wafer surfaces, thin films, wiring patterns, chip appearance, and more, during or after semiconductor manufacturing, to find abnormalities that lead to defects. Because semiconductor circuits are extremely fine, even particles invisible to the naked eye or a slight pattern shift can be a big problem for the product.

For example, if a small particle is left between two wires, it can cause an electrical short. On the other hand, if a thin line that should be connected is broken, it becomes an open circuit. If film thickness or line width drifts from the standard, electrical characteristics vary. Defect inspection exists to find these problems early.

Why defect inspection directly affects yield

Yield is the share of chips made that can actually be used as good products. If defect inspection is weak, you miss where defects are increasing. Then you keep making defects with the same tool, the same materials, and the same process conditions.

In fabs with strong defect inspection, people can ask questions like "Which lot did they increase in?", "Are there more at the center of the wafer or at the edge?", "Did they increase after passing through a specific tool?", and "Was it after cleaning, lithography, or etching?" Narrowing down possible causes from how defects appear is more important than simply finding them.

The essence of defect inspection is "making the next decision faster."

Defects do not disappear the moment you inspect them. But once you know the location, type, and timing of defects, you can take action, such as changing process conditions, checking tools, reviewing cleaning, or checking material lots. This is yield improvement.

Main defects found by inspection

Not all defects are the same. Particles, scratches, bridges, opens, and overlay errors have different causes and fixes. Understanding them separately makes it easier to see how the steps of semiconductor manufacturing connect.

Defect maps show which part of the process is suspicious

In defect inspection, people sometimes look at where defects are on the wafer like a map. It is easiest to think of this as a defect map, or wafer map. Whether defects are scattered randomly, concentrated at the edge, or appear as streaks in one direction changes which causes you suspect.

For example, if there are many defects only at the wafer edge, wafer transfer, holding, chemical flow, or edge processing are suspected. If only a specific lot is bad, material lots or tool conditions are suspected. If defects increase only in specific patterns, lithography or etching conditions may be involved.

Equipment and companies involved in defect inspection

Defect inspection involves optical inspection tools, electron beam inspection tools, SEM review tools, mask inspection tools, metrology tools, data analysis software, and more. Inspection equipment plays the role of "turning invisible defects into visible information" inside a semiconductor fab.

Leading companies include KLA, known for inspection, metrology, and process control; Lasertec, known for photomask and EUV-related inspection; and Applied Materials, which covers manufacturing equipment as well as metrology and control. For readers in Japan, understanding inspection equipment companies shows that the semiconductor industry is not made up only of "companies that make chips."

Summary

Defect inspection is the step that finds tiny defects on wafers and chips. But its real value is not just "finding defects." It lies in reading defect types, locations, timing, and lot trends, and sending clues for improvement back to the process, equipment, materials, cleaning, lithography, etching, CMP, and more.

If you want to understand semiconductor manufacturing at an intermediate level, you cannot skip defect inspection. That is because yield, quality, process improvement, equipment maintenance, and data analysis all connect here.
