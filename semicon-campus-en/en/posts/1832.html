On October 5, 2026, Rapidus announced that it would build "Rapidus Collaborative Open Rapid Ecosystem (Rapidus CORE)," a partnership framework to support the development and mass production of advanced semiconductors. As a first step, it named 17 companies from Japan and overseas as "Design Solution Associates," including Cadence Design Systems, Synopsys, Dai Nippon Printing, TOPPAN, and Toshiba Information Systems. This news does not announce new performance figures or mass production results for 2nm chips. It is an announcement about setting up points of contact with design companies and related firms, to make it easier for customers to design chips using Rapidus's 2nm process.Rapidus announcement

What Rapidus CORE has started

According to Rapidus, Design Solution Associates connect customers with Rapidus and provide each company's design resources and expertise. Developing advanced semiconductors requires not only a manufacturer, but many specialized fields such as circuit design, EDA tools, semiconductor IP, design verification, and packaging. CORE is an ecosystem, in other words a network of partner companies, that organizes these firms so customers can move more easily from the start of design to manufacturing.

The 17 companies announced include EDA leaders Cadence and Synopsys, as well as AION Silicon, GreatAsic Technology, HCLTech, Infinecs Systems, Infosys, MavissDesign, MosChip Technologies, NSW, Oppstar Technology, Quest Global Services, SkyeChip, TOPPAN, Toshiba Information Systems, Wipro, and others. Rapidus also indicated that it plans to expand the categories covered beyond design to include EDA, IP, and more. Rather than competing on the number of participants alone, this can be seen as a move to increase the design environments available to customers.Rapidus English announcement

Understanding 2nm and GAA for beginners

"2nm" does not mean that every part is literally 2 nanometers in size. It is a process node name that the semiconductor industry uses to label generations. As generations advance, chips are expected to fit more transistors in the same area and improve the balance between performance and power consumption.

The background technology Rapidus describes is GAA (Gate All Around). A transistor is a switch in which a gate controls the channel, the path electricity flows through. In conventional FinFETs, the gate surrounds a 3D fin-shaped channel on three sides. In GAA, on the other hand, the gate surrounds the channel on all four sides, which is expected to allow finer control of current and reduce leakage current, where electricity flows unintentionally.

IBM Research explains that its joint research with Rapidus dealt with nanosheet GAA, which stacks thin sheets of silicon. It is also researching manufacturing techniques such as selective layer thinning to achieve different threshold voltages, for example for low power and for high performance. Threshold voltage is the approximate voltage at which a transistor starts to conduct. A low threshold makes it easier to operate but makes balancing leakage current a challenge, while a high threshold makes it easier to save power but requires a trade-off with speed.IBM Research explainer

Why a design ecosystem matters

Building a semiconductor fab does not mean customers can make products right away. Customers first design circuits and verify them with design automation tools called EDA (Electronic Design Automation). Next, they integrate IP, which are functional blocks developed by other companies, and use a PDK (Process Design Kit) to check that the design fits that fab's manufacturing rules. A PDK is a set of rules, such as wire widths and electrical constraints, for designing for a specific process.

Without enough of these in place, the designed circuit cannot be manufactured correctly at the fab. Especially at advanced generations like 2nm, transistor structure, wiring, power delivery, and verification methods differ from earlier generations, so design companies also need specialized experience. CORE is significant because Rapidus is trying to increase not only its manufacturing equipment, but also its points of contact with the people and tools that help customers start designing.

Rapidus says it is running pilot production of 2nm GAA chips at its IIM facility in Hokkaido. It has also presented a plan to shorten development time by combining single-wafer processing and AI analysis of manufacturing data, linking design, the front-end process, and the back-end process. However, these are plans and business descriptions put forward by the company, and they are not data showing that CORE has achieved a measured reduction in time.Rapidus technology and business overview

What this announcement does not confirm

What the CORE announcement confirms is that 17 companies have stated their participation, and that Rapidus plans to strengthen collaboration from design to manufacturing. On the other hand, each company's specific role, contract terms, number of projects, service start dates, and details of supported PDKs and IP have not been revealed. Having participating companies does not mean mass production orders, product tape-outs, yield, performance, power consumption, or mass production capacity have been confirmed.

Also, this CORE announcement is not a new demonstration of the 2nm manufacturing process itself. The joint research by IBM and Rapidus shows background on nanosheet GAA and manufacturing technology, but this announcement does not prove mass production performance. So it would be premature to read it as "2nm mass production has succeeded because 17 companies have gathered."

Points to watch going forward

Going forward, what matters is which EDA flows, IP, PDKs, and design services the CORE companies will actually provide. If specific customer names, product areas, tape-out results, prototype chip performance, yield, and mass production contracts are published, it will be easier to judge whether the ecosystem is really working.

For Rapidus, a system that lets customers complete their designs and mass-produce stable products is as important as the technology that runs the fab. This news is a step that made that design-side foundation visible through the participation of 17 companies. From here, we need to check the announced plans separately from actual results in design, prototyping, and mass production.
