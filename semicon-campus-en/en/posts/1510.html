On September 29, 2026, MIPS announced a strategic, non-exclusive collaboration with Xcelsa Labs, a company specializing in semiconductor design optimization. The effort combines MIPS's workload-oriented processor IP with Xcelsa's "Apex Design Optimization" platform to explore design candidates for custom silicon aimed at AI, automotive, industrial, robotics, edge, and other uses. September 29, 2026 is the announcement date. It does not indicate when the design optimization case was carried out or when volume production starts. MIPS official announcement

What was announced?

The core of this collaboration is tuning the design of processors and logic blocks starting from specific software workloads, in other words, "what kinds of calculations are done most." According to MIPS, Apex combines verified intelligence and physical intelligence and aims to iterate through many design alternatives at AI speed. Rather than simply generating circuits automatically, it is described as a system that searches for candidates while watching performance and implementation conditions, then narrows down options that meet design constraints. MIPS official announcement Xcelsa official website

In a concrete example, MIPS says Apex fixed a critical timing-violation path in the load-store unit of one of its real designs in under 3 hours, cutting critical path delay by 33%. MIPS also says it formally proved that the rewritten design block was functionally equivalent to the reference design, and that the equivalent manual work would have taken about one engineer-month. However, these are figures from a single case announced by MIPS, not an independent third-party benchmark. MIPS official announcement

Technical terms made easy

Formal verification and functional equivalence

Formal verification checks a circuit's behavior using mathematical methods. In semiconductor design, when you change a circuit description such as RTL, there is a risk that calculation results or control behavior change under some conditions, even if you meant only to improve performance. Functional equivalence checking confirms, over a defined scope, whether the optimized design performs the same function as the reference design, without relying only on individual test cases. In this announcement, this idea is used as a guardrail to check whether design candidates found by AI keep the original function. MIPS official announcement

Timing, critical path, and PPA

Timing describes whether a circuit's signals travel within the set time and arrive in time for the next operation. The slowest signal path in a circuit is called the critical path, and shortening it may allow the whole design to run at a higher frequency. On the other hand, semiconductor evaluation looks not only at performance but also at power and area. These three together are called PPA: performance, power, and area. On its product page, MIPS promotes configurable RISC-V processors, custom instructions, and PPA optimization. But the 33% reduction in this announcement is in delay, and it does not mean power or area improved by the same amount. MIPS official product page MIPS official announcement

Background on RISC-V and workload specialization

The design philosophy of RISC-V is also important for understanding this story. RISC-V is an open instruction set architecture that lets you combine base integer instructions with standard and custom extensions. An instruction set architecture, or ISA, is the set of instruction rules visible to software. Because an ISA does not fix the specific circuit structure or manufacturing method, designers can consider internal structures and dedicated instructions for each use while keeping the same basic rules for software. Official RISC-V specification

For example, image recognition, robot control, and automotive sensor processing can repeat the same kinds of calculations. Adding dedicated instructions or accelerators suited to that processing may make it more efficient than a general-purpose setup. This is the idea of workload specialization. MIPS offers a lineup of RISC-V processor products such as the P8700, I8500, and S8200, and describes their configurability and features for Physical AI. This collaboration aims to refine such use-specific configurations efficiently through AI-driven candidate search and formal verification. MIPS official product page Official RISC-V specification

What this announcement means, and its limits

What is meaningful is that it tries not only to have AI propose design candidates, but also to link functional equivalence checking and timing improvement in implementation into the same flow. In semiconductor design, even if you find an option that raises performance, problems can arise: the function changes, the area grows, power gets worse, or it does not work under manufacturing conditions. If search and verification can be repeated, it may reduce the burden on designers of trying candidates one by one by hand. However, the collaboration is an announcement to support future design optimization. At the time of the announcement, shipments of volume-production chips, customer adoption, product schedules, and shortening of the entire design process had not been confirmed. MIPS official announcement Xcelsa official website

Also, the conditions behind the figures "under 3 hours," "33%," and "about one engineer-month" have not been disclosed, including the target block, clock constraints, manufacturing process, tools used, and the scope of the manual work compared. So we cannot conclude that every chip design will see the same effect, or that development time will shrink by the same ratio. In particular, even if timing improvement is confirmed, final power, area, yield, and software compatibility are not automatically guaranteed. These need to be checked through independent benchmarks with stated conditions or measurements after volume production. MIPS official announcement

What to watch next

  • Whether the number of design candidates explored by AI, the constraints given, and the conditions for comparison with existing EDA tools are disclosed.
  • Whether measured PPA values are shown, including power and area, not just timing.
  • Whether reproducibility is confirmed beyond a single real design case, across multiple process conditions and customer uses.
  • Whether customer designs, tape-outs, or volume shipments using the collaboration's technology are announced.

What can be confirmed this time is that MIPS and Xcelsa announced a collaboration, and that MIPS presented one real design case. Whether AI-driven semiconductor design optimization leads to real industrial impact should be judged by future independent verification, customer adoption, and volume production results.
