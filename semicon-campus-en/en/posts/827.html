What happened? Parts that power AI were announced

On September 7, 2026, Infineon announced the "TDA235E5" and "TDA235E0" power semiconductors for AI accelerators. An AI accelerator is a semiconductor that handles AI computation, and these products are "smart power stages" used in the circuits that supply power to it. According to the company, engineering samples for customer evaluation were available as of the announcement. Infineon product announcement

The key point is that the company claims support for peak currents of up to 300 A in a small 6×6×0.8mm package. However, what we can confirm is the availability of evaluation samples, not the start of mass production. September 7 is the announcement date. It does not indicate the first shipment date or when customers actually received parts. The mass-production start date and adopting customers cannot be confirmed from these materials. Announcement date and availability stage

How to read "6 mm square" and "300 A peak"

Both products integrate the company's "OptiMOS 6 MOSFET" and a two-phase driver IC. A MOSFET is a semiconductor switch that controls whether electricity flows or stops. The driver IC operates that switch. It is easiest to think of a power stage as the part that combines these components to handle power conversion. Product configuration

Two current values were published. One is a peak current of up to 300 A, and the other is a total design current of 120 A. Total design current corresponds to "total design current," or TDC, in the announcement. A 300 A peak does not mean 300 A can flow continuously. The 120 A figure also cannot be read as a continuous rating regardless of temperature or cooling conditions. Current specifications

Why does it matter? Shortening the "path of electricity" to the chip

When thinking about power delivery for AI, you need to look not only at power components but also at the path that carries power to the processor. The system that includes this path is the "power delivery network," or PDN. Infineon describes a design that reduces power lost in the delivery network by placing power modules near the processor. Besides the parts being small, it also matters that they can be placed where they are needed. Technical explanation of high-density power modules

"Vertical power delivery" is the idea of using an up-and-down arrangement to make the path shorter and more direct, as opposed to carrying power sideways across the board. Think of it as turning a roundabout path for electricity into a shortcut. The company says it can reduce losses in the delivery network and the resulting heat, and also reduce board area. However, this is the design aim. It does not mean the same improvement rate will be achieved in every implementation. Technical aims of vertical power delivery

The two new products are said to support both lateral and vertical power delivery. The significance of this announcement seems to be that components for designing high-density power delivery circuits have reached a stage where customers can evaluate them. It should be distinguished from an announcement proving faster processing or power savings in a finished AI system. Supported configurations and sample availability

Background: the 2025 collaboration with Delta

Vertical power delivery is not a technology appearing for the first time here. On August 28, 2025, Infineon announced a strengthened collaboration with Delta on developing vertical power delivery modules. That announcement said Delta would use Infineon's 90 A silicon power stages in its development. These are not the same parts as the two new products, but they show the background of efforts to shorten the power delivery path. Collaboration announcement from August 28, 2025

The difference between a "power stage" and a "power module" is also important here. In the company's explanation of high-density power modules, the configuration integrates inductors, capacitors, and other parts on a common board in addition to the power stage. The 6 mm square here is the outline of the power stage, which is one component of that setup. It does not mean the whole power delivery circuit, including all necessary parts, is 6 mm square. Thermal and density figures still need checking

Infineon says the way heat is conducted from the junction inside the semiconductor to the top of the package is advantageous for integration with liquid cooling. However, the announcement does not give thermal impedance values or comparison conditions. From this explanation alone, we cannot judge how many degrees cooler it runs than earlier products or how much it cuts cooling costs. Company explanation of thermal characteristics

The "over 2A/mm²" claimed by the company is an indicator of current per unit area. A is the unit of current, which is different from W/mm², which expresses power per unit area. Because the area and conditions used for the calculation cannot be confirmed, the relationship between this indicator, the 300 A peak, and the package size cannot be judged from these materials alone. What to watch next: mass production and real-world effects

Points to check going forward include how long and under what temperature and cooling conditions the 300 A peak can be handled, when mass production starts, which customers adopt it, and the detailed functional differences between the TDA235E5 and TDA235E0. Once these are clear, it will be possible to judge more concretely what kinds of power delivery circuits they can be used in. The current announcement is positioned as the entry point to that evaluation. Current product announcement

Another point to watch is the losses, temperatures, and power savings when the parts are built into actual power delivery circuits and AI systems.
