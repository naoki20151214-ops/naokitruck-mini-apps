What is a silicon wafer?

A silicon wafer is high-purity silicon shaped into a thin disk. In semiconductor manufacturing, tiny circuits are built on the surface of this wafer. In the back-end process, the finished wafer is cut into individual chips, which are put into packages to become the final semiconductor products.

SEMI describes silicon wafers as a basic semiconductor material and the substrate on which many semiconductor devices and chips are made. SUMCO also describes a silicon wafer as a thin, round disk cut from high-purity silicon, with its surface carefully polished and cleaned.

This is all you need to start

A wafer is "the white disk used to make semiconductor chips." The circuits are not finished yet. In the front-end process, hundreds of steps are layered on it to build transistors and wiring.

How are wafers made?

For beginners, it is easier to follow the flow below than to imagine sand turning straight into a disk.

1. Start with the silicon in quartz and sand

In nature, silicon exists as silica or quartz, bonded with oxygen. For semiconductors, it is processed into silicon of extremely high purity.

2. Make high-purity polycrystalline silicon

In semiconductors, even tiny amounts of impurities affect electrical properties. So impurities are cut down drastically at the material stage. Shin-Etsu Chemical describes the process of making extremely pure polycrystalline silicon for semiconductor-grade single-crystal wafers.

3. Grow a single-crystal ingot

Polycrystalline silicon is melted and grown into a single-crystal cylinder in which the atoms are neatly lined up. This cylinder is called an ingot.

4. Slice, polish, and clean

The ingot is sliced thin, and the surface is polished flat and cleaned. To build tiny circuits, a flat and clean surface is extremely important.

Why are wafers round?

Wafers are round because single-crystal ingots are made in the shape of a cylinder. When you slice a cylinder thin, you get round disks, like slices of a log.

The final chips, however, are square. Many copies of the same circuit pattern are laid out on the round wafer, and at the end it is cut into individual chips. Rather than cutting a pizza into squares, think of it as laying out square chip areas on a round canvas.

What happens on a wafer?

Once you understand wafers, the articles on the front-end process suddenly connect. The front-end process is where circuits are built on the wafer. Deposition creates thin layers, lithography transfers patterns, etching removes unneeded parts, cleaning removes contamination, and CMP makes the surface flat.

If the quality of the wafer itself is poor, circuit building will not be stable, no matter how advanced the equipment is. An uneven surface, contamination, crystal defects, or variations in thickness can all lead to defects and lower yield in later steps.

Companies involved with wafers

The companies that make wafers are not always the same as the companies that build circuits on them. Understanding this difference makes company research much easier.

Materials makersCompanies that make the silicon wafers themselves. SUMCO and the Shin-Etsu Chemical group are typical examples.
Device makers and foundriesCompanies that build circuits on wafers, such as TSMC, Rapidus, Kioxia, and Sony Semiconductor Solutions.
Equipment and inspection companiesCompanies that make equipment to process, measure, and inspect wafers. This includes lithography, deposition, etching, cleaning, and inspection equipment.

For example, SUMCO supports the semiconductor industry with high-quality silicon wafers. Shin-Etsu Chemical handles electronic materials, including silicon wafers for semiconductors. Rather than memorizing company names, it is easier to picture the work if you sort them by "Does it make wafers as a material?", "Does it build circuits on wafers?", or "Does it make equipment that processes and inspects wafers?"

There are substrates other than silicon wafers

Silicon wafers are the standard for semiconductors, but not every semiconductor is made from the same material. For power semiconductors that handle electrical power and for high-frequency uses, materials and substrates such as SiC, GaN, and GaAs may come up.

As a beginner, it is enough to remember that "silicon wafers are central for common logic, memory, sensors, and so on." Then, if you see words like SiC, GaN, or compound semiconductors in job postings or company research, you can expand your understanding: the role may involve power semiconductors or special applications.

Summary: Even AI chips start from the foundation of a wafer

A silicon wafer is a thin, round foundation for making semiconductor chips. The silicon in sand and quartz is purified, grown into a single-crystal ingot, sliced thin, polished, and cleaned to become a material ready for the front-end process.

Once you understand wafers, you can see "what" deposition, lithography, etching, cleaning, CMP, and inspection are done on. It also becomes easier to sort out the roles of materials makers such as SUMCO and Shin-Etsu Chemical, device makers such as TSMC and Rapidus, and inspection and equipment companies.

When you read job postings, pay attention to words like "wafer," "substrate," "materials evaluation," "defect inspection," and "yield improvement." The more you know about the technology, the easier it is to see the kinds of jobs available.
