On September 24, 2026, EdgeCortix announced RAIDEN, an AI chiplet platform for physical AI. Physical AI is AI that lets robots, vehicles, industrial machines, and similar systems process information from sensors and send decisions and control back into the real world. This announcement is not a shipping record for a mass-produced chip. It presents a new platform that can scale by combining multiple semiconductor dies, along with plans for future customer samples and mass production.EdgeCortix official announcement

What was announced

RAIDEN is designed to scale from the single-die X1 to the two-die X2 and the four-die X4. All versions use the same DNA-X accelerator architecture and MERA software stack. If the company's description holds, the goal is to let customers keep using their software even when the use case or scale changes. However, detailed specifications for the X1 and X2 will be announced in stages later.Announcement

For the top-end RAIDEN-X4, EdgeCortix lists maximum values of 3.36 PFLOPS of FP4 AI compute, 256 GB of memory, 548 GB/s of memory bandwidth, and 1.54 TB/s of die-to-die bandwidth. For chip-to-chip connections, the press release says up to 6.4 Tb/s, while the product page says up to 800 GB/s bidirectional. One byte is 8 bits, so 800 GB/s equals 6.4 Tb/s, and the two figures are consistent.RAIDEN product technology page

Chiplets and UCIe for beginners

What is a chiplet?

A chiplet design connects several small semiconductor dies inside one package so they work as a single system. Compared with putting compute, memory, I/O, and everything else on one huge die, it makes it easier to split functions and combine them. It may let designers add more compute or choose memory and connections to fit the use case.

On the other hand, connecting dies does not automatically mean higher performance. Latency and power in die-to-die communication, signal synchronization, heat, inspection, manufacturing yield, and software compatibility all become challenges. The RAIDEN product page describes a design that integrates four compute dies and combines LPDDR5X, a shared memory fabric, an on-package 64-bit RISC-V core, and more.EdgeCortix technical description

What is UCIe?

UCIe is an open specification for connecting chiplets inside a package. Put simply, it is a common set of rules that lets different dies talk to each other. The UCIe Consortium says it standardizes the die-to-die I/O physical layer, communication protocols, software stack, and interoperability. The aim is to make it easier to combine chiplets from different companies.UCIe Consortium overview UCIe specifications

RAIDEN is described as integrating four compute dies with a UCIe fabric. However, the available materials do not tell us which UCIe version RAIDEN follows, whether it is certified, or whether interconnection with other companies' chiplets has been shown on real hardware. "A design that uses UCIe" and "interoperability confirmed under the standard" do not mean the same thing.

What FP4 and PFLOPS mean

FP4 is a way of computing with a 4-bit floating-point format. Using fewer bits per number may let a chip handle many calculations efficiently, but depending on the model and workload, it must be balanced against accuracy. PFLOPS is a measure of how many quadrillion floating-point operations a chip can do per second.

So even though 3.36 PFLOPS is a big number, it does not mean ordinary applications will always run at that speed. It is the company's published maximum design value, based on FP4. Real performance depends on the AI model, data flow, memory access, software optimization, power, and temperature. The product page also mentions sparse computing and 2:4 sparsity, so be careful when comparing it directly with figures for dense matrix math.Product specifications

Why physical AI?

Generative AI in the cloud often runs huge models in data centers. In contrast, robots, vehicles, industrial machines, and aerospace equipment need to process sensor data on site, decide quickly, and feed the result back into control. Sending all data over a network can cause problems with latency, communication costs, privacy, and weakness against lost connections. That is why edge AI, which runs inference close to the site, depends not only on compute performance but also on memory capacity, bandwidth, connections, and power.

RAIDEN points toward handling a continuous processing loop on one scalable base, including sensor processing and application processing in addition to AI compute. However, these materials alone do not tell us which AI models it can run at how many watts, or how stable it is and how much latency it has during long-term operation.

EdgeCortix says Kawasaki Heavy Industries has selected a RAIDEN-based solution for next-generation AI-enabled aerospace and defense products, and that Unigen is developing a RAIDEN-based server platform. These are customer programs announced by EdgeCortix. They do not mean that contract values, specific product model numbers, shipping dates, or independent performance evaluations by the customers have been confirmed.Official announcement

Links to semiconductor development in Japan

EdgeCortix says RAIDEN uses results from NEDO's "Research and Development Project of the Enhanced Infrastructures for Post-5G Information and Communication Systems (JPNP20017)." NEDO positions this project as R&D that includes strengthening Japan's domestic base, such as core technologies for post-5G and semiconductor design, packaging, and manufacturing technologies. It can be seen as an example of a fabless company turning public R&D results into a product platform.NEDO project description

However, a link to a NEDO project alone does not guarantee that RAIDEN will succeed in mass production or win market share. For chiplet products, even after the design is done, package manufacturing, testing, the supply chain, software support, and integration into customer equipment all need to come together.

From physical AI to public documents and a practice setup

After reading the RAIDEN announcement, make a comparison table that separates the workload, power, communication, and evaluation conditions. Rereading the English public documents and trying small image-processing tasks at home are two different ways to deepen your understanding. Choose a practice PC based on the requirements of the software you will use, and do not treat a general PC as a substitute for a dedicated chiplet. If you use generative AI to organize public information, check the numbers and sources in its output.

Notes to check before comparing: the GPUs and VRAM supported by your image-processing software, CPU and main memory, OS, and budget. Separate what you can try with your current setup from what needs an upgrade, and then pick candidates.

Separate announcements from results

The core of this news is not the track record of a mass-produced product, but a new platform with customer programs and a supply plan. EdgeCortix plans customer samples in early 2027 and mass production in late 2027. These are future plans, not facts showing that samples have shipped or mass production has started.Japanese press release

Also, figures such as 3.36 PFLOPS, 256 GB, and 1.54 TB/s are specifications published by the company. These materials do not confirm independent benchmarks, power efficiency, thermal design power, manufacturing yield, or the performance of mass-produced units. When evaluating it, look not only at peak performance but also at latency with real AI models, work done per watt, and stability during long-term operation.

What to watch next

  • Whether the customer samples planned for early 2027 actually ship.
  • Whether the late-2027 mass production plan holds, and whether yield and supply capacity for production units are shown.
  • Whether performance, latency, and power with physical AI models are published, not just the FP4 maximum.
  • Whether RAIDEN's supported UCIe version, certification status, and interconnection with other companies' chiplets are confirmed.
  • Whether Kawasaki Heavy Industries or Unigen announce details on the products using it, shipping dates, and uses.

RAIDEN shows a design that tries to scale not just compute, but chiplets, memory, connections, and software together. It is notable that a Japanese fabless company has taken this direction for physical AI that makes decisions on site. On the other hand, at this stage it is mostly announcements and plans. Going forward, it is important to look at real samples, independent benchmarks, mass production results, and confirmation from customers, in that order.
