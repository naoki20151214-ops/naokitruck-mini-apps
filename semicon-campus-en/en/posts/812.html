What happened? Equipment sales for April–June hit a record

On September 3, 2026, SEMI announced that global semiconductor equipment sales for the second quarter of 2026, that is, April–June, were $40.53 billion. That is up 23% year over year and 11% quarter over quarter, and SEMI says it set a record for the second quarter in a row. The news came out in September, but it does not cover September sales. SEMI's results announcement

"Year over year" compares with April–June 2025, and "quarter over quarter" compares with January–March 2026. This time, sales rose both compared with a year earlier and compared with the previous three months. However, 23% and 11% use different comparison periods, so they should not be added together to get a growth rate. Comparison periods and published figures

What do "manufacturing equipment" and "sales" mean?

Semiconductor manufacturing equipment is the machinery and tools used to make semiconductors. This is a different market from sales of finished semiconductors themselves. This news shows not "how much chips sold" but "how much the equipment used to make chips sold." It helps to think of it as an indicator that looks at efforts to build manufacturing capacity from the equipment market side. SEMI's statistics and forecast definitions

The data comes from WWSEMS, a monthly statistic that covers global semiconductor equipment sales by region and equipment category. This time, it was published as quarterly results. Data for companies headquartered in Japan is provided through SEAJ, the Semiconductor Equipment Association of Japan. A third-party financial firm collects the participating companies' information, which keeps individual company data confidential. How the statistics are compiled, WWSEMS definitions

Another important point is the difference between sales and orders. Orders show that an order was received, and they are not the same number as the sales results published this time. WWSEMS sales data started in 1991, and order data was collected from 1997 to 2016. If you read the $40.53 billion as "the value of new orders received," you change the meaning of the statistic. History of sales and order data

Why does it matter? Investment is flowing to the manufacturing side that supports AI

SEMI explained that the record was driven by continued investment in advanced manufacturing capacity to support demand for AI infrastructure. What stands out here is the view that AI-related demand is also linked to the market for equipment used to make semiconductors. However, this should be read as SEMI's own analysis of the drivers, as the organization making the announcement. SEMI's explanation of the growth drivers

Looking back, global semiconductor equipment sales for full-year 2025, announced on April 7, 2026, were $135.1 billion, up 15% from the previous year. SEMI then also cited capacity expansion for leading-edge logic, memory, and AI as drivers. This increase fits into a trend in which the equipment market was already growing the year before. Full-year 2025 results

In technical terms, logic refers to semiconductors that handle computation and control, and memory refers to semiconductors that store data. It is easy to think of them as the computing role and the information-holding role. SEMI's explanation the previous year mentioned both as drivers of equipment investment. On the other hand, the annual $135.1 billion and this quarter's $40.53 billion cover different periods, so you cannot compare them directly as an increase or decrease. Sales results do not mean a fab is finished or volume production has started

What was announced this time is past equipment sales results. It is not an announcement of future investment plans or sales forecasts. SEMI also treats WWSEMS, which tallies equipment sales; equipment sales forecasts, which use manufacturers' outlooks and other inputs; and the World Fab Forecast, which tracks construction and capital investment by fab, as separate sources. You need to tell results and outlooks apart by the type of source. How results, forecasts, and fab investment are categorized

For example, "equipment was sold," "fab investment was planned," and "a fab was completed and started volume production" are each separate facts. Fab investment also involves construction and more, so the $40.53 billion cannot be restated as total investment in the world's semiconductor fabs. This sales statistic alone cannot confirm how much chip supply will increase in the future or when that production will start. Scope of the sources that track fab investment

Likewise, the explanation that "AI is a driver" does not mean that all of the increase was for AI. This total and SEMI's explanation alone do not tell us the sales value for AI uses or AI's contribution to growth. Even if an overall rise in market sales can be confirmed, it should be kept separate from independent proof that measures AI's effect in numbers. Details of this announcement

What it means for Japan, and what to check next

Japanese companies also provide data through SEAJ, so this statistic is directly connected to Japan's equipment industry. However, a 23% increase worldwide does not mean Japanese companies' sales all rose by 23%. The global total, the Japanese market, and individual company results need to be checked separately. How Japanese companies provide data

In the next announcement, the points to check are whether the same sales statistic keeps rising and how the breakdown by region and equipment type moves. If you want to know about expansion of manufacturing capacity, you need to look at the progress of fab projects separately from equipment sales. When you see manufacturers' outlooks, too, telling apart forecasts, completed sales results, and information confirming the start of volume production makes it easier to grasp the meaning of AI-related news.
