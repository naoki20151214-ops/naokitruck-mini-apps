What happened? Global sales reached $146.8 billion

On September 4, 2026, the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) announced that global semiconductor sales for July 2026 were $146.8 billion, up 135.1% from the same month a year earlier and up 6.4% from the previous month. A 135.1% increase means about 2.35 times the previous year. However, this is a statistic on sales value. It does not announce that the number of semiconductors produced grew by the same factor. SIA's September 4 announcement

The news was published in September, but the statistic covers July. In addition, the published figure is a three-month moving average of May, June, and July. It is neither July's sales alone nor the total for the three months. This news is not about a capital investment plan or the start of mass production at a fab. It is the release of statistics summarizing past sales. SIA's attached statistics table

The Japan market also grew. Read growth rate and market size separately

According to the attached table, sales in the Japan market were $5.52 billion, up 50.8% year over year and up 9.2% month over month. All five listed regions grew both year over year and month over month, and Japan had the highest month-over-month growth rate of the five. The notable point is that sales value grew not only worldwide but also in the regional category of Japan. Statistics table showing regional figures

Also, the regional category "Japan" cannot be swapped for the global sales of Japanese companies or the output value of fabs in Japan. These materials alone do not settle the detailed accounting basis for each region. So we cannot conclude that "sales of all Japanese companies rose 50.8%" or that "fabs in Japan increased output by 9.2%." SIA attached statistics table

Key terms: three-month moving average and year over year

A three-month moving average adds the figures for three consecutive months and divides by three. For this July figure, sales for May, June, and July are averaged. The next month, the window shifts by one month, which is why it is called "moving." It is a number for smoothing out month-to-month swings to read the trend, and WSTS historical materials also treat monthly sales and three-month moving averages separately. WSTS explanation of historical statistics

The June figure covers April to June, and the July figure covers May to July, so May and June overlap. That is why the 6.4% month-over-month increase does not mean "July sales alone were 6.4% higher than June sales alone." Remembering that this compares averages is the key to not misreading short-term changes. Statistics table showing the averaging periods

Year over year compares with the same month of the previous year, and month over month compares with the month just before. Both compare published averages here. For example, if last year's value is 100, a 135.1% increase brings it to 235.1. Year-over-year and month-over-month comparisons start from different points, so a 135.1% increase and a 6.4% increase do not contradict each other. SIA's published figures

Background on the statistics: who collects them, and what do they measure?

WSTS, which compiles the data, is a nonprofit organization that handles semiconductor shipment statistics and market forecasts. Member companies submit monthly sales data, and WSTS produces the statistics. This SIA announcement is based on that data. It is not a case of SIA and WSTS measuring the market separately and two independent surveys agreeing. The attached table also presents the same statistics in more detail. WSTS organization overview, SIA announcement

WSTS offers about 40 years of historical statistics, a body of data for following the semiconductor market over a long time frame. Those historical materials also distinguish single-month values from averages. When comparing with past markets, you need to check not only how big the numbers are but also whether you are lining up the same kind of indicator. WSTS Historical Billings Report

Why does it matter? Market growth and AI's contribution are separate questions

What we can read from these statistics is that growth in sales value is shared by the world as a whole and by each listed region. It is meaningful because it lets us check whether growth is limited to some regions or spreads across several. On the other hand, this data alone cannot tell us each company's profits or how busy their fabs are. Global and regional sales statistics

When reading sales value, it is easier to understand if you think about sales volume, price, and product mix separately. Product mix is the combination of products sold. As an example of the idea, even if the number of units sold stays the same, sales value rises if expensive products make up a larger share. However, from these totals, we cannot break down how much volume, price, and product mix each contributed to the increase. Statistics table showing the scope of this data

It is a number that draws attention as a market trend in the AI era, but these materials have no breakdown that identifies what share of the increase came from AI demand. There is not enough evidence to state causes such as "AI demand produced the 135.1% increase" or "Japan's growth is the result of domestic investment policy." We need to read the fact of overall market growth separately from hypotheses about its causes. SIA announcement, Attached statistics table

Differences from past announcements, and what to watch next

There is a difference in numbers from a past announcement that needs attention. In SIA's August 6, 2026 announcement, June global sales were $134.5 billion, but in the table attached to the September 4 announcement, the June comparison value is $137.91 billion. If you simply connect the numbers from the two announcements, you cannot reproduce this month's 6.4% month-over-month figure. The announcement text we reviewed does not explain the difference, so we should not conclude it is due to a revision. When reading this month-over-month figure, we need to use the comparison value in the latest table. August 6 announcement, Table attached to the September 4 announcement

Next, we want to check whether growth continues in future sales statistics, whether an explanation is given for the difference in the June value, and whether information by application or product can confirm the background of the growth. These numbers are a summary of actual sales, not a company's future plans or growth forecasts. They also do not prove full-year growth or mass production at any particular fab, so it is important to read later announcements with the averaging period and comparison values lined up.
