On September 21, 2026, imec, a research institute for semiconductors and optical communications, announced a germanium/silicon (Ge/Si) avalanche photodiode (APD) made on a 300mm silicon photonics platform. According to the official announcement, this APD has a 100GHz bandwidth, operates at 5V, and has a responsivity of 1.8A/W in both the O-band and the C-band. It reportedly showed a net 400Gbps optical link using an APD receiver and a 3dB improvement in receiver sensitivity. The results are scheduled to be presented at ECOC 2026 on September 24. In other words, September 24 is the scheduled presentation date at the event, not the date the news was released.imec official announcement ECOC official program

What is new

The key point is not just a faster single photodetector. imec combined a silicon photonics manufacturing platform using 300mm wafers, 100GHz-class bandwidth, a low 5V operating voltage, and better receiver sensitivity from the APD. It also showed a net data rate of 400Gbps for an optical link.

However, "demonstrated" here does not mean that products for data centers are already in mass production. This is a research result announced by imec. The timing of commercialization, the start of mass production, and the power consumption of a real full system are not yet fixed. For the 400Gbps link, the official release text alone does not confirm the modulation format, transmission distance, bit error rate (BER), forward error correction (FEC), optical input power, or details of the receiver electronics.

What is an APD?

A photodiode is a part that converts light into an electrical signal. An APD has a mechanism inside called "avalanche multiplication." Electrons and holes created by incoming light are accelerated by a strong electric field and create more charges, which amplifies the original signal internally.

This amplification can make the signal larger for the receiver and may improve the receiver sensitivity and link margin of an optical link. Link margin is the headroom that keeps the signal quality needed for reception, even when the transmitted light suffers losses along the way or parts vary. More headroom may allow lower laser output or setups with higher optical loss, but the actual power savings depend on the system design.

On the other hand, APD amplification is not always an advantage. Challenges include noise from multiplication, changes in characteristics with temperature, tolerance to strong incoming light, long-term reliability, and electrical matching with the transimpedance amplifier (TIA) that follows. A TIA is an amplifier circuit that converts the small current from a photodiode into a voltage signal.

Background on Ge/Si and silicon photonics

A Ge/Si APD combines germanium, which absorbs light, with silicon, which handles charge multiplication. Silicon alone has trouble efficiently detecting light at communication wavelengths, so the idea is to build germanium, which is well suited to light detection, into a silicon photonics platform.

Silicon photonics is a technology that builds optical waveguides, which are paths for light, inside silicon and integrates splitting, modulation, combining, and detection of light. Passive parts such as wavelength multiplexing filters and active parts such as modulators and Ge photodetectors can be placed on the same platform. Electronic circuits such as drivers and TIAs can also be made on a separate wafer and connected by flip-chip bonding.imec's explanation of silicon photonics

Being able to use 300mm wafers also matters. In general, large-diameter wafers let you process many chips from one wafer and may let you use the know-how of semiconductor manufacturing equipment and process control. So it points toward moving optical parts from small-volume lab production closer to larger-scale manufacturing. However, wafer size alone does not determine yield, cost, assembly, inspection, or suitability for mass production.

Why it gets attention for AI data centers

AI training and inference move huge amounts of data between accelerators, servers, and racks. When electrical wiring alone handles fast, long-distance communication, signal loss and power consumption tend to become problems, so optical interconnects are becoming more important inside data centers.

100GHz-class bandwidth relates to the headroom for receiving fast optical signals. Combined with the APD's better sensitivity, it can help aim for higher data rates on the same link or add headroom against optical loss. Low-voltage operation also relates to the power design of the receiver. However, even if only the photodetector runs at 5V, the power consumption of the whole transceiver, including the laser, TIA, driver, cooling, and signal processing, will not necessarily drop by the same ratio.

How it differs from past research, and cautions

A peer-reviewed paper in the Journal of Lightwave Technology published on March 3, 2026, reported a waveguide-coupled Ge/Si APD on 300mm SOI wafers with a peak 3dB bandwidth above 100GHz. It also reportedly maintained 70GHz bandwidth in the O-band and C-band at a responsivity of 2A/W, measured at a reverse bias of 7V.Paper in the Journal of Lightwave Technology

The figures in this announcement (1.8A/W, 5V, 100GHz) and those in the paper (2A/W, 7V, above 100GHz) may come from different measurement conditions, samples, or device generations. So they cannot be treated as numbers comparing the same sample under the same conditions. The paper is a peer-reviewed technical report, but it does not explain all the details of this 400Gbps link demo.

What to check going forward

First, watch how much the September 24 ECOC presentation reveals about the conditions for 400Gbps. If transmission distance, modulation format, BER, FEC, optical input power, temperature conditions, and receiver electronics setup are disclosed, it becomes easier to link single-device performance to its value in a real optical communication system.

Next, reliability across temperature ranges, changes in optical input, and long-term operation is important. imec itself names wider evaluations of temperature and optical input conditions, and integration with high-speed receiver electronics, as future tasks. For an APD to move from a research demo to a product, it must be checked as a whole, not just by a one-time peak value, but also by variation, yield, performance after assembly, power consumption, and cost.

This news is a technical result that tries to link faster, more sensitive photodetectors for AI data center optical communications to a 300mm manufacturing platform. On the other hand, it is not an announcement of mass production or commercial launch. Going forward, it is important to judge how far it is from practical use by looking at the measurement conditions added at ECOC and data on temperature, reliability, and electronics integration.
