On October 5, 2026, Skyworks Solutions announced that it had completed its combination with Qorvo. This means a deal worth about $22 billion, whose agreement was announced on October 28, 2025, has closed after the required procedures. This news is an industry reshuffle that brings together RF semiconductors used in wireless devices such as smartphones, GaN for high-power communications, power management, and analog technology in one company.Skyworks announcement of combination completion

What happened: from agreement to completion

In time order, the combination agreement was announced on October 28, 2025, and completion was announced, and took effect, on October 5, 2026. So the 2025 agreement announcement and the October 5, 2026 completion announcement are separate events. The figure of about $22 billion given in 2025 was the enterprise value at the time of the agreement announcement. It does not represent market capitalization or enterprise value as of October 5, 2026.2025 combination agreement announcement

Skyworks says the combined company will keep the name Skyworks and the NASDAQ ticker SWKS. As deal terms, Qorvo shareholders were offered $32.50 in cash and 0.960 Skyworks shares per share. On a fully diluted basis, former Skyworks shareholders own about 63% and former Qorvo shareholders about 37%. This is a description of the deal terms, not a result showing business performance after the combination.Deal description at completion

What is an RF front end?

RF means radio frequency. Smartphones, base stations, radar, and similar devices need circuits that amplify weak signals received by the antenna, pass only the needed frequencies, and switch between transmit and receive paths. The part between the antenna and the transmit/receive circuits that handles amplification, switching, filtering, and so on is called the RF front end.

Filters select the needed frequency bands from many radio waves, amplifiers strengthen signals, and switches change signal paths. Qorvo has offered amplifiers, filters, front-end modules, power management, switches, and more, using semiconductor materials such as GaAs, GaN, and Si, and filter technologies that use acoustic waves, called BAW and SAW.Qorvo product and technology list

The more frequency bands a wireless device supports, the more complex its design becomes. This is because miniaturization of parts, power consumption, heat, and signal quality must all be managed at once. In defense, aerospace, and telecom infrastructure, high power, environmental durability, and long-term reliability matter even more.

Why GaN and power/analog technology matter

GaN is a semiconductor material that uses gallium nitride. It can handle high power while operating at high frequencies, and is drawing attention for high-power RF uses such as base stations, defense radar, and satellite communications. On the other hand, higher power means more heat. If heat cannot be removed, it leads to lower performance and reliability problems, so materials, transistor structure, manufacturing process, and packaging must be designed together.

On September 22, 2026, Qorvo announced that, in connection with the DARPA THREADS program, it had increased the RF power density of X-band GaN transistors by more than 400%. The company says it developed materials, structures, and processes that lower thermal resistance. However, this figure is a company announcement by Qorvo. Within the materials checked this time, third-party reproduction tests or peer-reviewed evaluations could not be confirmed.Qorvo GaN technology announcement

Power management semiconductors are components that stably supply the voltage and current each circuit in a device needs. In battery-powered devices they reduce power consumption, and in data centers and telecom equipment they efficiently convert large amounts of power. Analog semiconductors handle continuous real-world signals such as temperature, light, sound, and voltage, while mixed-signal semiconductors connect analog signals and digital processing.

The strategic meaning of the combination

This combination is expected to make it possible to offer solutions that combine RF front ends, high-power GaN RF, power management, and analog/mixed-signal technology. From the customer's point of view, it may become easier to source multiple functions from one company. It could also lead to offerings that span the markets both companies have served, such as mobile, defense and aerospace, telecom infrastructure, automotive, data centers, and edge IoT.

However, this is an interpretation of the strategic meaning of the combination. Completion does not mean that all products have been merged into one product lineup, that customers have decided to adopt them, or that revenue and profit have increased. Restructuring of manufacturing sites, integration of product roadmaps, consolidation of overlapping businesses, and changes in sales by customer also cannot be confirmed from these materials alone.

Separate the company's plans from confirmed results

Skyworks expects to achieve annual cost synergies of $500 million or more within 24 to 36 months after completion, and projects that non-GAAP earnings per share will increase immediately. However, these are company plans and forecasts, not post-completion results. The amount and timing achieved may change depending on integration costs, costs of restructuring staff and sites, product development costs, and when customers switch.Company outlook at completion

When reading the news, it is important to separate the confirmed fact that "the combination is complete" from future outlooks such as "synergies will be achieved" and "profits will increase." For GaN performance improvements as well, company announcements need to be distinguished from verification by independent organizations.

Points to watch going forward

  • How revenue, profit, integration costs, and realized synergies are disclosed in post-combination financial results.
  • Whether sales outside mobile, such as defense, telecom infrastructure, automotive, and data centers, actually grow.
  • Whether new products or customer adoptions combining RF, GaN, power management, and analog products are announced.
  • Whether policies on manufacturing sites, R&D structure, and consolidation of overlapping products and businesses are made clear.
  • Whether independent evaluations or practical results in mass-produced products can be confirmed for GaN performance.

This completed combination is notable not only because the company has grown larger, but also because it creates a company that broadly handles signal processing and power control for wireless communications. On the other hand, judging the real results requires following future financial results, products, customer adoptions, and mass production results, not the announced vision.
