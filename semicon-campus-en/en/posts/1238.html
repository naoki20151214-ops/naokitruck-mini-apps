On September 24, 2026, Mitsubishi Electric announced that its US subsidiary, Mitsubishi Electric Power Products (MEPPI), had released a "Chip-to-Grid DSX reference design" that supports NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 and other systems. This is a model for designing not just the AI chips but everything from the power grid to the data center racks and cooling equipment as one system. Mitsubishi Electric's announcement

The first important point is that the news we can confirm is "the release of a design" and "a direction for future solution development." It does not announce installation at a specific customer facility, commercial operation, or completion of mass-production equipment. We need to separate the stage where blueprints and concepts are shown from the stage where something is actually built and running.

What was announced?

Chip-to-Grid is, literally, an approach that covers everything "from the chip to the grid." Mitsubishi Electric's design treats grid connection, power transmission and distribution, power supply to the chips, energy storage, power distribution inside the rack, and cooling from the rack up to the whole facility as a single design scope. The announcement shows on-site power generation, BESS, microgrids, 415/480VAC and 800VDC power distribution, and dual-loop cooling. Mitsubishi Electric announcement materials

BESS stands for "Battery Energy Storage System." It stores electricity in batteries and supports operation during changes in power demand and during outages. A microgrid is a system that normally works together with the power grid but, during an outage or similar event, can supply power on its own within the facility using generators and batteries. AI data centers need not only computers but also equipment that delivers power reliably.

Why look at power and cooling in the Vera Rubin era?

According to NVIDIA's product information, Vera Rubin NVL72 is a rack-scale AI platform that combines 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs. Its configuration also includes connectivity and networking elements such as NVLink Switch, ConnectX-9, and BlueField-4. However, NVIDIA describes the specifications on its product page as preliminary and states clearly that they may change in the future. NVIDIA DGX Vera Rubin NVL72

A point to note here is that better AI performance does not always rise in simple proportion with power use and heat. In semiconductor and system design, improving power efficiency while raising performance is also a goal. On the other hand, depending on the number and density of compute and networking equipment in a rack and the workloads, the facility side must carefully design power capacity, load fluctuations, and heat-handling capacity. We need to look not only at higher performance but also at efficiency, configuration, and operating conditions together.

Liquid cooling uses liquid to carry heat away from chips, compared with air cooling, which circulates air with fans. The dual-loop cooling Mitsubishi Electric describes combines direct liquid cooling for high-heat chips with air cooling for the other parts. Instead of liquid-cooling everything, the idea is to choose the cooling method for each part based on its heat conditions. Mitsubishi Electric's description of the cooling design

What does 800VDC mean?

800VDC is a candidate power distribution method inside data centers. AC means alternating current, and DC means direct current. The announcement treats both conventional 415/480VAC and 800VDC as design options. This does not mean that all AI data centers will move to 800VDC because of this announcement.

The choice of power distribution method, including 800VDC, depends on safety measures, protective devices, equipment standards, maintenance methods, utility interconnection requirements, facility configuration, and more. MEPPI's FAQ also describes the Chip-to-Grid design not as a fixed finished product but as a starting point to be adjusted to local regulations, standards, utility requirements, and site conditions. It may combine current products, technologies in development, and future concepts, and it is not a document that shows the configuration of a real project as is. MEPPI FAQ

The link to NVIDIA's DSX

NVIDIA describes DSX as a set of technologies for handling the chips, thermals, systems, and software of an AI factory in an integrated way. It is an approach that designs an AI data center not as a collection of individual servers but as a system that gets the most compute performance and operating efficiency out of a limited power budget. Related documents include facility designs for Vera Rubin NVL72, a BESS compatibility guide, and power orchestration that responds to signals from the grid. NVIDIA DSX Documentation

NVIDIA's technical articles also describe NVL72 as a rack-scale execution domain that integrates compute, networking, liquid cooling, power control, and energy storage. Mitsubishi Electric's Chip-to-Grid design can be seen as a proposal to connect this rack-side concept to facility-side equipment such as power transmission and distribution, power conversion, energy storage, and cooling. However, this view is an analysis that combines official materials, and it does not mean Mitsubishi Electric's design is already running in the field. NVIDIA Developer Blog

What we can confirm, and what we still don't know

The confirmed fact is that on September 24, 2026, Mitsubishi Electric released a design model for Vera Rubin NVL72 and future NVIDIA AI infrastructure. The company says it plans to use this design for future solution development and global business expansion. This is the company's plan and does not show orders, commercial installations, or operating results. Mitsubishi Electric's announcement

These materials do not let us confirm specific customers, construction sites, equipment capacity, price, delivery dates, or power efficiency and cooling performance in real operation. We also cannot confirm that on-site generation, BESS, 800VDC, and dual-loop cooling will all be adopted together at the same facility.

Also, NVIDIA's first preview submission for MLPerf Inference v6.1, announced on September 16, 2026, is the company's benchmark information on Vera Rubin NVL72. It is not evidence that Mitsubishi Electric's design has been installed or is running. The performance figures are also based on NVIDIA's submission and explanation, and they do not prove the real-world performance of Mitsubishi Electric's design. NVIDIA's MLPerf update

What to watch next

  • Whether Mitsubishi Electric's design is adopted in real customer projects or construction plans.
  • In which regions, sizes, and power conditions 800VDC or conventional AC is chosen.
  • How BESS and microgrids are used for load swings from AI processing and during outages.
  • How the maintainability of liquid cooling equipment, management of water and coolant, and long-term operating costs are evaluated.
  • How the design conditions change with Vera Rubin's final specifications and real operating data.

Competition in AI semiconductors is spreading beyond chip compute performance to the whole data center: securing power, removing heat, and running equipment reliably. This announcement matters because a Japanese company has shown an integrated design that includes power, energy storage, distribution, cooling, and control. On the other hand, between releasing a design and actually installing equipment, there are still stages for considering regulations, investment decisions, grid capacity, and customers' operating conditions.
