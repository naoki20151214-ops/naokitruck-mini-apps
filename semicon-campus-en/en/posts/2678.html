Fabs that make cutting-edge semiconductors rely on one machine they cannot do without. That machine is made by ASML, a company in the Netherlands. ASML began in 1984, in a leaky shed.

The small company was once pushed to the brink of collapse. Even so, in about 40 years, it became a company that builds the equipment supporting chipmaking. This article walks through ASML's history and how a special light tool called "EUV" came to be.

The official events and dates in this article are based on ASML's official pages and press releases published by ASML. The photos are AI-generated illustrations, not real places or people.

ASML makes machines that print circuit patterns

ASML's official page explains that ASML makes lithography (exposure) machines, and that these machines are an essential part of chipmaking. ASML's tools use light to print the extremely fine patterns of the circuits inside modern chips. ASML does not make the chips themselves. It makes the machines that print circuits onto chips.

Semiconductor chips are made on a wafer, a thin disk of silicon. The process of printing a circuit pattern onto the wafer with light is called lithography (exposure). The machine that does this is a lithography tool. It is a little like printing an image onto photographic film.

Quick quiz: Does ASML make chips, or machines that make chips?

Answer: Machines that make chips. It makes lithography tools that print circuits.

1984, in a leaky shed

According to ASML's official history page, in 1984 the electronics giant Philips and the semiconductor equipment company ASMI created a new company. Its name was ASM Lithography. Its home was a leaky shed next to a Philips office in Eindhoven, the Netherlands. In the same year, it released its first product, a lithography tool called the PAS 2000.

In 1985, with about 100 employees, the company moved to a newly built office and factory in Veldhoven. In 1986, it launched the PAS 2500 and formed a partnership with lens maker Carl Zeiss. ASML started out from a leaky shed as a lithography tool company.

A survival crisis and a last loan

However, in a fiercely competitive market with many suppliers, the small, unknown company struggled to win customers. According to ASML's official history page, in the late 1980s, its investor ASMI could not keep investing and decided to pull out. The whole electronics industry was in a slump, and Philips also announced major cost cuts. The page explains that the company's survival hung by a thread.

At this point, Philips board member Henk Bodt persuaded the other board members to approve a last loan. ASML then released a tool called the PAS 5500. With high productivity and resolution, this tool attracted the key customers the company needed to make a profit. In 1995, ASML listed in Amsterdam and New York and became a fully independent company. The last loan and the PAS 5500 brought ASML back on its feet.

Quick quiz: After its survival was in doubt, which tool supported ASML?

Answer: The PAS 5500, released in the 1990s.

Two stages, and immersion using water

According to ASML's official history page, in 2001 ASML released a tool called TWINSCAN. It has two stages that hold wafers: while one wafer is being exposed, the next wafer is measured and aligned. Less waiting time means higher productivity and accuracy.

In 2003, the first immersion tool appeared. Immersion is a method that passes light through a layer of water between the lens and the wafer. This makes even smaller circuit shapes possible. In 2006, the first immersion tool for mass production, the XT:1700i, came out. Immersion is a method that puts water between the lens and the wafer.

Quick quiz: In immersion, what is placed between the lens and the wafer?

Answer: A layer of water.

EUV: how the light is made, and a roughly 10-year challenge

The next barrier was EUV (extreme ultraviolet). According to TRUMPF's official page, ASML, Zeiss, and TRUMPF worked together to develop technology for producing EUV light with a wavelength of 13.5 nanometers on an industrial scale. Inside a vacuum chamber, 50,000 tiny droplets of tin are fired every second, and each one is hit with a laser pulse that turns it into plasma. This gives off EUV light.

According to an Electro Optics article (2015), all matter, including air, absorbs EUV. That is why this process takes place in a vacuum. The light is guided to the wafer not through lenses but through a series of mirrors arranged in a vacuum.

Key points of the figure

  • A laser hits droplets of tin
  • The plasma gives off EUV light
  • Everything happens in a vacuum

According to ASML's official history page, the first EUV prototype, the NXE:3100, shipped in 2010. In 2013, ASML acquired the light source company Cymer to speed up EUV development. In 2016, customers began placing bulk orders for the NXE:3400, the first model for mass production. Then, in early 2020, EUV entered mass production, and EUV tool shipments reached 100 units. That means it took about 10 years from the first prototype shipment to mass production. EUV took about 10 years to go from prototype to mass production.

Quick quiz: Why is the EUV light equipment placed in a vacuum?

Answer: Because all matter, including air, absorbs EUV.

The 2012 decision: customers invest

Developing EUV required a lot of money. According to ASML's 2012 press release, in July 2012 ASML announced a "Customer Co-Investment Program," in which its chipmaker customers would invest in development. Under this program, Intel, TSMC, and Samsung would fund research and development for next-generation lithography, especially EUV. The target for R&D funding was €1.38 billion in total, and Samsung agreed to contribute €276 million over five years.

In addition, the three companies together acquired about 23% of ASML's shares for €3.85 billion. Except in special cases, these shares carry no voting rights. It was an unusual 2012 arrangement in which the users of the tools invested in the toolmaker's development.

Key points of the figure

  • Intel, TSMC, and Samsung invested
  • Development funding for next-generation lithography, especially EUV
  • The users supported the maker
Quick quiz: Which three customers funded ASML's development in 2012?

Answer: Intel, TSMC, and Samsung.

And on to High NA

According to ASML's official history page, in 2023 ASML shipped the first unit of its next-generation EUV tool. Called "High NA," it raises the numerical aperture (NA) to 0.55, and the model name is EXE. It has a new optical design and much faster stages. Key terms review

  • Lithography (exposure): The process of printing a circuit pattern onto a wafer with light.
  • Lithography tool: The machine that performs lithography.
  • Wafer: A thin disk of silicon that serves as the base material for semiconductors.
  • Immersion: A method that creates a layer of water between the lens and the wafer.
  • EUV: Extreme ultraviolet light with a wavelength of 13.5 nanometers.
  • High NA: A next-generation EUV tool with the numerical aperture raised to 0.55.

Common misconceptions

Q. Is ASML a company that makes chips?

No. ASML makes lithography tools and other equipment used by fabs that make chips.

Q. How is EUV light made?

Inside a vacuum chamber, a laser hits 50,000 tin droplets per second, turning them into plasma that gives off EUV light.
