The short answer: semiconductor jobs are "roles that solve process problems"

Jobs in the semiconductor industry are not neatly split into a single line. Even among "process engineers," the person in charge of deposition, the person in charge of etching, and the person in charge of CMP use different equipment and look at different data.

So at first, it is better not to just memorize job titles. What you should look at first are the problems that happen in each process.

Deposition problemsFilm thickness, uniformity, adhesion, step coverage. Process, equipment, and materials roles are involved.
Lithography problemsResolution, misalignment, resist conditions. Lithography, materials, and metrology roles are involved.
Cleaning problemsParticles, metal contamination, chemical residue. Cleaning process, water treatment, and quality roles are involved.
CMP problemsFlatness, scratches, residue, slurry. Process, materials, and equipment roles are involved.
Inspection problemsDefects, line width, film thickness, misalignment. Inspection, metrology, and yield analysis roles are involved.
Mass production problemsKeeping production going at the same quality. Quality assurance, production engineering, and yield improvement roles are involved.

Process engineer: the job of creating process conditions

A process engineer fine-tunes "how to make" semiconductors. For deposition, for example, they think about how to set temperature, pressure, gas flow, and time to get the target film thickness and film quality.

In mass production, it is not enough to make something well just once. The conditions must keep variation small and defects few, even across thousands or tens of thousands of wafers. So process engineers connect experiments, data analysis, equipment knowledge, and materials knowledge.

Naoki

Is a process engineer someone who searches for conditions in a lab?

Professor Owl

That's part of it. But in mass production, what matters is finding "conditions that produce stable results." It's a job of growing the process while watching film thickness variation, defects, yield, and equipment condition.

Equipment engineers and FSEs: the job of keeping equipment running

In a semiconductor fab, when manufacturing equipment stops, it leads to big losses. Equipment engineers and field service engineers (FSEs) support equipment start-up, maintenance, troubleshooting, and improvement.

Applied Materials' description of the FSE role also explains field service as supporting the installation, maintenance, and upgrading of equipment at customer sites. If you work for an equipment maker, you will often handle on-site problems directly, close to the customer's fab.

If you are interested in mechanics, electrical systems, controls, vacuum, fluids, heat, or troubleshooting, it is easy to see how you might fit with equipment-related jobs.

Materials development: supporting invisible performance through materials

Semiconductor manufacturing uses many materials, such as wafers, photoresist, cleaning chemicals, etching gases, CMP slurry, and polishing pads. Materials development is the job of creating the performance and stability of these materials.

In CMP, for example, slurry particles, chemical reactions, and the condition of the polishing pad affect flatness and scratches. In cleaning, chemicals, ultrapure water (UPW), filters, and piping materials affect contamination and yield. If you are interested in chemistry or materials, rereading the process articles from a materials point of view makes it easier to see a way into the work.

Inspection, metrology, and yield: finding invisible defects

With semiconductors, making them is not the end. You need to measure whether what you made is as intended, whether there are defects, and whether line width or film thickness is off.

KLA's careers page introduces work that detects defects too small to see and contributes to manufacturing precision and yield. In advanced semiconductors, measuring technology is just as important as manufacturing technology.

Yield improvement is the job of tracking down the causes of defects by looking at inspection data, process data, and defect maps. For people who enjoy data analysis, statistics, and working things out with the people on the floor, it is a very interesting field.

You can learn more about the scope of jobs that inspect products and handle measurements and quality records in the article on inspection and quality evaluation.

Summary: look at process problems, not job titles

Jobs in the semiconductor industry are hard to understand from job titles alone. But looking at them through the problems that happen in each process makes them clear.

  • Process engineers create process conditions
  • Equipment and FSE roles run equipment, keep it running, and improve it
  • Materials development supports chemicals, resists, slurries, and more
  • Inspection and metrology measure defects and dimensions
  • Yield improvement and quality assurance track down defect causes and make stable production possible
