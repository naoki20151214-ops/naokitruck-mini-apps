What SK hynix presented in the United States

On September 18, 2026 (U.S. local time), SK hynix held its "2026 Global Forum" in Santa Clara, California. The official news article was published on September 21. Because the event date and the article date differ, it is easiest to understand this news as discussions held on September 18 that the company made public on September 21. SK hynix's official announcement

The forum's theme was not limited to current memory products, including HBM. SK hynix explained the memory technologies that will come after HBM, its technology development roadmap for the AI era, and the direction of R&D that links design through manufacturing, packaging, and systems. The CEO laid out a policy of building on the track record in HBM and opening up new possibilities for AI with next-generation memory now in preparation.

However, caution is needed here. This was not an announcement of a specific product name, start date for volume production, performance figures, or customer adoption for next-generation memory. Phrases like "in preparation" and "will strengthen" describe company policy and plans; they do not mean the products are already in volume production and widely used in actual AI servers.

At the venue, leaders in charge of DRAM design, AI interfaces, advanced packaging, and memory systems, along with university researchers, held discussions. In addition, a total of eight small seminars for experts and researchers were held in four areas: Silicon Design, Process & Device, Advanced Package, and AI System. A key point this time is the focus on the whole system, not just the performance of individual parts.

Why does memory matter so much for AI?

When people talk about AI chips, they tend to focus on the computing performance of GPUs and AI accelerators. But even if the compute unit is fast, it cannot deliver its full performance unless the data it needs arrives fast enough. This situation is commonly called the "memory wall." It is the problem where the speed and capacity of reading data from memory cannot keep up with the growth of computing power, so the compute units are left waiting.

HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) is a leading type of memory that addresses this challenge. Multiple DRAM dies, the basic memory chips, are stacked vertically and placed close to the AI accelerator. The structure aims to move large amounts of data at once using a wider connection path than ordinary memory. AI model training and inference frequently read and write weights and intermediate data, so bandwidth, which indicates the amount of data transferred, becomes important. SK hynix's AI memory explainer

On the other hand, HBM is not just about stacking. Stacking more chips makes it harder to remove heat and increases power consumption, signal distortion, connection defects, and inspection difficulty. That is why it is necessary to optimize not only the DRAM cell design but also the interfaces that exchange signals, the manufacturing process, logic circuits, advanced packaging, and the AI system configuration together.

How to read the published figures and next-generation technology

On a separate technology page, SK hynix lists for its HBM4 a 2K I/O, bandwidth of over 2.8TB/s, about 40% better power efficiency using a logic foundry process, and stacks of up to 16-Hi with Advanced MR-MUF. "2K I/O" here means there are about 2,000 connection points for moving data in and out. "16-Hi" refers to a configuration that stacks up to 16 layers of DRAM dies. SK hynix Research's HBM4 description

However, these are not figures newly announced at this Global Forum; they are company-published values that SK hynix shows on a separate page. They are not the same as third-party measurements or effective performance guaranteed in every product and system. Actual AI server performance also depends on the accelerator, cooling, software, packaging, memory capacity, and more.

SK hynix does not describe AI memory only in terms of HBM. It positions AI-DRAM as system memory for servers and computing platforms, and AI-NAND as storage for large amounts of data and retrieval during inference. In other words, it is a "full-stack" approach that combines memory that feeds data quickly near the accelerator, memory that supports the whole server, and storage that holds large amounts of data. This can be read as a sign that competition in AI memory is widening from a bandwidth race over single products to a race to optimize the entire flow of data.

Connection to R&D and production plans in the United States

At this forum, SK hynix described its plan to establish a Global AI R&D Center in the United States, along with the Yongin Cluster in South Korea and the Indiana Fab in the United States, as foundations for its technology and growth. However, the official announcement we can confirm this time does not give the location, size, investment amount, or start of operations for the Global AI R&D Center. A plan to establish a center must be distinguished from a research center that is already running.

As for the Indiana Fab, SK hynix said in a separate announcement on August 28 that total investment will exceed $4 billion, the cleanroom is scheduled to open in October 2028, and volume production of next-generation HBM is planned to start in the second half of 2029. The plan is to send wafers made in South Korea to Indiana for advanced packaging and testing, and R&D cooperation with Purdue University was also noted. This is a concrete future plan, but it is not news that volume production had started as of September 2026. SK hynix's Indiana Fab announcement

Limits of this announcement and points to watch

This Global Forum was less a product launch than a place to share the direction of R&D and the business foundation for AI memory. The fact that SK hynix held discussions across technology fields shows its view that improving HBM performance requires cooperation that extends to packaging and systems, not just the memory itself. On the other hand, the demand outlook for AI memory and claims of technical advantage are SK hynix's own views, not confirmed by independent market forecasts or third-party evaluations.

Going forward, checking the following points will make it easier to judge what the announcement really means:

  • The official product names, specs, and sample shipment timing for next-generation memory
  • Whether figures for HBM4 and others are confirmed in customer systems or third-party evaluations, not just company-published values
  • Whether construction, equipment installation, qualification, and volume production at the Indiana Fab proceed on schedule
  • Whether the location, opening date, and research topics of the Global AI R&D Center become concrete
  • How capacity, power consumption, heat, yield, supply volume, and price are evaluated in practice, not just bandwidth

When reading semiconductor news, it is important to separate "announced plans," "company forecasts," "completed facilities," "products in volume production," and "independent performance checks." This news shows that SK hynix is trying to pursue AI memory not as a single HBM product but as a comprehensive strategy linking design, manufacturing, packaging, systems, talent, and regional sites.
