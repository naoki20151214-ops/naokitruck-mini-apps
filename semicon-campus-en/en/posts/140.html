The short answer: Fujimi is easiest to understand through CMP materials

Fujimi's official product page introduces CMP slurries, abrasives, and other products for semiconductor devices. The product lineup organizes CMP slurries by target film, such as oxide, W, Poly-Si, Cu, and Cu/Ta.

For beginners, it is easy to start by seeing Fujimi as "a company that makes materials for polishing semiconductors." But it is not just about removing material. You need to target the film that should be removed, leave the films that must not be removed, and keep surface defects down.

Why CMP slurry matters

CMP is not a process that flattens the surface with equipment power alone. Stable planarization is possible only when the polishing pad, slurry, chemicals, cleaning, film thickness measurement, and defect inspection all work together.

For example, the properties needed in the materials change between polishing oxide and polishing tungsten or copper. Which film to remove quickly, which film to leave, and how well to prevent scratches and particles: that is where a materials maker's technology comes in.

How is it different from Ebara or SCREEN?

Ebara is a company that is easy to understand in the context of CMP equipment. SCREEN is easy to understand in the context of cleaning equipment. Fujimi is easy to understand as a company that supports the CMP process from the materials side, with CMP slurries, abrasives, and more.

CMP cannot work with equipment alone or with materials alone. It is a process that links equipment, materials, cleaning, metrology, and quality control. That is why looking at companies through CMP makes the jobs in the semiconductor industry much easier to see.
