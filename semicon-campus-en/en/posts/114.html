The short answer: look at semiconductor companies "by process"

The semiconductor industry is not completed by a single company. Companies that design chips, companies that manufacture them, companies that make wafers and chemicals, companies that build manufacturing equipment, companies that inspect and measure, and companies that package chips in the back-end process are all connected.

That is why, in company research, it is important to look not only at "whether it is a famous company" but at "which process's problems the company solves."

Companies behind materialsThey supply wafers, resists, chemicals, slurries, and more.
Companies behind equipmentThey supply lithography, deposition, etching, cleaning, CMP, and inspection equipment.
Companies behind manufacturingAs foundries or IDMs, they make chips for customers.
Companies behind inspectionThey measure defects, film thickness, line width, and electrical characteristics.
Companies behind back-end processesThey support cutting, bonding, packaging, and testing.

How to read companies by process

Wafers and materials: supporting the foundation of semiconductor manufacturing

Wafers are the foundation for making semiconductor chips. Wafer companies like Shin-Etsu Chemical and SUMCO support the quality, crystals, and surface condition of silicon wafers. If wafer quality is poor, it affects yield no matter how hard later processes try.

Lithography: supporting the heart of miniaturization

ASML, Nikon, and Canon are companies closely tied to lithography equipment. Lithography technologies like EUV and DUV, in particular, are essential for forming fine patterns in leading-edge chips. Lithography involves equipment, resists, masks, and metrology, so it is also an important starting point for company research.

Deposition and etching: supporting the core of the front-end process

Tokyo Electron, Applied Materials, and Lam Research are leading equipment companies involved in several processes, including deposition, etching, and cleaning. Deposition creates thin films, and etching removes unwanted parts. This area requires understanding of process conditions, equipment condition, and materials.

Cleaning and ultrapure water: supporting the processes that protect yield

Cleaning is not "just washing with water." It is the process that reduces particles, metal contamination, and chemical residue so a clean wafer can be passed to the next process. SCREEN makes cleaning equipment, and Kurita Water Industries and Organo are companies that help you understand ultrapure water and water treatment.

CMP: a process where equipment, materials, and inspection connect

CMP is the process that flattens the wafer surface. Equipment companies like Ebara and Applied Materials and slurry and materials companies like Fujimi and Entegris are involved. CMP connects equipment, materials, cleaning, inspection, and yield, so it is a process that links easily to understanding job types and job posting keywords.

Inspection, metrology, and testing: companies that measure what is made

In leading-edge semiconductors, measuring technology is as important as making technology. If you understand KLA in terms of process control, inspection, and metrology, and Advantest in terms of semiconductor test equipment, terms in job postings such as "inspection," "metrology," "evaluation," and "yield analysis" become easier to read.

Summary: company names come into focus when placed within processes

If you look only at company names, there are too many semiconductor companies to remember. But looking at them by process makes them easy to organize.

  • Cleaning and CMP support yield and flatness
  • Inspection, metrology, and testing measure what is made and protect quality
  • Back-end processes and assembly turn chips into usable products
