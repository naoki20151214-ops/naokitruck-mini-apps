What happened? 2026 funding reaches $650 million in total

Ayar Labs announced on September 10, 2026 that it had raised an additional $150 million. The company develops a technology called "CPO," which uses light for data communication between AI chips. It plans to use the new money for product development and validation, expanding its manufacturing capabilities, and its design center in Bengaluru, India, among other things. This news can be read as funding and organizational groundwork for bringing optical links into mass production. Company funding announcement

According to the company, its total primary capital raised in 2026, including this new round, is $650 million. This is a 2026 figure, not the total raised since the company was founded. Also, September 10 is the date the funding was announced, not the date the money was paid in. The strategic investment by Wiwynn mentioned in the announcement is described as a separate deal made earlier that year, so we cannot conclude that Wiwynn provided the full $150 million. Why does it matter? In AI, moving data between chips is also a challenge

To make multiple AI chips work together, each chip must not only compute but also exchange data with other chips. Think of several workers sharing a job: besides how fast each person works, you also need a way to pass documents between them. Ayar Labs aims to handle this data handoff with light and expand how AI systems can be connected. The company's optical interconnect products for AI

It is important to separate computing from communication. In the company's technical explanation, electrical data from the chip is converted to light and sent, then converted back to electricity on the receiving side. So it is not accurate to say that AI computation itself is being replaced by light. The setup combines chips that do the computing with optical links that carry the data. A simple guide to CPO and silicon photonics

CPO stands for Co-Packaged Optics. It is the idea of placing optical connection functions near the computing chip and building them into the semiconductor package. A package is the structure that holds a chip and connects it to the outside and to other chips. Ayar Labs' products combine the TeraPHY optical engine, which sends and receives light, with the SuperNova light source, which supplies light from outside. The key point is that the role of providing light is separated from the role of sending and receiving data with light. TeraPHY and SuperNova product lineup

Silicon photonics is a technology that builds light-handling functions onto silicon chips. The company describes a setup that uses the CMOS manufacturing technology used in semiconductor production. However, not everything inside the package becomes optical. Inside, there is an electrical interface for chip-to-chip connection called UCIe, and beyond that the signal is converted to light and sent outside the package. It is easier to understand if you picture the path as "electrical up close, optical between packages." Explanation of silicon photonics and electrical connections

The company explains that placing the light source outside is a design choice that considers thermal management, reliability, and serviceability. Its product materials also show detachable optical fiber connections. In putting optical communication into practical use, the design must cover not only sending data but also dealing with heat and making the connection points easy to handle. However, these are design advantages described by the company. They do not mean the same effect has been proven at every customer site. Background: from communication demos to manufacturing and testing

A technical article the company published on August 28, 2025 helps explain this funding. It said the latest TeraPHY at the time had completed EVT and was moving into the final stage of DVT. EVT is engineering validation testing, which checks basic functions and operation. DVT is design validation testing, which checks whether the design meets its required conditions. You can think of it as a step that, after confirming that a prototype works, checks whether the design holds up under expected conditions. Validation report from August 28, 2025

The same article also covers wafer testing and handling temperature changes. A wafer is a thin, disk-shaped material on which many chips are made at once, and testing at that stage is needed to sort the manufactured chips. Besides being able to communicate with light, the challenges for mass production include testing manufactured chips, building them into packages, and making the connections work. Note that this report shows the state as of 2025. It is not a basis for concluding that DVT is still incomplete in 2026. Technical explanation of manufacturing and validation steps

Read funding and mass production separately

What this announcement confirms is the additional funding and a plan to strengthen development, validation, and manufacturing capabilities. Securing money alone does not mean mass production or large-volume shipments to customers have been achieved. The materials do not show a mass-production start date, production capacity, shipment volume, or adoption and qualification status by customer. For product performance advantages and power savings, too, the company's claims need to be separated from independent test results. Use of funds, The company's product description

The Bengaluru design center appears both in the funding announcement and on the official careers page. However, being listed is not evidence that hiring is complete or that the site is fully operational. The exact opening date and headcount cannot be confirmed, so for now it is reasonable to treat this as information about building up the design team.
