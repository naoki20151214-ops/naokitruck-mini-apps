On October 12, 2026, NVIDIA and Cisco announced "Ultra-Dense Small Form Factor Pluggable (USFP)," a new form factor for pluggable optical modules for AI factories. It is a joint proposal at the planning and design stage, aimed at making connections for AI servers and network switches denser. It is not an announcement that volume-production products are on sale or have already shipped in large numbers to customers. Cisco's announcement NVIDIA's announcement

What was announced?

USFP is a new module shape that supports electrical signals at 400Gbps per lane. The initial plan fits 16 full-duplex lanes into a housing about the size of OSFP and expects it to be used in both switches and SuperNICs. A lane is an independent signal path that carries data. Think of it as a road: the idea is not only to add more lanes but also to raise the speed of each lane. Cisco Newsroom

The capacity numbers need careful reading. Multiplying 16 lanes by 400Gbps gives 6.4Tbps in one direction. Simply adding full-duplex transmit and receive together gives 12.8Tbps. Meanwhile, Cisco's explanation puts the initial 16 lanes at 6.4Tbps and also shows a path to expand to 12.8Tbps by moving to 32 lanes in the future. So we cannot conclude that 12.8Tbps is "the effective speed of the first product." The meaning changes depending on how directions are counted, whether it is the initial or future configuration, and whether protocol overhead is included. Cisco blog NVIDIA Newsroom

Technical terms for beginners

Pluggable optical modules

A pluggable optical module is a replaceable part that plugs into a switch or network card. It converts electrical signals inside the equipment into optical signals and sends them out over optical fiber. One advantage is that a module can be swapped out easily when it fails or when performance is upgraded. USFP is a concept that aims to raise the electrical lane speed and the bandwidth density on the front of the equipment while keeping this ease of maintenance and replacement. Cisco blog

PAM4 and equalization

PAM4 is a modulation method that represents 2 bits of information with each signal change. It suits high-speed communication, but because the signal levels are packed closely together, noise and waveform distortion need attention. Cisco's existing 400G QSFP112 uses four lanes of 100Gbps PAM4 and is designed for operation based on IEEE 802.3 and MSAs. Cisco 400G QSFP data sheet

Equalization is processing that corrects a signal weakened while passing through boards and connectors, so the receiver can read it more easily. The higher the lane speed, the tougher transmission loss, reflections, correction-circuit power, and manufacturing variation become. OIF technical materials also treat power, density, reach, cost, modulation, equalization, and interoperability as challenges for 400G-per-lane-class links. OIF technical material

Goals for the connector and power

USFP plans to use "two-stage insertion," which connects the high-speed signal contacts after the module is plugged in, and a two-dimensional array of contacts. The two companies explain that the goals are to reduce contact wear, improve signal quality, and make insertion force more predictable. At 400Gbps per lane, the connector's shape and contact condition also affect signal quality, so the form factor itself becomes an important technical element. Cisco Newsroom

The two companies also describe a design that can handle up to 100W of power without a separate dedicated built-in cooling circuit inside the module. However, this is an announced design spec. It does not guarantee that actual volume-production units will perform the same under any temperature, or that total system power will go down. Results will vary with the optical method, ambient temperature, switch chassis, and rack cooling capacity. NVIDIA Newsroom

Why it matters in AI factories

In AI factories, huge amounts of data move between accelerators such as GPUs, network switches, and storage. Even if you add more computing equipment, accelerators spend more time waiting if the network cannot carry all the data. So what matters is not just total bandwidth but also how many connections fit on the limited front panel of a switch, whether power and cooling can be kept down, and whether parts can be replaced when they fail.

If USFP reaches practical use, more connection bandwidth may fit in the same equipment area. If the pluggable approach is kept, optical modules can be maintained without replacing the whole piece of equipment. However, effects such as "fewer network tiers" or "AI factory performance will surely improve" depend on the actual topology, fiber distances, switch port configuration, and cooling equipment. They are not effects confirmed by this announcement alone. Cisco Newsroom

Standardization and volume production are still ahead

TE Connectivity and Amphenol are said to be working with them on USFP cages, connectors, and module development. NVIDIA and Cisco expect more companies to join and plan to create an open industry specification through a community-led multi-source project. If several suppliers take part, it may widen options for purchasing and maintenance, but it has not yet become a finished industry standard. NVIDIA Newsroom

As of October 13, 2026, we could not confirm an official USFP specification, a start date for volume production, product model numbers, prices, the actual optical method, reach, volume adoption by customers, interoperability testing, or independent performance verification. The figures of 12.8Tbps, 4x bandwidth density, about 1.3x power efficiency, and up to 100W are all design values or company comparisons and plans based on the announcements. They should not be treated as confirmed performance measured by a third party in volume production. Cisco Newsroom NVIDIA Newsroom

What to check next

Going forward, we need to check the release of the official specification, interoperability tests with other companies' switches and NICs, measured values from early samples, thermal design, yield, prices, and actual customer adoption. In particular, it matters whether the 400Gbps-per-lane target can achieve both stable operation and replaceability, and which lane configuration and direction definition the 12.8Tbps figure is based on. This news is best seen not as the launch of a finished product, but as the start of joint design and ecosystem building for next-generation AI network connections.
