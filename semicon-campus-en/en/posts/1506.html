On September 29, 2026, TOPPAN, Broadcom, and other stakeholders held an opening ceremony at ADVANCED SUBSTRATE TECHNOLOGIES (AST) in Singapore. The next day, September 30, TOPPAN announced that it had opened an FC-BGA substrate production plant at the facility. The plant is described as TOPPAN's first FC-BGA production site outside Japan, and as Singapore's first facility to manufacture high-performance FC-BGA substrates.TOPPAN announcement, Broadcom announcement

What happened

AST plans to focus mainly on large, high-layer-count FC-BGA substrates used in generative AI, machine learning, AI compute, large networking equipment, and similar applications. According to TOPPAN, the facility's total floor area is about 95,000 square meters, of which about 70,000 square meters will operate in Phase 1. The number of employees was 315 as of the end of September 2026.TOPPAN facility overview

However, "the plant has opened" is not the same as "volume shipments of AI substrates have started and supply is stable." TOPPAN lists the start of operation as December 2026, and Broadcom describes a goal of starting high-value manufacturing in the second half of 2026. These are plans indicating future start dates; as of October 2, 2026, they do not show production volumes or shipment results.Broadcom's explanation

What is an FC-BGA substrate?

FC-BGA stands for "Flip Chip-Ball Grid Array." It refers to a high-density substrate that houses a semiconductor chip in a package and connects it electrically to the board of an electronic device. It is used not only in small devices like smartphones but also in chips that process large amounts of data at high speed, such as AI processors, network ASICs, server CPUs, and GPUs.TOPPAN FC-BGA technical explanation

"Flip chip" is a mounting method in which the chip's connection side faces down and is connected to the substrate. Compared with methods that route wires out from the edges of the chip, this makes it easier to place more connection points over short distances. In a ball grid array, where connection points are arranged in a grid, the solder balls lined up on the back of the substrate ultimately become the terminals that connect to the system board.

In manufacturing, holes are first drilled in the core material, copper plating is applied, and circuit patterns are formed. Next, build-up wiring, which stacks insulating layers and wiring layers, is repeated. Finally, solder bumps for mounting the chip are formed, followed by singulation, visual inspection, and electrical testing. Build-up is the process of stacking thin insulating layers and copper wiring to create complex wiring in a limited area.TOPPAN manufacturing process

Why it matters in the AI era

As chips for generative AI and data centers grow more powerful, they need to exchange huge amounts of data at high speed between the chip and surrounding components. So package substrates are also required to support many connection points, fine wiring, and larger sizes. The "high-layer-count substrates" drawing attention for AI are a technology direction that stacks many wiring layers to make it easier to handle complex signals and power within the substrate.

However, the published materials alone do not reveal the specific layer count, dimensions, electrical characteristics, yield, or per-product output of the substrates AST will make. The description "large, high-layer-count" shows applications and development direction; it does not prove adoption in a specific AI chip or how many times better they perform than earlier products.

What it means for the supply chain

FC-BGA substrates are not chips themselves, but they are a key material that connects chips to electronic systems. Even if a chip performs well, if the matching package substrate cannot be sourced reliably, the overall production of servers and networking equipment may be constrained.

TOPPAN says it is setting up the Singapore site as a two-site system with its Niigata plant in Japan, aiming to strengthen its global production network while spreading out natural disaster and geopolitical risks. Broadcom also describes AST as a joint venture between TOPPAN Holdings and Broadcom and says it will help address supply constraints on substrates for AI and networking and strengthen supply chain resilience. These are the companies' explanations; they do not mean that actual supply stabilization has already been proven.2024 announcement at the start of construction, Broadcom's explanation

The Singapore government explains the significance of adding FC-BGA manufacturing and R&D to the country's base of chip design, wafer fabrication, semiconductor equipment, advanced packaging, research institutions, and talent. AST also plans R&D, training and internships with educational institutions, and partnerships with local suppliers. These are plans showing the facility's role and expectations, not figures for local procurement, research results, or final employment numbers.Singapore government ceremony remarks

Cautions when looking at the numbers

TOPPAN has set a goal of raising its FC-BGA production capacity to at least 2.5 times the fiscal 2022 level by fiscal 2027. However, this is a company target, not an announcement that 2.5 times the capacity has already been achieved. Also, opening a facility, trial operation, starting high-value manufacturing, and volume shipments to customers each mean different things. When reading the news, you need to check which stage is being referred to.TOPPAN production capacity target

Broadcom's materials include the company's view that the plant will help address supply constraints and contribute to its next-generation product roadmap. On the other hand, ownership ratios, contract terms, supply volumes by customer, and actual yield and shipment results cannot be confirmed from the published materials. Broadcom's statement that it supported the facility's development as the largest customer also does not show which products will use it or in what quantities.Broadcom official announcement

What to watch from here

The first focus is whether AST starts production as planned during 2026, or in December 2026. Second, it will matter whether it can move from prototypes and initial production to stable volume shipments for customers. Third, we want to see whether actual production capacity, yield, product specs, and shipment volumes to customers are disclosed.

In addition, how far progress has been made toward the goal of at least 2.5 times capacity by fiscal 2027, how roles are divided with the Niigata plant, and whether backup supply works when problems occur will all help judge how effective the supply chain strengthening really is. This news is an important step: one more production site for materials that support AI chips. However, to judge that supply problems for AI chips have been solved, we need to wait for results after operations begin.
