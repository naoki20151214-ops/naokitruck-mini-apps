On October 5, 2026, Navitas Semiconductor and Microchip Technology jointly announced a reference design that converts 800V direct current (DC) directly to 6V DC for rack power in AI data centers. This is not the start of shipments of a mass-produced product. It is a design example that customers and designers can use as a starting point for evaluation and development. As the power used by AI GPUs and accelerators grows, the questions of what voltage to distribute power at and where to convert it are becoming important. Joint announcement by the two companies

What was announced

The core of the announcement is a 20kW-class reference design that converts 800V DC to 6V DC in a single stage. According to Microchip's public page, it uses a 128:1 full-bridge LLC topology with two 10kW conversion modules, connected in series on the input side and in parallel on the output side. It is a power circuit that converts the high voltage on the 800V side to the low voltage used by servers and accelerators. Microchip reference design page

The announcement shows a configuration that uses sixteen Navitas 650V, 17mΩ GaN FETs switching at 1MHz. It also cites a peak efficiency of up to 96% and a power density of 2,100W/in³, but these are design targets and performance claims from the companies. They do not represent independent third-party measurements, guaranteed values under mass-production conditions, or the efficiency of a whole data center. Specifications in the announcement

Why is 800V DC getting attention?

Power can roughly be expressed as "voltage × current." To carry the same amount of power, raising the voltage lets you lower the current needed. Resistive losses in wires depend on the square of the current, so lowering the current makes it easier to reduce wiring losses and may allow smaller busbars and cables.

In AI data centers, the power density of racks packed with many GPUs is rising. If power is increased while keeping the traditional 54V rack setup, the copper wiring, connectors, cooling, and protection equipment needed to handle large currents become a challenge. NVIDIA describes 800V DC as an architecture that may reduce current, copper use, cabling, and the number of conversion stages compared with setups such as 54V. NVIDIA's technical description of 800V DC

This design aims to combine the traditional multi-stage conversion of "800V to 50V, then 50V to 6V" into a direct 800V-to-6V conversion. Fewer conversion stages may reduce the number of parts and conversion losses. However, the benefit depends on system conditions, including input voltage, load factor, cooling method, wiring, and facility-side conversion equipment.

Technical terms for beginners

GaN power semiconductors

GaN is a type of wide-bandgap semiconductor made from gallium nitride. In power supplies, it works as a switch that turns electricity on and off at high speed. It is easier to run at higher frequencies than silicon switches, which may reduce switching losses and allow smaller magnetic components such as transformers.

However, using GaN does not automatically mean high efficiency. Performance depends on the whole design, including magnetic components, parasitic inductance in the wiring, control algorithms, load, input voltage, and cooling conditions.

LLC resonant converters

An LLC resonant converter changes voltage by combining a switching circuit with the resonance of coils and capacitors. By switching in step with the resonance, it can reduce losses and noise during switching and aim for high-frequency operation. On the other hand, control that follows changes in input voltage and load, insulation, heat, and magnetic component design are not easy.

Digital control and hardware security

In Microchip's configuration, a dsPIC33AK digital signal controller handles resonant control, telemetry, thermal protection, PMBus, SPDM communication, and live firmware updates. A TA100 security device, secure boot, authenticated updates, and a root of trust are also built in. A root of trust is the trusted reference point a device uses to verify genuine parts and software. Control and security configuration

In environments where power equipment is monitored and updated over a network, mechanisms that reject unauthorized firmware or control nodes are important. However, security features in components alone do not make the whole system secure. Key management, network isolation, update procedures, and operational monitoring are also needed.

Technology and standardization background

800V DC distribution is not a brand-new idea. Setups are being studied in which the facility converts AC to DC, distributes 800V DC inside the data center, and steps it down to low voltage near the racks and servers. However, higher voltages require insulation, arcing, protection coordination, connectors, safety during maintenance, and cooling to be designed together.

At the IEEE, the P3710.1 standardization project, covering DC distribution from 300V to 1500V, is under way. Its page shows it was approved as a PAR on March 26, 2026, and it is still listed as an Active PAR. This shows that high-voltage DC distribution is a topic of discussion for design guidelines, but it does not mean 800V DC is already widely established as an industry standard, or that this design has received IEEE certification. What is still unknown, and what to watch next

What we can confirm from public materials is that the two companies announced a reference design and presented a 20kW-class conversion method, GaN devices, digital control, security features, and efficiency and power density targets. We cannot confirm when joint development began, when prototyping was completed, or whether it is ready to ship as a mass-produced product.

The 96% figure is also peak efficiency. In real data centers, load factors vary, and cooling, wiring, standby power, and protection circuits are added. Going forward, we need to check the efficiency curves of real hardware, thermal design, insulation and safety measures, long-term reliability, redundancy during failures, and total system power consumption.

The companies said they plan to show this design at Microchip's OCP event held October 12–15, 2026. This is a future company plan, and as of October 6, 2026, it does not indicate post-event results, customer evaluations, or mass-production adoption. Joint announcement including the exhibition plan

  • Whether measurement conditions and efficiency curves for real hardware are released at the event
  • How safety standards, maintenance procedures, and connectors for 800V DC are sorted out in practice
  • Whether this design is adopted in customer trial deployments or mass-produced products
  • Whether standardization at the IEEE and elsewhere leads to common specifications and safety guidelines
  • Whether overall efficiency and cost from AC input to the GPU improve, not just for the power supply alone

This announcement makes concrete one direction for handling the rising power needs of AI data centers: raising the distribution voltage and combining GaN with digital control. It is too early to say that 800V DC will soon become the industry standard, but this reference design is worth watching when thinking about where to convert power and how to manage it safely.
